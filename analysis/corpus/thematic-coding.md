# Inductive Thematic Coding — Corpus A01–A16

**Stage 2, Step 2.** Analytical dimensions derived inductively from the whole-corpus
evidence matrix (`analysis/corpus/evidence-matrix.md`).

## What this document is and is not

This is a **derived coding framework**, not a summary. The dimensions below were built by
comparing matrix entries against each other, splitting concepts that shared a word but not
a phenomenon, merging concepts that shared a phenomenon but not a word, and testing each
candidate against all sixteen studies individually. Dimensions that required forcing
studies to fit were rejected (see *Rejected candidate dimensions*).

The provisional per-study concept lists (field 20 of the matrix) were treated as **raw
material to be interrogated**, not as a framework to be concatenated. Several dimensions
below (D3, D4, D8, D9) do not correspond to any field-20 entry in any study; they became
visible only through comparison.

**Provenance note.** All content derives from `analysis/corpus/evidence-matrix.md`. One
targeted verification was performed against `analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`, to
confirm that A03 reports no institutional adoption, governance approval, or
implementation stage (confirmed: its validation is internal/model-consistency-based, and
post-implementation outcome data are recorded as UNSUPPORTED in that analysis). No
dissertation material, no `literature/` notes, and no other analysis files were consulted.

## Standing limitations carried from Step 1

- **NOT APPLICABLE** marks a field that is meaningless for a study type. It is never
  evidence that something is absent from the world. A03, A07, A09, A15 and A16 accumulate
  such markers because of what they are, not because of what they lack.
- **NOT REPORTED** marks silence in the source. It is never converted below into a
  substantive finding about a study. Where a corpus-level *pattern of silence* is claimed
  (D8, and the Gaps section), it is labelled as such and its inferential basis is stated.
- Group 1 / Group 2 extraction differed in verbosity and DERIVED-labelling density. **No
  dimension, contrast, or cluster below is built on that artifact.** Where a study appears
  thin on a dimension, the reason given is always substantive and traceable to a specific
  matrix field.
- Field 22 (note-layer caveats) was checked before every strong per-study claim.
- Several notes carry translated/paraphrased quotations. **No dimension below rests on
  fine-grained terminological argument about author wording.**

## Frequency is not strength

Two facts govern the confidence ratings below.

First, **ten of sixteen studies are single-case, non-comparative designs** (A01, A02, A03,
A04, A05, A07, A08, A10, A13, A14). Every corpus claim about *variation between units
facing the same reform* therefore rests on exactly three studies (A06, A11, A15). A theme
recurring in twelve studies may be weaker than a finding in one, if the twelve are
structurally incapable of testing it.

Second, several apparent convergences rest on studies sharing the same methodological
vulnerability (insider self-evaluation). Convergence among studies with a **common
vulnerability does not compound into strength**; it compounds the vulnerability. This is
flagged explicitly at D8 and in the Cross-study relationships section.

# Part I — Analytical dimensions

## D1. Conception of curriculum and corresponding object of inquiry

### Definition

What each study takes a curriculum fundamentally *to be*, and — relatedly but not
identically — what it therefore selects as the thing to observe. The dimension pairs an
ontological commitment (curriculum as object / text / process / system) with an
observational choice (what data the study collects).

### Analytical question

When two studies use the word "curriculum," are they investigating the same phenomenon?
And where the ontology and the observational choice come apart, what does that reveal?

### Possible values / manifestations

**Qualitatively different forms, not a continuum.** At least six distinct conceptions are
documented, and they are not points on one scale:

1. **Combinatorial / structural object** — a set of courses, credits and prerequisite
   relations that can be selected, sequenced and balanced. A03, A11, A05 (72 credits, 11
   new courses), A16 (steps and content blocks).
2. **Relational information structure** — a network whose meaning lies in links between
   nodes, representable and searchable. A07 (explicitly: "the essence of a curriculum map
   is not the nodes but the links"), A02 (objectives mapped upward to competencies), A10
   (alignment map).
3. **Text / discourse / contractual artifact** — A06 alone: strategy documents, redesign
   forms, and programme specifications described as a "negotiated pact."
4. **Contextualized social process / ongoing negotiation** — A14 (Cornbleth 1988,
   explicitly), A04 (Doll/Slattery, "curriculum-in-action"), A12 (process model as one pole
   of its central contrast), A01 (iterative, non-linear), A13 ("complex, dynamic,
   overlapping, non-linear"), A15 (change processes as the object).
5. **Living system with health states** — A09 alone: viability, deterioration,
   Abrahamson's "curriculum diseases."
6. **Domains of personal formation** — A08 (knowing / acting / becoming-being), where the
   third domain is argued to be *constitutively unrepresentable* in institutional systems.

### Supporting studies

- **A03** shows the combinatorial conception in its purest form: 53 candidate courses, a
  mapping index, and a goal-programming schedule. Nothing in the account requires the
  curriculum to be enacted by anyone.
- **A07** shows that a relational conception generates a *communication* problem rather
  than a *design* problem: its motivating question is that teachers and students do not
  know what is taught where.
- **A06** shows that a discursive conception makes uptake measurable as textual gradation
  (Absent → Vague → Implicit → Present → Explicit) across Language, Intent, Application.
- **A14** and **A04** show that a process conception makes the *decision-making group*,
  not the curriculum, the unit of analysis (A04's unit is a ten-person team; A14's is a
  redesign team plus support team).
- **A09** shows that an organismic conception generates a diagnostic rather than a
  compliance question: why standards are not met, not whether they are met.
- **A08** supplies the corpus's sharpest ontological challenge: the "becoming/being"
  domain could not be entered into TEQSA-derived documentation because it could not be
  reduced to observable behaviours — i.e. some curricular content is claimed to exist that
  conceptions 1–3 cannot register at all.

### Variations

Conception 1 divides on purpose. **A03 formalizes in order to optimize**; **A11
formalizes in order to measure and then explicitly argues against optimizing** ("blindly
reducing complexity should not be established as a final goal"). Same ontology, opposite
normative conclusion. This is the single most informative variation in the dimension,
because it shows conception does not determine stance.

Conception 4 divides on whether the process is *facilitated* (A14, A10, A12) or
*self-organized* (A04, A01) or *led* (A13).

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A11** holds conceptions 1 and 4 simultaneously and treats the tension between them as
  its finding. It cannot be assigned a single value.
- **A12** does not hold a conception of curriculum at all; it thematizes the
  product-vs-process contrast as its *object of study* and reports that practitioners
  hybridize both. It sits above the dimension rather than within it.
- **A05** holds conception 1 (credits, courses) while arguing normatively for a
  reorientation of every course around an integrating lens — a content-structural
  conception with a pedagogical-philosophical overlay.
- **A16** operates at a meta-level: it classifies models by whether they are
  competency-centred or objective-centred, and the matrix records (field 21) that whether
  its own four-step framework resolves or merely sets aside that distinction is NOT
  REPORTED. Its conception is therefore genuinely indeterminate, not merely unstated.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Correlates with **D7 (prescription)** — formal conceptions tend to yield procedural
outputs — but **A11 breaks the correlation decisively** (formal conception,
explicitly non-prescriptive output), so the two must not be collapsed.

Risk of collapse with **D3 (representation gap)**: D1 asks what curriculum *is*; D3 asks
what a study *measures* relative to enactment. A03 has a fully formal conception and
simply does not engage the representation gap at all — demonstrating the two are separable.

### Analytical role

Conceptual · problem framing · explanatory.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for the existence of qualitatively distinct conceptions: each is anchored to a
named theoretical source recorded in matrix field 6, not inferred.
**MODERATE** for the claim that conception shapes findings: A11 and A12 show hybridization
and counter-directional reasoning within a single conception.

## D2. Locus of difficulty in the change trajectory

### Definition

The point in a curricular change trajectory at which a study locates the thing that goes
wrong, is under-theorized, or is hard — and, consequently, where it directs its remedy.
This is *not* a description of which phase a study covers; it is a claim each study makes
about where the problem actually lives.

### Analytical question

Are the corpus's recommendations competing answers to one question, or answers to
different questions?

### Possible values / manifestations

A trajectory with identifiable positions, each explicitly claimed by at least one study:

| Located difficulty | Studies claiming it | Remedy proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation / how to begin | A12 (explicitly: revision is "more intimidating than designing from scratch"; scarce practice literature) | Long preliminary dialogue; four entry pathways |
| Diagnosis is shallow | A09 (audit ≠ diagnosis), A07 (nobody knows what is taught where) | Viability framework; mapping |
| Diagnosis → action transition | A02 (prior literature "stops at diagnosis") | Theory of Change |
| Design / model selection | A16 (educators confused among models), A03 (planning done "manually and intuitively"), A05 (perception that building from scratch is too hard) | Unified framework; MCDM model; 10-step roadmap |
| Deliberation and decision-making | A04 (shared governance under-studied), A14 (process is the literature's "blind spot"), A01 (linear models misrepresent messy practice) | Vision-first; support team; non-linear participative model |
| Implementation / enactment | A08 (design is easy, implementation is hard), A06 (policy enactment ≠ policy) | Fix workload/documentation; pedagogical translators |
| Depth of uptake | A11 (only low-hanging fruit is taken), A06 (language ≫ application) | Tiered incentives; multi-stage intervention |
| Sustaining / institutionalization | A15 (the only study whose central construct this is), A10 (collapse expected post-facilitation), A08 (funding withdrawal ends the practice) | Ownership, coordination, continuous facilitation |

### Supporting studies

The dimension's payoff is the **tight coupling of located difficulty to proposed remedy**,
visible study by study in the table above. Because the corpus distributes its located
difficulties across the whole trajectory, its recommendations are largely
**complementary rather than competing**. A12's "listen longer" and A08's "revise workload
models" are not rival answers; they address different failure points.

### Variations

Two studies locate difficulty at more than one point without ranking them: **A13**
(review, redesign *and* approval each described as burdensome) and **A06** (both uptake
depth and enactment).

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A13** and **A01** actively deny the trajectory's segmentability: A13 argues real
  processes are "complex, dynamic, overlapping, and non-linear, in contrast to the
  simplified cyclical schemas formally presented in the literature"; A01's core argument
  is against linear models. For these two, the dimension applies as a *contested*
  framework rather than a descriptive one.
- **A15** empirically supports non-linearity at the pattern level: HEIs move between
  patterns, and patterns are "not linear, obligatory life-cycle stages."
- **A03** locates no difficulty in a change trajectory at all — its difficulty is a
  computational one (how to choose and schedule courses). It sits outside the dimension.
  This is a boundary-defining fact about the corpus, not a misfit (see D-notes and
  `clusters.md`).

### Relationships with other dimensions

Strongly predicts **D8 (criterion of success)**: a study that locates difficulty in
documentation will accept documentary success (A02, A13); one locating it in relationships
will accept relational success (A04, A10).

Distinct from **D3**: a study can locate difficulty in implementation (D2) while measuring
only documents (D3) — A06 does exactly this.

### Analytical role

Problem framing · processual · explanatory.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG.** Each placement is drawn from an explicit problem-framing statement in matrix
field 1 or an explicit argument in field 17, not inferred from topic.

## D3. The representation gap, and each study's own position within it

### Definition

The distance between curriculum as *represented* (documents, learning objectives, maps,
specifications) and curriculum as *enacted or experienced* — combined with the second-order
question of **which side of that gap a study's own evidence actually sits on**.

### Analytical question

The corpus almost unanimously endorses this distinction. How much of the corpus actually
measures across it?

### Possible values / manifestations

Three positions, plus non-engagement:

1. **Theorizes the gap; measures only the represented side.** A02 (counts written
   objectives: 7 of 781 in Phase 2, 1 of 642 in Phase 3; its own first declared limitation
   is that actual assessments were never audited, and the rebuttal is a *policy inference*,
   not an audit). A06 (documents only, by design; and its own field 21 records the
   sharpest version of the problem — its "Application" score is itself a measure of
   *declared* application, subject to the same mimicry risk it identifies for "Language").
2. **Gathers some enactment-side evidence.** A08 (student work samples, retention,
   observed workload reality), A14 (non-participant observation in the formative pilot),
   A11 (incidental but important: catalog requirements found to be "routinely waived by
   advisors and faculty in actual practice").
3. **Invokes the distinction conceptually without measuring either side.** A07
   (declared / taught / tested, after English 1978), A09 (planned / delivered / learned,
   in connection with mapping).
4. **Does not engage the gap.** A03, A04, A05, A10, A12, A13, A15, A16, A01.

### Supporting studies

- **A02** is the corpus's clearest instance of representational evidence carrying an
  enactment-level conclusion: objective-count scarcity is treated as confirming "a real
  gap," while the study simultaneously interprets student and faculty satisfaction data as
  showing the *experienced* curriculum was adequate. The matrix (field 21) records an
  unaddressed alternative reading — that the evaluation instruments were simply not
  designed to probe that competency.
- **A06** provides the corpus's only quantified gradient across the gap's *representational
  layers*: a full categorical drop (e.g. "Present" → "Implicit") moving from Language to
  Application, across four pillars.
- **A11** provides the corpus's only incidental empirical demonstration that formal
  documentation and actual practice diverge — a by-product of an analytics exercise, not a
  designed test.
- **A08** provides the strongest claim that the gap is *structurally produced*: the
  "becoming/being" domain could not enter documentation at all.

### Variations

Studies differ on whether the gap is a *measurement problem* (A02, A07: better mapping
closes it), a *cultural translation problem* (A06: only disciplinary fusion closes it), or
a *constitutive institutional problem* (A08: institutional epistemology guarantees it).
These are three different theories of the same gap.

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A15's** depth scale (denial → bolt-on → build-in → redesign) is *frequently mistakable*
  for this dimension and is not it. Depth concerns how far sustainability is embedded
  institutionally, not whether documents match practice. Keeping them separate matters:
  a "redesign"-level curriculum could still exhibit a representation gap.
- **A06's** Active/Engaged/Passive typology likewise measures uptake *within documents*,
  which the study itself states.
- **A03** is a pure boundary case: it produces a representation with no enactment side to
  compare against, since adoption is NOT REPORTED (verified against
  `analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`).

### Relationships with other dimensions

Depends on **D9 (comparative reach)**: crossing the gap requires either observation or a
second data stream, which most single-case document-based designs do not have.
Feeds **D8**: studies stuck on the represented side can only accept documentary success
criteria.

### Analytical role

Conceptual · methodological · evaluative.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** that the distinction is near-universally endorsed (six studies name a version of
it explicitly: A02, A06, A07, A08, A09, A11).
**STRONG** that it is rarely measured across — this is directly countable, not inferred.
**TENTATIVE** for any claim about the *size* of the gap: only A06 quantifies anything, and
only within documentary layers.

## D4. Basis of curricular justification

### Definition

The source of authority a study accepts as making a curricular content or structure
decision defensible. Not "who decides" (D5) but "what settles the argument."

### Analytical question

When a study says a curriculum should contain X, what makes that claim legitimate — and is
that source itself ever examined?

### Possible values / manifestations

Eight recurrent warrants, usually combined rather than exclusive:

| Warrant | Studies |
|---|---|
| Labour-market / employer demand | A01, A05, A13, A11 (broad competencies), A03 (market demand for sustainability competencies) |
| Peer / competitor benchmarking | A01 (peer, competitive, aspirant institutions), A05 (regional mapping), A14 (8 partner programs) |
| Externally authored competency or standards frameworks | A03 (UNESCO eight competencies), A02 (UVA's twelve), A01 (ITAA), A09 (WFME/LCME/GMC), A13 (accreditation guidelines) |
| Externally imposed regulatory format | A08 (TEQSA-derived templates — imposed, not chosen), A06 (PSRBs) |
| Disciplinary epistemology | A06 (learning outcomes formed "largely around disciplines' own content"), A08 (threshold concepts), A14 (instructors' prior local practice) |
| Internally negotiated philosophical vision | A04 (shared vision explicitly precedes syllabus work), A12 (educational philosophy as an entry pathway), A14 |
| Formal alignment logic | A02 (Bloom), A08 (SOLO), A12/A13 (constructive alignment), A16 (model steps) |
| Quantitative structural property | A11 (complexity score), A03 (credit/difficulty balance) |
| Student / alumni voice | A01, A05, A10, A13, A14 |

### Supporting studies

- **A01** and **A05** show benchmarking used as an *external legitimation device* that
  settles internal argument: A01's logistics course was added as "a direct result of
  benchmarking."
- **A08** supplies the corpus's most radical claim about warrant: the institutional warrant
  (behavioural learning outcomes) does not merely compete with the disciplinary warrant, it
  *excludes* a curricular domain from existing officially.
- **A06** supplies the corpus's only comparative claim between warrants — that PSRBs exert
  substantially more power over local curriculum than central institutional policy.
- **A04** is the clearest counter-pole: warrant is constructed internally and
  collectively, and the study argues this construction must *precede* technical work.

### Variations

**A05 is internally divided**: it uses demand data (99% survey support, vocational demand)
while explicitly attacking "cosmetic" market-driven relabeling. **A16 is internally
divided**: it argues for standardization and for model-context fit in the same breath.

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A07** does not supply a warrant for content at all; it supplies a warrant for
  *visibility* of content. Boundary case.
- **A09** inverts the dimension: its object is why warranted standards fail to be met,
  not which warrant is right.
- **A12** treats warrant selection as the practitioner's *choice variable* (outcomes-first
  vs philosophy-first vs graduate-profile-first) rather than as something to be settled.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Interacts with **D5**: externally warranted reforms need less internal deliberative
machinery — except that **A01 breaks this** (strong external warrant *and* heavy conflict
management), so the relationship is a tendency, not a dependency.

### Analytical role

Normative · conceptual · decision-related.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for the existence and variety of warrants (all explicit in matrix field 10).
**MODERATE** for the corpus-level observation that **no study interrogates the provenance
or validity of the external competency frameworks it adopts**. Reasoning: this is an
inference from silence, but the silence is documented rather than merely absent — A02's
field 22 explicitly records that how the twelve competencies were developed or validated is
not reported, and A03's field 21 records that the mapping index's correspondence to actual
pedagogical content is not evidenced. Within what the notes preserve, the pattern holds;
it cannot be strengthened beyond MODERATE from the note layer alone.

## D5. Configuration of decision authority, and the intermediary function

### Definition

Who holds authority to decide, who influences without deciding, and whether a
non-deciding intermediary (supplying method, data or facilitation) is interposed between
them.

### Analytical question

Is "top-down vs. bottom-up" adequate to describe how curricular decisions are actually
organized in this corpus?

### Possible values / manifestations

**Not a binary and not a single continuum — a configuration space.** At least eight
configurations are documented:

1. **Unitary intermediate leader** — A13 (one Program Director carries review, redesign,
   and ~200 hours of approval writing).
2. **Peer collective / plenary** — A01 (full departmental faculty), A04 (ten-person mixed
   faculty–administrator team), A05 (formulation group plus dean).
3. **Small technical committee** — A02 (three named officers).
4. **Expert panel plus algorithm** — A03 (five experts supply weights; the model decides).
5. **Central initiative with devolved unit enactment** — A06 (central strategy, fifteen
   autonomous departments), A11 (provost's office plus departments, described by its own
   source as an unresolved hybrid).
6. **Local decision-makers plus external non-authoritative support** — A14 (support team
   as "coach," explicitly not technocratic), A10 (developers cap their own speaking time
   below 10%), A12 (developers as advisors to academic "clients").
7. **Team lacking authority over part of its own object** — A08 (7 of 11 required units
   controlled; attempts to modify the other 4 failed).
8. **Empirically derived multi-pattern** — A15 (six patterns: collaborative, bottom-up,
   top-down mandated, externally driven, isolated, limited).

### Supporting studies

- **A15** is the only study that derives configurations empirically rather than describing
  one, and it finds them associated with *different depths of outcome*: collaborative
  (N=19) and bottom-up (N=26) reach redesign; top-down mandated (N=30) mostly reaches
  build-in with "little formal participation, absent unified vision, low ownership, and
  faculty resistance."
- **A11** documents the hybrid case honestly: technical-rational origin in the provost's
  office plus deliberate department empowerment, with its own source declining to resolve
  whether this is genuine reconciliation or a buy-in strategy.
- **A06** shows that authority configuration inside the institution is not decisive:
  external PSRBs are argued to outrank both centre and department.

**The intermediary function** is a recurrent sub-value with seven instances: A10 and A12
(educational developers), A14 (scientific support team), A06 ("pedagogical translators"),
A11 (instructional designers named as a *requirement* for deep change), A08 (instructional
designer, partial involvement), A13 (a plain-language "Reference Guide" functioning as a
substitute for an absent intermediary). The convergent proposition: **an actor who supplies
method and absorbs labour without holding decision authority recurs as a condition of
curricular change.**

### Variations

Intermediaries differ in what they supply: *method* (A12), *labour and data* (A10),
*neutral framing of the decision space* (A14's Briefing Note documenting alternatives and
their impacts), *translation between institutional and disciplinary registers* (A06).

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A04 is a decisive counterexample to the necessity of a neutral intermediary**: its
  facilitator was the academic vice-president — an *authority holder* — and although his
  leadership was contested ("questions about leadership of the process being held by an
  administrator"), a four-year comprehensive reform is reported as completed. A01 was
  similarly self-facilitated.
- **A10 supplies the counterweight from inside its own findings**: the intermediary creates
  dependency. Participants stated a low likelihood of continuing without facilitation.
- **A03** has no meaningful authority configuration to describe: "stakeholder engagement"
  reduces operationally to five experts making pairwise comparisons, a point its own
  field 21 flags.
- **A07, A09, A16** address actors generically; NOT APPLICABLE / generic reference is
  correct here and must not be read as a finding about actor absence.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Feeds **D6**: A10, A11 and A13 explain non-participation by absent enablers rather than
opposition. Feeds **D8**: facilitated configurations report relational success criteria.
Does *not* reduce to **D7**: A14 and A03 both interpose an expert, but one is deliberately
non-prescriptive and the other is algorithmic.

### Analytical role

Actor-related · organizational · decision-related.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for configuration variety (all explicit in matrix fields 3 and 9).
**MODERATE** for the intermediary proposition: seven instances, but two clear
counterexamples (A04, A01), and the strongest supporting evidence (A10, A14) is
insider self-evaluation.
**TENTATIVE** for claims about the relative power of non-participating actors: A06's
comparative claim is the corpus's only one and its own field 21 records that how the
comparison was operationalized is not described.

## D6. Nature of the impediment

### Definition

What kind of thing is claimed to stop, slow, or deform curricular change. The dimension
distinguishes *kinds* of impediment, not their severity.

### Analytical question

Is what the corpus calls "resistance" one phenomenon or several — and is it opposition at
all?

### Possible values / manifestations

Four qualitatively distinct kinds, all documented:

**(i) Resource and labour-accounting impediments** — the required work exceeds what
institutional systems allocate or recognize.
A08 (workload model allocates 39 hours per major unit redevelopment; team found 200–250
hours required), A13 (~200 hours of bureaucratic writing on top of regulated teaching
duties, causing temporary abandonment of the author's own research), A10 (~100 collective
developer-hours for a three-faculty program), A04 (no financial resources or teaching-load
reduction at project outset), A14 (time and human resources), A07 (workload credit named
as a necessary condition), A11 (deep change argued to require "sustained external
resources and instructional designers"), A01 (budget constraints, faculty fatigue), A15
(lack of resources in the "limited institutional change" cluster).

**(ii) Governance and procedural throughput** — approval machinery consumes the change.
A13 (103 documents through Academic Board; historical average 9), A11 (bureaucratic
barriers in the university/state approval system named as one of the two most-cited
obstacles), A05 (state-level approval), A01.

**(iii) Epistemic-infrastructural impediments** — the *format* of institutional systems
excludes legitimate curricular content or misrepresents it.
A08 (behavioural-only outcome templates make the "becoming/being" domain unregisterable),
A06 (contractually binding programme specifications; exam structures at 70–90% of final
grades), A09 (documental compliance can coexist with severe hidden dysfunction), A11 (the
complexity algorithm scores a pedagogically beneficial change as worse).
**This is the corpus's most distinctive impediment type and is irreducible to (i) or (iv).**

**(iv) Dispositional and cultural impediments** — attitudes, identity, territory.
A01 (senior faculty comfort, junior faculty tenure-clock constraints, departmental power
disputes), A04 (faculty autonomy, disciplinary "territory wars"), A15 (faculty resistance
under top-down mandate; ownership deficits), A09 (student resistance; leaders as
monopolistic "gatekeepers"), A13 (systemic resistance; "behind closed doors"
decision-making), A05 (academic silos).

### Supporting studies

The dimension's payoff is a **derivable reinterpretation**: three studies recast (iv) as a
consequence of (i).
- **A11** is pivotal because it measured attitude and behaviour separately and found them
  decoupled: faculty reported high perceived usefulness (4.23 ± 0.75) and *very low*
  concern (1.27 ± 0.46), yet no department attempted signature work or learning-outcome
  transparency. Its own field 21 records this as a documented discrepancy the source does
  not resolve.
- **A10** locates the impediment in absent time and expertise, not opposition — faculty
  welcomed the process and still expected not to continue it.
- **A13** names workload allocation systems that "reward pure research over pedagogical
  innovation" and describes bureaucracy as "an active disincentive."

### Variations

A08's version of (iii) is *critical/epistemological* (positivist institutional epistemology
vs. constructivist pedagogy); A11's version is *technical* (a metric with poor construct
validity). Same category, very different theoretical register — worth preserving rather
than merging.

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A01 and A04 genuinely locate impediments in disposition**, and their evidence is
  first-hand: comfort with prepared courses, tenure-clock pressure, territoriality,
  contested administrator leadership. They are not reducible to the structural reading and
  must be preserved as a divergent explanation, not smoothed away.
- **A09 has no "facilitators" construct at all** (matrix field 13): its positive pole is
  standard attainment, not enabling conditions. This makes A09 the corpus's cleanest case
  of the *barriers vs. absent enablers* distinction being collapsed by design.
- **A03** reports no impediments in the organizational sense (field 12: the closest
  analogues are methodological limitations). This is correct for a planning-model study and
  must not be read as a finding that its context had no barriers.
- **A16's** impediments (scheduling, insufficient practical activities, textbook quality)
  are *delivery-condition* impediments reported by students — a fifth kind that appears in
  only one study and is therefore recorded but not generalized.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Directly conditions **D8**: if impediments are structural, success cannot be attributed to
attitude change. Overlaps with **D5** where authority *is* the impediment (A08's lack of
control over 4 of 11 units).

### Analytical role

Explanatory · organizational · implementation-related.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for the four-kind taxonomy: each type is explicitly documented, several with
figures.
**MODERATE** for the structural reinterpretation of resistance: it rests on three studies,
of which A11 presents it as an unresolved discrepancy rather than a conclusion, A13 is a
single-author autoethnography, and A10 has three participants. The reading is well
motivated but not established.
**MODERATE** for the magnitudes in (i): A08's 39-vs-200–250-hour comparison is the corpus's
most striking figure, but its field 22 records that how those hours were measured is not
reported; A13's 200 hours is author self-report; A10's ~100 hours is study-internal.

## D7. Degree and form of prescription of the study's output

### Definition

How strongly, and in what form, a study tells others what to do — from an algorithm that
returns an answer to a stance that explicitly refuses to supply one.

### Analytical question

What does this corpus think curricular knowledge should *look like*?

### Possible values / manifestations

A continuum with qualitative breaks:

| Form | Studies | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Algorithmic / optimizing | A03 | BWM–FFR–MCGP returns a selection and a schedule |
| Staged procedural roadmap | A07 (9 steps), A16 (4 steps), A05 (10 steps), A02 (ToC as roadmap) | Numbered, generic, transferable |
| Diagnostic instrument / framework | A09 (37 standards, 19 inhibitors, 8 domains), A06 (28-indicator rubric), A15 (typology plus self-reflection scheme) | Structures judgement without dictating action |
| Heuristic process model | A01 (non-linear two-phase, claimed "replicable"), A12 (Figure 1 model), A13 (claimed "replicable guidelines") | Offers shape, denies determinacy |
| Deliberately non-prescriptive | A11 ("non-prescriptive reflective guide"), A14 (coach role; "preservation of multiple didactic frameworks, avoiding dogmatic imposition"), A10 (facilitator speech capped below 10%), A12 ("no universal recipes") | Refusal is the position |
| No prescriptive output | A08 (barriers identified; only an indirect "buffers" suggestion), A04 (grounded theory plus role-specific recommendations) | Descriptive/critical |

### Supporting studies

- **A16** is the corpus's strongest prescriptive claim: standardization and scientific rigor
  are treated as normative goals, and ten models are collapsed into four steps argued to
  eliminate redundancy while preserving systematicity.
- **A14** is the strongest anti-prescriptive claim: scientific support "functions not as a
  technocratic imposition of inaccessible theoretical models but similarly to a coach."
- **A11** occupies a rare position: it deploys a quantitative instrument and frames the
  accompanying guide as explicitly non-prescriptive.

### Variations

**Four studies hedge their own prescriptions internally**: A12 supplies a model and denies
universal recipes; A11 supplies metrics and warns against optimizing them; A13 claims
replicable guidelines while acknowledging institution-specific vulnerabilities; A16 argues
for standardization and model-context fit simultaneously. The corpus's prescriptive outputs
characteristically arrive with self-limiting clauses.

### Negative / boundary cases

- Prescription is **not** reducible to study type. A05 is a descriptive single case that
  produces a ten-step prescriptive roadmap; A11 is an empirical intervention that refuses
  prescription; A15 is a meta-study that produces a deliberately non-developmental
  typology. Any attempt to read D7 off "article type" fails on these three.
- **A09** is a hard boundary case: its framework is highly structured (37/19/8) yet its
  central argument is *against* checklist application ("reviewers" vs. "interpreters").
  Structured form, anti-procedural intent.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Partially predicted by **D1** but broken by A11. Independent of **D9**: A05 and A13 make
strong prescriptive claims from non-comparative single cases, and A15 makes weak
prescriptive claims from 131 cases — an inverse relationship between evidentiary breadth
and prescriptive confidence in these instances.

### Analytical role

Normative · methodological · conceptual.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG.** Every placement rests on explicit statements in matrix fields 17, 18 or 21.

## D8. Criterion of success accepted by the study

### Definition

What each study is willing to count as evidence that a curricular change worked.

### Analytical question

On what basis does this corpus claim that anything succeeded?

### Possible values / manifestations

| Criterion | Studies | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Learner-outcome evidence | A16 only | Second-hand: 13 of 18 underlying studies report knowledge/skill score gains; its own field 21 flags a probable selection filter |
| Institutional proxy metric | A05 (enrollment 13→25→31→projected 40), A08 (first-year retention >90% amid 30% enrollment growth), A11 (complexity score) | A11 is the only one to report a **null** result |
| Structural / documentary output | A02 (maps filled; assessments modified), A13 (103 documents approved; 11→2 programs; 25 subjects redesigned), A03 (schedule balance; sensitivity stability), A05 (state approval; Center founded), A11 (rubric progress) | The corpus's modal criterion |
| Relational / cultural outcome | A01 ("re-established more honest relationships," described as an unexpected behavioural benefit), A04 (boundary dissolution; shared responsibility; community), A10 (CoP formation), A14 (transparency; instructors cross-referencing) | A genuinely distinct category |
| Participant self-report / satisfaction | A10, A11 (survey), A12 ("people return"), A14 (pilot questionnaires), A04 | Often the sole evaluative instrument |
| Depth of institutional embedding | A15 | denial → bolt-on → build-in → redesign |
| Feasibility / internal coherence only | A03, A07, A09, A16 (framework not yet field-validated) | Correct for the study type |

### Supporting studies

- The **relational criterion** is the most under-recognized finding here. A01, A04 and A10
  — three different countries, disciplines, methods and decades — independently report
  improved collegial relations as a *primary* outcome of curricular review, not a
  side-effect. A01 explicitly calls it unexpected.
- **A11** is the corpus's most disciplined evaluative case: it declared a metric in advance,
  measured it pre/post, and reported that no average reduction occurred (four programs
  down, four up, one unchanged), then argued against the metric as an end-goal.
- **A02** demonstrates how far documentary success can travel: the reported outcome is that
  objective maps were filled and assessments modified; whether student prognostication
  competence improved is recorded in field 22 as not reported.

### Variations

Within "institutional proxy," A08's use of retention is **internally contested by its own
analysis**: the study critiques metric-driven quality evaluation while evidencing its own
success with retention (field 21 records this tension).

### Negative / boundary cases

- **A06 accepts no success criterion at all** — it evaluates degree of documentary
  engagement at a two-year snapshot of a nine-year reform and explicitly declines
  outcome claims. This is a boundary case that strengthens rather than weakens the
  dimension.
- **A09, A07, A16, A03** produce frameworks or models whose "success" is coherence and
  feasibility. NOT APPLICABLE is correct and is not a deficiency finding.

### Relationships with other dimensions

Determined jointly by **D2** (located difficulty) and **D3** (which side of the
representation gap the evidence sits on). Constrained by **D9**.

### Analytical role

Evaluative · methodological · conceptual.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for the taxonomy and for the scarcity of learner-outcome evidence — this is
directly countable across the sixteen entries and is explicitly acknowledged as future
work by A01, A02, A05, A11 and A13 themselves.
**MODERATE, and deliberately not upgraded, for the relational-outcome convergence.** The
three supporting studies (A01, A04, A10) share a methodological vulnerability: in each,
the people reporting the improved relationships are the people who underwent the process,
with no independent instrument or external evaluator (A01 field 21; A04's insider-chair
limitation; A10's three-participant self-report). **Three converging studies with a common
vulnerability do not compound to strong evidence.**

## D9. Evidentiary architecture: researcher position and comparative reach

### Definition

Two coupled properties of how a study generates evidence: (a) the researcher's position
relative to the change being studied, and (b) whether the design permits any comparison at
all.

### Analytical question

Which corpus-level claims are structurally supportable, and which are supported only by
repetition?

### Possible values / manifestations

**(a) Position**
- *Insider as change agent*: A04 (researcher chaired the reform he studied), A13 (the
  Program Director is the author), A08 (project leader), A14 (support team evaluates its
  own intervention), A10 (developers evaluate their own facilitation), A05 (first author
  became program director), A01 (the department's own faculty).
- *Institutionally internal but external to the studied unit*: A11 (provost's office team
  studying departments).
- *External / documentary*: A06.
- *Practitioner studying other practitioners*: A12.
- *Modeller, not implementer*: A03.
- *Secondary literature*: A09, A15, A16.
- *Conceptual*: A07.

**(b) Comparative reach**
- *None (single, non-comparative)*: A01, A02, A03, A04, A05, A07, A08, A10, A13, A14 — **ten
  of sixteen**.
- *Within-institution cross-unit*: A06 (15 departments), A11 (9 programs, 7 departments,
  5 colleges).
- *Pre/post within case*: A02 (objective counts), A11 (complexity), A08 (retention).
- *Cross-case aggregate*: A09 (13 articles), A16 (18 articles), A15 (131 cases).
- *Cross-practitioner*: A12 (6 interviews, 22 questionnaires).

### Supporting studies

- **A15** is the only study with the reach to establish that different change
  configurations produce systematically different depths of outcome (N=19/26/30/22/26/8
  across six clusters, with indicator species analysis).
- **A06 and A11** are the only primary studies able to observe *heterogeneity of response
  to the same reform*. Both find it. Ten other studies could not have found it had it
  existed.

### Variations

Insider position varies in acknowledgement. A04 describes explicit bias-control procedures
(pre-recording responses; a six-month wait). A13 acknowledges reliance on a personal
reflective diary. A14 and A10 do not report an independent evaluator (both flagged in
their field 21).

### Negative / boundary cases

**A08 is the decisive counterexample to any simple "insiders report success" rule.** It is
an insider action-learning study whose findings are predominantly barriers and structural
failure, and whose single positive result (retention) it partially undercuts. A14 is
insider and reports a partial failure of its integrative block course. A11 is
semi-internal and reports a null result on its own headline metric. The
position→valence covariation is therefore **partial and must not be stated as a rule**.

**A12** is a boundary case for reach: it compares practitioners but has no curriculum case,
and its own field 21 records an unresolved divergence between what interviewees named as a
legitimate starting point (presenting research) and what questionnaire respondents actually
preferred (low interest in that same starting point).

### Relationships with other dimensions

Governs the confidence attachable to every other dimension. Interacts with **D8**: nine
insider studies plus documentary success criteria is the configuration most likely to
generate favourable findings.

### Analytical role

Methodological · evaluative.

### Evidence strength

**STRONG** for the comparative-reach facts: directly readable from matrix fields 4, 5 and 7.
**TENTATIVE** for the position→valence covariation: it is a real pattern with at least
three named counterexamples (A08, A14, A11), and it is offered as an interpretive caution,
not a finding.

# Part II — Rejected candidate dimensions

Rejecting a dimension with reasoning is a result. Each below was tested against all
sixteen studies and dropped.

**"Domain / discipline" (medical, sustainability, business, etc.).** Rejected as a
dimension. It has descriptive value and one real corpus-composition signal (the four
medical-education studies A02, A07, A09, A16 all concern mapping, standards, or models,
while the four sustainability studies A03, A05, A14, A15 all concern depth of integration).
But it explains nothing that D1, D2 and D7 do not explain better, and using it would
license domain-based clusters that A09 immediately breaks — a medical-education study
arguing *against* checklist standardization.

**"Study type / methodology."** Rejected as a dimension, retained only as a component of
D9. Tested and failed: prescription (D7) does not track it (A05 descriptive→prescriptive;
A11 empirical→non-prescriptive), and conception of curriculum (D1) does not track it (A03
and A11 are both quantitative and hold opposite normative positions).

**"Institutional scale" as a standalone dimension.** Rejected and absorbed into D9(b). Scale
mattered only through what it allowed a design to detect; once that was captured by
comparative reach, the residue was pure description.

**"Top-down vs. bottom-up."** Rejected as a dimension and demoted to one value inside D5.
It fails on A11 (self-described hybrid, unresolved by its own source), A06 (neither pole is
dominant; PSRBs are), A13 (a middle leader who is neither), and A14 (an external actor who
deliberately holds no authority). It survives only as a descriptor of *initiation impetus*,
which is exactly how A15 uses it.

**"Barriers" as a dimension.** Rejected as too coarse — it merges four phenomena (D6) that
have different remedies and different evidentiary status. Retaining it would have made the
corpus look convergent where it is divergent.

**"Curriculum mapping" as a dimension.** Rejected. Mapping is an artifact that serves at
least three different functions across the corpus (see Conceptual distinctions, below); a
dimension named after the artifact would conceal the functional difference that matters.

**"Crisis / external shock as change condition."** Rejected as a dimension: A13 is the sole
instance (COVID-19 as catalyst), and A06 mentions COVID and Black Lives Matter only as a
future-research question. Recorded below as a gap and a singleton, not built into a
framework. One study is not a dimension — though it may still be an important finding.

# Part III — Adjudication of the proposed distinctions

Each was tested against the corpus. Verdicts are independent of whether the distinction is
intuitively appealing.

### 1. Curricular diagnosis vs. decision-making vs. design vs. implementation vs. evaluation
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.** Diagnosis is clearly separable and the corpus says so: A02 frames
prior literature as stopping at diagnosis; A09 separates auditing from diagnosing; A07's
map is representation. Implementation is clearly separable: A08's entire premise. Design is
separable: A03, A16, A05. But **"decision-making" as a distinct stage is weakly supported**
— only A03 and A12 isolate it, and A14 and A04 argue decision-making *is* the process, not
a stage within it. More importantly, four studies explicitly deny the sequence is lived as
stages (A01, A04, A13, A15). Verdict: analytically useful as an *analyst's* partition;
misleading if presented as a description of practice.

### 2. What should change vs. how change decisions are made vs. how change is institutionalized
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED**, and the strongest structural feature of the corpus. The three
question-types have nearly disjoint study sets: *what* — A03, A05, A13, A16, A02; *how
decided* — A04, A12, A14, A10, A01; *how institutionalized* — A15 (the only study whose
central construct it is), with A10 and A08 contributing failure cases. **Only A05 and A15
substantially span more than one.** The near-disjointness is itself the finding.

### 3. Initiation vs. sustaining
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED.** A12 is initiation-only by explicit design. A15 finds different
mechanisms govern each of its two phases. A10 documents success at initiation with an
explicit expectation of non-continuation. A08 documents practice ending with funding.
A01, A02, A04, A05, A11 and A13 all record institutionalization as NOT REPORTED or as
future work. The corpus is heavily initiation-weighted and knows it.

### 4. Top-down vs. bottom-up vs. collaborative vs. distributed vs. externally mandated
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED for initiation impetus; MISLEADING if applied to whole processes.**
A15 derives these categories empirically and they hold there. Applied to whole processes
they fail on A11, A06, A13 and A14 (see D5, Rejected dimensions). Use A15's vocabulary
only for the question A15 asked.

### 5. Formal structures vs. informal processes
**SUPPORTED, with a directional refinement the corpus makes available.** A15 states that
informal collaboration among champions can compensate for absent formal leadership
support; A04 finds informal community was the "principal motivational fuel" where formal
resourcing was absent; A13's informal advocacy changed a formal rite. But A08 finds
informal pedagogical intent *cannot* survive adverse formal documentation requirements, and
A06 finds formal external requirements outrank formal internal policy. **Synthesized
proposition: informality can substitute for missing formal support, but does not override
adverse formal structure.** Confidence MODERATE (four studies, different objects; the
proposition is derived by juxtaposition, not asserted by any study).

### 6. Actors participating in decisions vs. actors exerting influence without formal participation
**SUPPORTED as a distinction; the corpus's evidence about the second category is almost
entirely indirect.** Non-participating influencers are documented throughout: PSRBs (A06),
TEQSA (A08), accreditors (A09, A10, A13), state bodies (A05, A11), government and the
Supreme Court (A15, India subgroup), employers (A01, A13), students as a source of
resistance (A09). **No study in the corpus studies any of these actors directly.** They
appear as inferred pressures. A06 is the only study making a comparative power claim, and
its own field 21 records that the operationalization is not described. This is a real
distinction sitting on thin evidence — see Gap G4.

### 7. Decision criteria vs. contextual constraints
**SUPPORTED but better handled as a sub-distinction than a dimension.** A14 gives it its
best articulation: the permanent "area of tension" between the theoretically optimal and
the institutionally possible curriculum. A03 separates them cleanly by design (competency
weights are criteria; credit limits and prerequisites are constraints). A11 shows a
constraint overriding a criterion (course-splitting for practical benefit *raises* the
complexity score). A08 shows constraints determining which criteria can be applied at all.
Kept inside D4 and D6 rather than promoted, to avoid duplicating D6.

### 8. Barriers vs. absence of enabling conditions
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED, and one of the corpus's most consequential distinctions.** A09 is the
clearest case *by omission*: it has no facilitators construct at all, using standard
attainment as the positive pole (field 13). A10's impediment is absent time and expertise,
not opposition. A11 measured attitudes and behaviour separately and found openness without
action. A13 names absent reward structures alongside active bureaucratic disincentive —
both kinds in one study. **Counter-position preserved:** A01 and A04 document impediments
that are genuinely dispositional (comfort, tenure-clock pressure, territoriality, contested
leadership) and are not reducible to missing enablers.

### 9. Institutional requirements vs. disciplinary/professional priorities
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED.** A06 is the paradigm case (disciplinary identity vs. central
strategy; PSRBs outranking both). A08 (institutional documentation epistemology vs.
professional ethical formation). A11 (departmental ownership vs. provost initiative). A04
(disciplinary territoriality vs. corporate curriculum). A05 (academic silos requiring a new
organizational home). A13 (accreditation and bureaucracy vs. disciplinary reform).
**Boundary cases:** A03 exhibits no such conflict (the expert panel is simultaneously
disciplinary and institutional); A07 and A16 abstract from it entirely.

### 10. Curriculum as content structure vs. curriculum as organizational process
**SUPPORTED but must be expanded beyond binary.** See D1: the corpus documents at least
six conceptions, and the two proposed here are only the two most populated. A06
(curriculum as text/contract) and A09 (curriculum as organism) are neither, and A08's
"becoming/being" domain is claimed to be unrepresentable within the content-structure pole.
Presenting this as a binary would erase four distinct positions.

### 11. Intended/documented vs. enacted curriculum
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED as a concept; the corpus's endorsement far exceeds its measurement.**
Six studies name a version (A02, A06, A07, A08, A09, A11). Only A08, A14 and A11 gather
any enactment-side evidence, and A11's is incidental. A06 measures documents while
theorizing the gap, and its own analysis notes that its "Application" measure is itself
documentary. See D3.

### 12. Incremental/local adaptation vs. structural/transformation-level change
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED — the corpus's strongest convergence.** Five studies, four countries,
five methods: A15 (four-level depth scale), A11 (low-hanging fruit vs. deep pedagogical
change), A06 (Active/Engaged/Passive; language-application drop), A04 (Fullan's reculturing
vs. restructuring), A05 (cosmetic relabeling critique). **Minority position preserved:**
A06 alone offers a *developmental* reading of superficial uptake — linguistic conformity as
a "necessary but insufficient threshold" that "may represent the beginning of a minimal
contractual agreement enabling later reforms." Every other study reads shallow uptake as
failure. The corpus cannot adjudicate this, because no study tracks a shallow-uptake unit
long enough to see whether it deepens (Gap G2).

### 13. Standardized approaches vs. context-sensitive adaptation
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED as a live disagreement.** Standardizers: A16 (standardization named as
a normative goal), A07 (a definitive nine-step process), A03 (an algorithm), A05 (a
ten-step roadmap). Contextualizers: A12 ("no universal recipes"), A14 (anti-technocratic;
multiple didactic frameworks preserved), A15 (six patterns; context-dependent
trajectories), A11 (explicitly non-prescriptive), A01 (against linear models).
**Counterexample to any domain-based explanation:** A09 is a medical-education study and
sits firmly with the contextualizers ("reviewers" vs. "interpreters").

### 14. Technical/analytical decision-support vs. social/participatory processes
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED — the corpus's sharpest single cleavage.** A03 is the technical pole
in pure form; its own field 21 records that the "He Principle" of stakeholder engagement
reduces operationally to five experts supplying ratings. A04, A14, A10 and A12 are the
social pole. **The most informative cases are the middle:** A11 uses an algorithm inside a
consultative process and then argues against the algorithm's normative use; A10's
analytical report functions socially, as a boundary object; A14's Briefing Note supplies
neutral data precisely so that local actors can decide. A03's He-Xie framing *claims* to
unite the poles; the matrix records that the human side is thinly realized. So the corpus
contains one asserted synthesis and two achieved ones.

### 15. Curriculum mapping as representation/diagnosis vs. mapping as support for decisions
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED, and should be expanded to three functions** — this is a distinction
the corpus generates rather than one imposed on it. See Conceptual distinctions, C-1.

### 16. Implementation success vs. institutionalization vs. sustainability
**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — the corpus demonstrates the distinction's necessity mainly through
its own silence.** A15 is the only study designed to address institutionalization. A10 and
A08 report sustainability failure explicitly (post-facilitation collapse expected; funding
withdrawal ending paid participation). A05 reports institutionalization markers — approval,
a director, a Center, transfer partnerships, enrollment growth — over roughly two years and
without outcome data. A13 reports one durable procedural change (peer-review
simplification). Everyone else records NOT REPORTED or future work. The distinction is
sound; the evidence base for the second and third terms is very thin. See Gap G1 and G2.

# Part IV — Important conceptual distinctions the corpus itself generates

These were not on the tested list. They emerged from comparison and are promoted here.

### C-1. Three functions of a curricular mapping artifact
The corpus uses "mapping" for three non-equivalent functions:
- **Representation / communication** — A07: making visible what is taught where, so that
  teachers and students can know it.
- **Diagnosis** — A02 (locating a competency gap by counting), A11 (finding publication
  errors and revealing that catalog requirements are routinely waived), A09 (explicitly
  positioning its viability framework as the layer mapping *cannot* supply: not where the
  gaps are, but why).
- **Social mediation** — A10: the report and alignment map function as a "point-at-able
  object" and, interpretively, as a Wenger boundary object; their value lies in anchoring
  collective reflection, not in their informational content. A14's Briefing Note serves the
  same function for macro-level decisions.
Confidence: **STRONG.** All three functions are explicitly described in their respective
matrix entries, and A02 and A09 explicitly criticize the limits of the others.

### C-2. Curricular labour and its institutional accounting
A distinction between the work curricular change requires and what institutional systems
allocate, recognize or reward. Supported by A08 (39 vs. 200–250 hours), A13 (~200 hours),
A10 (~100 developer-hours), A04 (no load reduction), A07 (workload credit as a stated
necessary condition), A11 ($1,500 incentive; sustained resources needed for deep change),
A14, A01. **This is the corpus's most concrete and most quantified convergence** and it is
absent from every study's own concept list as a named construct.
Confidence: **STRONG** for the qualitative claim; **MODERATE** for magnitudes (measurement
procedures are NOT REPORTED for A08's figures; A13's is self-report).

### C-3. Non-transferability of a curricular artifact from the process that produced it
A14's integrative block course partially failed when delegated to an instructor who had not
participated in the redesign — described in its source as a critical and vulnerable point.
A10's participants expected not to sustain changes after the facilitators left. A06 found
that only departments fusing the reform into their own epistemology enacted it. A15 links
participatory vision-building to durability. A04 makes shared vision a precondition of
technical work.
**This sits in direct tension with the corpus's transferable outputs** — A07's nine steps,
A16's four steps, A05's ten steps, A03's model — all offered for use elsewhere.
Confidence: **MODERATE.** Four supporting studies, but A14's causal attribution has
unexplored alternatives (its own field 21), A15 is second-order with acknowledged success
bias, A10's evidence is prospective concern rather than observed failure, and A06 measures
documents only. **This is a case where four studies do not compound to STRONG.**

### C-4. Reform-by-creation vs. reform-by-modification
The two cases of genuinely deep change in the corpus achieved it under unusual conditions:
A05 **created a new program** rather than modifying an existing one (and still had to
revise it substantially after launch when it "resembled a modified biology degree"); A13
achieved 11→2 program rationalization **under crisis conditions** it explicitly credits as
having broken habitual resistance. A04's comprehensive reform took four years with senior
leadership chairing and no dedicated resourcing. By contrast, modification-under-normal-
conditions studies (A11, A06) report shallow uptake.
Confidence: **TENTATIVE.** This is a pattern across four studies with different designs; no
study makes the comparison, and there are too few deep-change cases to exclude alternative
explanations. Offered as a hypothesis the corpus makes visible, not as a finding.

# Part V — Cross-study relationships

Each entry states the *exact* point of relationship, not a topic label.

## CONVERGENCE

**V-1. Shallow curricular change is preferentially adopted; deep change is not.**
Studies: A06, A11, A15, A05, A13(diagnostic phase).
Exact point: not that reform is hard, but that *when given a choice, units select the
changes with the lowest negotiation and labour cost*. A11: prerequisite removal attempted
by 8 programs and completed by 100% of them, while **zero** departments attempted signature
work or learning-outcome transparency. A06: Assessment Reform and Active Learning were
"Present" or "Explicit" at the language level in **all** departments while only about
one-fifth applied them explicitly. A15: the top-down mandated cluster (N=30) reaches
build-in rather than redesign.
Contextual independence: UK / US / international meta-study / US / Australia; discourse
analysis / mixed-methods intervention / case survey / descriptive case / autoethnography.
The independence is high and the methods are unrelated.
Counterexamples and minority positions: **A06 itself** supplies the minority reading that
shallow linguistic uptake is a necessary threshold rather than a ceiling. **A13** and **A04**
document deep change actually achieved. **A02** documents narrow-but-deep change (one
competency, systemic intervention across three phases).
Confidence: **STRONG** for the tendency; the counterexamples constrain it to a tendency, not
a law.

**V-2. Curricular change labour is systematically under-accounted by institutions.**
Studies: A08, A13, A10, A04, A07, A11, A14, A01.
Exact point: the discrepancy is between *measured or estimated effort* and *allocated
recognition*, not between effort and willingness. A08 gives the sharpest figure (39 hours
allocated vs. 200–250 required per unit); A13 gives ~200 hours of approval writing carried
by one person; A10 gives ~100 developer-hours for a three-faculty program.
Confidence: **STRONG** qualitatively; **MODERATE** for magnitudes (A08's field 22 records
that the measurement method is not reported; A13's figure is author self-report).

**V-3. External regulatory and accreditation bodies materially shape curricula.**
Studies: A06, A08, A09, A10, A13, A05, A11, A15.
Exact point: these bodies determine not only content but **the format in which curriculum
may be expressed** (A08: TEQSA-derived templates requiring behavioural outcomes; A06:
programme specifications as contractually binding).
Confidence: **MODERATE.** Widely reported as context, almost never measured; A06's claim
that PSRBs outrank central policy is the only comparative claim and its own source does not
describe how it was established.

## PARTIAL CONVERGENCE

**V-4. Participation in the design process conditions faithful enactment.**
Studies: A14, A15, A04, A06, A10.
Exact point: not "participation is good," but that the *specific individuals who will
enact* a curricular change must have been part of producing it. A14's block course is the
only direct observation.
Confidence: **MODERATE** — see C-3 for why this does not reach STRONG despite four
supporting studies.

## DIVERGENCE

**V-5. Whether curriculum development can and should be standardized.**
A16, A07, A03, A05 (yes, in different forms) vs. A12, A14, A15, A11, A01 (no).
Exact point of disagreement: whether a general procedure can substitute for local judgement.
A16 treats standardization as directly affecting teaching-learning effectiveness; A12
concludes that "there are no universal recipes" from data on 6+22 practitioners.
Possible contextual explanation (**DERIVED, tentative**): the standardizers cluster in
medical education and operations research, fields with strong accreditation and
formal-method traditions. **This explanation is broken by A09**, a medical-education
scoping review that argues against checklist evaluation. Report the divergence; do not
attribute it confidently to domain.
Confidence: **STRONG** that the disagreement is real and substantive.

**V-6. Whether "resistance" is dispositional or structural.**
A01, A04, A15 (dispositional: comfort, territoriality, autonomy, ownership deficits) vs.
A10, A11, A13 (structural: unrewarded labour, bureaucratic cost, absent enablers).
Exact point: these are competing *explanations of the same observable* — non-adoption of
deep change. A11 is pivotal because it measured attitude and behaviour separately and found
them decoupled.
Confidence: **MODERATE** that the structural reading is better supported by the one study
that measured both; A11's survey is 18 respondents with an acknowledged self-selection
caveat, so this cannot be stated more strongly.

**V-7. Whether curricular structure should be simplified.**
A03 optimizes toward balance and treats deviation from targets as cost. A11 explicitly
argues that "blindly reducing complexity should not be established as a final goal," since
an unstructured curriculum shifts the coherence burden onto students — and documents its own
metric rising for a change it regards as pedagogically beneficial.
Exact point: both studies formalize curricular structure mathematically, so this divergence
**cannot be explained by a quantitative-vs-qualitative methodological split**.
Confidence: **STRONG.** Both positions are explicit.

**V-8. What a curriculum map is for.** A07 (representation) vs. A02 (diagnosis is
insufficient; the point is the transition to intervention) vs. A09 (mapping shows where,
not why) vs. A10 (its value is social mediation). See C-1.
Confidence: **STRONG.**

## CONTRADICTION

**Strict contradiction is rare in this corpus, and that is itself a finding.** Studies
differ so widely in object, scale, conception (D1) and success criterion (D8) that they
rarely make commensurable claims. The closest approach is V-7, where A03 and A11 formalize
the same property and draw opposite normative conclusions — but even there they are
answering different questions (how to plan vs. whether to optimize). Analysts should not
manufacture contradiction where the corpus offers incommensurability; the fragmentation is
the more important observation.

## COMPLEMENTARITY

**V-9. The trajectory is jointly covered but never by one study.**
A12 (initiation) + A04/A14/A10 (deliberation) + A08 (implementation) + A06/A11 (uptake
depth) + A15 (sustaining). Each is weak alone and none overlaps enough to corroborate
another. This is the corpus's principal complementarity — and also its principal
vulnerability, since each segment rests on one or two studies.

**V-10. Three layers of the mapping problem.**
A07 (how to represent) + A02 (how to move from a detected gap to an intervention) + A09
(why standards go unmet even when documented). Together they form a fuller account than any
one supplies, and A09 explicitly positions itself as the missing layer.

**V-11. The two halves of the technical/social cleavage.**
A03 (how to choose content formally) + A04/A14 (how content decisions are actually settled
socially). A03's He-Xie framework *claims* to unite exactly these two halves; the matrix
records that the human-coordination half is thinly instantiated. The corpus therefore
contains a stated synthesis that its own evidence does not realize — and two studies (A11,
A14) that realize a version of it without claiming a unifying theory.

## TENSION

**V-12. Studies that critique metric-driven evaluation evidence their own success with
metrics.** A08 (critiques "student-as-client" quality metrics; evidences success with
retention — flagged in its own field 21), A11 (warns against the complexity metric while
using it), A13 (critiques bureaucracy while counting approved documents), A05 (critiques
cosmetic programs while counting enrollment).

**V-13. Strong prescriptive claims from non-comparative single cases.** A05 (a ten-step
roadmap from one institution in one state policy context), A01 (a "replicable" framework
from one department), A13 ("replicable guidelines" from one crisis-affected university),
A02 (a single case described as demonstrating the ToC approach's effectiveness). All four
are flagged in their own field 21.

**V-14. Artifacts are argued to be non-transferable, yet transferable artifacts dominate the
corpus's outputs.** C-3 vs. A07/A16/A05/A03. Unresolved.

**V-15. Facilitator neutrality is argued to be vital, yet the corpus's most comprehensive
reform was chaired by an authority holder.** A10 argues neutrality is vital and its own
field 21 notes it was tested in a setting explicitly free of compulsory-reform pressure;
A04's four-year comprehensive reform was chaired by the academic vice-president, with the
administrator's leadership contested but not fatal. Coexistable, unresolved.

**V-16. Insider evaluation dominates the corpus's positive findings.** Seven studies
evaluate processes their authors ran (A01, A04, A05, A08, A10, A13, A14). A08 is the one
that reports predominantly negative findings — which is precisely why it carries
disproportionate evidential weight on impediments.

# Part VI — Gaps

A gap is something the corpus makes visible by not investigating it. Each is distinguished
from evidence of absence.

**G1. Learner-outcome evidence for curricular change.** Only A16 supplies any, at second
hand (13 of 18 underlying studies report post-course knowledge and skill score gains), and
its own field 21 flags a probable selection filter producing a 100% positive rate. Every
primary study either omits outcome data or explicitly defers it (A01, A02, A05, A11, A13).
**This is absence of evidence, honestly declared by the studies themselves — not evidence
that curricular change is ineffective.**

**G2. Whether shallow adoption deepens or plateaus.** A06's "language as threshold"
hypothesis is untestable within its own two-year snapshot of a nine-year reform; A15 asserts
that HEIs move between patterns but tracks no trajectory. No study follows a
passive/bolt-on unit long enough to find out. This gap is what prevents the corpus from
adjudicating its own strongest convergence (V-1).

**G3. Students as decision-making participants.** Across sixteen studies, students appear as
survey respondents (A01, A05, A10, A14), as institutional data (A11, A13), as a source of
resistance (A09), and as a mapping window (A07, window 10). **The single exception is
A15's bottom-up cluster (N=26), where initiation by "students and faculty through small
projects" is recorded** — second-order, aggregate evidence. No primary study in the corpus
places students in a curricular decision role.

**G4. Direct study of non-participating influential actors.** Accreditors, regulators,
employers and state bodies shape curricula throughout the corpus (V-3) but are studied by no
one. They are inferred from their effects.

**G5. Provenance and validity of the frameworks used as warrant.** A02's field 22 records
that how the twelve competencies were developed or validated is not reported; A03's field 21
records that the mapping index's correspondence to pedagogical content is not evidenced. The
UNESCO, ITAA, WFME/LCME/GMC and accreditation frameworks are adopted, not examined.
**Note-layer bounded:** the silence may lie in the literature notes rather than in the
original articles.

**G6. Failure cases and abandoned reforms.** A15 explicitly names success bias in its source
literature ("strong tendency to publish success stories, omitting structural failures").
A08 and A14 are the only studies foregrounding failure, and A14's is partial. **No study in
this corpus reports an abandoned reform.** Combined with V-16 (insider evaluation), this
means the corpus's overall optimism is structurally, not empirically, produced.

**G7. Comparative evaluation of alternative approaches.** A10 names it as future work
(comparing deep-advisory against standard faculty-development services); A03 names absent
comparison with other MCDM methods; A12 names the absence of any effectiveness measurement
for advising strategies. **Not one study in the corpus compares two approaches to curricular
change against each other.**

**G8. Geographic and system concentration.** Primary studies: United States ×6 (A01, A02,
A04, A05, A10, A11), Australia ×2 (A08, A13), UK ×1 (A06), Ireland/UK ×1 (A12),
Switzerland ×1 (A14), China ×1 (A03). Syntheses: A09 English-only; A16 English and Chinese
only; A15 with explicitly acknowledged geographic imbalance. Any generalization from this
corpus is a generalization about Anglophone and Western-European higher education plus one
Chinese case.

**G9. Disciplinary variation in change dynamics.** Only A06 compares disciplines within one
institution, and its field 22 records that only Medicine is named among the fifteen
departments. The corpus therefore cannot say whether curricular change works differently in
different disciplines, despite four studies attributing outcomes to disciplinary culture.

**G10. Crisis and external shock as change conditions.** A13 alone (COVID-19 as catalyst,
on the author's own interpretation, with no independent evidence recorded). A06 raises
COVID-19 and Black Lives Matter only as future research. A singleton finding on a condition
that plausibly matters a great deal.
