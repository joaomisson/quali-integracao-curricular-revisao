# Study Clusters — Corpus A01–A16

**Stage 2, Step 3.** Derived from the analytical dimensions established in
`analysis/corpus/thematic-coding.md` (D1–D9), not from domain, methodology or keyword
similarity.

## Method and standing caveats

Clusters below were built from **combinations** of established dimensions — principally D1
(conception of curriculum), D2 (locus of difficulty), D5 (authority configuration), D6
(nature of impediment), D7 (prescription) and D8 (success criterion). A grouping was
accepted only if it explained something beyond describing what its members are about.

**Overlapping and partial membership is used deliberately.** Primary membership means the
cluster's defining logic is the study's own organizing logic. Secondary membership means the
study exhibits the logic but is organized by something else. Boundary members exhibit the
logic with an important inversion or absence.

**Outlier protection.** A03, A07 and A12 have objects substantially different from
organizational change-process research (an optimization model, a guideline document, and a
study of practitioners rather than curricula). They are **not** forced into
change-process categories. Where the matrix records NOT APPLICABLE for them, that marker is
preserved as meaningful. Each is treated first as revealing a boundary of the corpus, and
only second as a classification problem.

**Note-layer bound.** Everything below traces to `analysis/corpus/evidence-matrix.md`, with
one targeted verification against `analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`. No dissertation
material was consulted.

**Headline conclusion, stated up front: a single mutually exclusive taxonomy is not adequate
for this corpus.** The five clusters below are one useful cut. The multi-axis grid in
Part III is the more faithful representation, and it is presented not as a supplement but as
the recommended primary structure.

# Part I — Proposed clusters

## Cluster 1 — Curriculum formalized as an analyzable object

### Defining logic

Studies that render a curriculum as a structure that can be represented, counted, computed
or searched, and whose principal contribution is an instrument or procedure operating on
that structure. Dimensional signature: **D1 values 1–2** (combinatorial object / relational
information structure) + **D7 toward the formal-procedural end** + **D8 = feasibility or
documentary output**. Crucially, in these studies the social organization of decision-making
is either absent, generic, or a small technical committee — never the object.

### Member studies

- **Primary:** A03, A07, A02
- **Secondary:** A09, A11

### Shared characteristics

The curriculum is knowable independently of the people enacting it. Contribution takes the
form of a portable artifact: a three-phase MCDM model (A03), a ten-window / nine-step
mapping framework (A07), a mapping-plus-Theory-of-Change procedure (A02). Success is
established internally — model consistency and sensitivity stability (A03), conceptual
coherence (A07), filled maps and modified assessment documents (A02).

### Important internal differences

These are three different projects sharing a conception, and the differences matter more
than the similarity:

- **A03 computes.** The model returns an answer (35 of 53 courses; a four-semester schedule).
- **A07 represents.** Its explicit purpose is communication — that teachers and students do
  not know what is taught where — and its own limitation section concedes little direct
  empirical evidence that maps improve learning.
- **A02 counts in order to intervene.** Its stated contribution is precisely the *transition*
  from diagnosis to action, framed as what prior mapping literature fails to do.

The secondary members sharpen this: **A09 formalizes the causes of failure rather than the
structure** (37 standards, 19 inhibitors, 8 domains) and explicitly argues against
checklist application. **A11 formalizes structure and then argues against optimizing it.**

### Boundary or partial members

**A11** is the corpus's principal cluster-bridging study. It deploys the Curricular Analytics
blocking/delay-factor model — as formal as anything in A03 — yet its findings and arguments
belong to Cluster 3. It is secondary here and primary there, and this dual membership is a
finding rather than an inconvenience: it demonstrates that formal instruments can be
deployed inside, and subordinated to, an institutional-process account.

**A09** is partial: its framework is highly structured but its central argument
("reviewers" vs. "interpreters") is anti-formalist.

**A16** is deliberately excluded as a member and treated instead in Cluster 5 — see below.

### Studies explicitly excluded despite surface similarity

- **A16** synthesizes *process models* rather than operating an instrument over a curriculum.
  Its four-step framework is a claim about how development should proceed, not a
  representation of a curriculum. Placing it here would conflate a procedure-for-people with
  an instrument-over-an-object.
- **A06** develops a 28-indicator rubric — formally as elaborate as A09's framework — but the
  rubric measures *departmental discourse about change*, not curriculum. Its conception of
  curriculum (text/contract) and its findings (cultural mediation of policy) belong to
  Cluster 3.
- **A15** applies a 111-variable coding scheme with cluster analysis, but the object coded is
  published literature about institutions, not curricula. Cluster 5.
- **A10** produced an alignment map and a fifteen-page report, but its finding is that the
  artifact worked *socially*, as a boundary object. Cluster 2.

### What this cluster helps explain

It explains why the corpus's formalizing studies do not accumulate. Representation (A07),
computation (A03) and diagnosis (A02, A09) are separate projects that partially criticize
each other: A02's premise is that mapping-as-diagnosis stops too early; A09's premise is that
standards-checking cannot reach causes; A07 concedes it lacks evidence that maps improve
learning. The cluster's payoff is showing that "curriculum mapping / modelling" names three
non-equivalent activities (thematic-coding C-1), which is why a reader encountering all three
finds apparent redundancy where there is actually disagreement.

### Alternative plausible grouping

Split by *function of the artifact* rather than by conception: {A07, A10} as artifacts for
communication and mediation; {A03, A11} as artifacts for computation; {A02, A09} as
artifacts for diagnosis. This grouping cuts A10 into the set and A11 out of Cluster 3, and it
tracks thematic-coding C-1 more directly. It is arguably more useful for anyone asking what
a curricular instrument is *for*, and less useful for asking what a curriculum *is*.

### Confidence

**MODERATE.** The shared conception is explicitly documented for all three primary members
(matrix fields 1, 2, 6). The downgrade from STRONG reflects genuine internal heterogeneity of
purpose and the fact that two of five members hold their membership only secondarily.

## Cluster 2 — Curricular change as facilitated social process

### Defining logic

Studies whose object is the deliberation itself: how a group comes to a shared position, who
helps it get there, and what the process does to the group. Dimensional signature: **D1 value
4** (curriculum as contextualized social process) + **D5 configurations 2 or 6** (peer
collective, or local decision-makers with non-authoritative external support) + **D8 =
relational outcome** + **D7 at the non-prescriptive end**.

The distinguishing test is not "did people participate" — participation appears almost
everywhere — but **whether the study's findings are about the group rather than the
curriculum**.

### Member studies

- **Primary:** A04, A14, A10, A12
- **Secondary:** A01

### Shared characteristics

The unit of analysis is a team or a practitioner, not a curriculum: A04's unit is a
ten-person faculty–administrator team; A14's is a redesign team plus a two-person support
team; A10's is three faculty plus two developers; A12's is individual practitioners. Success
is relational: boundary dissolution and community (A04), community-of-practice formation
(A10), transparency and cross-referencing among instructors (A14), "people come back" (A12).
Ownership is treated as a mechanism rather than a value.

### Important internal differences

- **Presence and character of the intermediary.** A14 and A10 interpose a deliberately
  non-authoritative external actor; A04's facilitator was the academic vice-president — an
  authority holder, whose leadership was contested; A01 was self-facilitated by the
  department itself.
- **A12 has no curriculum case at all.** It studies what practitioners do at the moment of
  initiation, across 6 interviews and 22 questionnaires. It belongs by orientation, not by
  object.
- **Theoretical register differs sharply**: A04 (grounded theory, postmodern curriculum,
  reculturing), A14 (Cornbleth's contextualized social process, teaching choreographies),
  A10 (Lave/Wenger communities of practice), A12 (Land's ecological model of developer
  orientations). These are not variants of one framework.

### Boundary or partial members

**A01** is secondary because its warrant is externally referenced (benchmarking, market,
alumni demand — Cluster 4) while its process machinery and one of its two headline outcomes
are relational. It is the study that shows the two clusters are not exclusive.

**A11** is a near-member and is deliberately not admitted: it emphasizes listening and
decentralized empowerment, but the initiative originated in the provost's office, the
process was instrument-led, and its findings are about depth of structural change. Its own
source declines to resolve whether the participatory framing was genuine reconciliation or a
buy-in strategy.

### Studies explicitly excluded despite surface similarity

- **A13** involved 17 faculty in review and 13 specialists in redesign, and used
  collaborative action research — yet the account is organized around a single leader's
  experience, the evidentiary instrument is her personal reflective diary, and the findings
  concern leadership burden and bureaucratic throughput. Deliberation is not its object.
- **A08** worked as a team through Action Learning cycles, but its findings are about
  workload models, casualization, documentation templates and quality metrics. The team is
  the vehicle, not the object.
- **A05** had a formulation group and surveys, but its process is organizational and
  developmental (design → approval → director-led revision → founding a Center), and its
  success criteria are enrollment and institutional structures.
- **A15** documents collaborative patterns across 131 cases, but at second order and as
  classification, not as an account of any deliberation.

### What this cluster helps explain

Two things. First, it explains **why relational outcomes appear in this corpus as primary
findings rather than side-effects** — for these studies, changing the group *is* the
mechanism by which the curriculum changes (A04's reculturing; A10's community of practice).
Second, it explains the specific form the sustainability problem takes in the corpus: where
change runs through a facilitated group, withdrawal of facilitation is a direct threat (A10's
participants expected not to continue; A14's block course failed when handed to a
non-participant). The facilitator-dependency problem is a property of this cluster, not of
curricular change in general.

### Alternative plausible grouping

Split by **whether an external intermediary is present**: {A14, A10, A12} as *third-party
facilitation studies* versus {A04, A01} as *self-organized collective studies*. This is
arguably sharper, because it isolates the corpus's recurring intermediary figure (D5) and
places A04 where it does real work — as a counterexample to the claim that neutral external
facilitation is necessary. The cost is losing the shared process ontology that unites all
five.

### Confidence

**STRONG** for A04, A14 and A10: the process ontology, the relational success criterion and
the facilitation focus are explicit in each.
**MODERATE** for A12, whose object is practitioners rather than a change process.

## Cluster 3 — Institutional apparatus as the determinant of curricular possibility

### Defining logic

Studies whose central finding is that what can be changed is set by institutional machinery —
workload models, staffing arrangements, documentation formats, quality regimes, approval
throughput, reward structures, and the relative authority of disciplines, centres and
external regulators — rather than by curricular ideas or willingness to change. Dimensional
signature: **D6 kinds (i), (ii) and especially (iii)** + **D2 located at implementation or
uptake depth** + **D8 = documentary or proxy metric**.

### Member studies

- **Primary:** A08, A06, A11, A13
- **Secondary:** A10, A09
- **Boundary:** A05, A04

### Shared characteristics

Reform succeeds "on paper" while enactment lags or shallows. The impediment is not
opposition. Each primary member supplies a distinct mechanism:

- **A08:** workload allocation (39 hours vs. 200–250 required), casual staffing, TEQSA-derived
  templates requiring behavioural outcome statements, and metric-driven quality evaluation
  inhibiting experimentation.
- **A06:** central strategy versus disciplinary epistemology, with external PSRBs argued to
  outrank both, and entrenched assessment structures (exams at 70–90% of final grades).
- **A11:** approval bureaucracy and departmental consensus named as the two most-cited
  obstacles, with 100% completion of low-cost structural changes and **zero attempts** at
  deep pedagogical change.
- **A13:** 103 approval documents against a historical average of 9, ~200 hours of
  bureaucratic writing, and workload systems rewarding research over pedagogical innovation.

### Important internal differences

**This cluster's most valuable property is that it unites two incompatible framings that
reach compatible conclusions.** A08 and A06 are critical and epistemological: A08 argues a
"chronic misalignment between an institutional positivist epistemology... and
social-constructivist/critical approaches"; A06 uses policy-enactment and transformational
learning theory. A11 and A13 are managerial and practitioner-facing: A11 offers a consultative
approach that reduces resistance to central administration; A13 offers replicable guidelines
for programme leaders. **Cross-framing convergence on the same mechanism is stronger evidence
than four studies sharing a framework would be**, because the framings do not share
assumptions that could jointly bias the result.

A second internal difference: A08 and A06 locate the impediment in the *format* of
institutional systems (D6-iii); A13 and A11 locate it in *throughput and reward* (D6-i, ii).

### Boundary or partial members

- **A05 exhibits the dimension with inverted valence.** Institutional apparatus appears first
  as an obstacle (academic silos; state approval) and then as the *solution*: a dedicated
  Center for Sustainability Studies was founded specifically to enable cross-college faculty
  affiliation. It shows the apparatus can be built rather than only endured.
- **A04** contributes an apparatus finding — the absence of financial resources or teaching-load
  reduction at project outset, leaving the reform dependent on intrinsic motivation — but
  organizes its account around deliberation (Cluster 2).
- **A09** supplies the cluster's theoretical vocabulary from a second-order position:
  documental compliance coexisting with hidden dysfunction; leadership as gatekeeping;
  faculty exclusion from decision-making as a structural inhibitor.
- **A10** supplies the labour-accounting finding (~100 developer-hours) but is organized
  around facilitation.

### Studies explicitly excluded despite surface similarity

- **A03** contains no institution in its account: no governance, no adoption, no
  implementation (verified against `analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`). Its field 12
  correctly records that the closest analogues to barriers are methodological limitations.
  Admitting it here would convert a NOT REPORTED into a substantive finding.
- **A07** names institutional support and workload credit as *conditions for success*, not as
  an empirical constraint it observed. It is a guideline, not a study of apparatus.
- **A16** identifies scheduling, practical-activity provision and textbook quality as
  problems — delivery conditions reported by students in secondary studies, not institutional
  machinery.

### What this cluster helps explain

It supplies the **mechanism** for the corpus's strongest convergence (thematic-coding V-1:
shallow change is preferentially adopted). A11 shows openness without action; A08, A13 and
A06 show why. Without this cluster, the depth finding is a description; with it, it is an
explanation.

It also explains a corpus-level asymmetry: the studies most critical of institutional metrics
are the ones with the least outcome data, because the metrics they reject are the ones their
institutions would have supplied.

### Alternative plausible grouping

Split by **kind of impediment** rather than by the fact of institutional determination:
{A08, A06, A09} as *epistemic-infrastructural* studies (institutional formats exclude or
misrepresent curricular content) versus {A13, A11, A10} as *labour-and-throughput* studies.
This is arguably more analytically precise, and it would relocate A09 from secondary to
primary status. Its cost: it separates A11 from A06, which together supply the corpus's only
evidence on within-institution heterogeneity.

### Confidence

**STRONG.** Every primary member's finding is explicit, several are quantified, the framings
are heterogeneous, and the cluster carries explanatory rather than descriptive weight.

## Cluster 4 — Externally warranted curricular modernization

### Defining logic

Studies in which the *content* question is answered from outside the academic unit — labour
market, employers, competitor programmes, accreditation, or a globally authored competency
framework — and the reform is framed as closing a currency or relevance gap. Dimensional
signature: **D4 warrants 1–3** (market demand, benchmarking, external frameworks) + **D2
located at design** + **D8 = documentary or proxy metric**.

### Member studies

- **Primary:** A01, A05, A13
- **Secondary:** A03, A02, A16

### Shared characteristics

External evidence is gathered specifically to justify content decisions: A01 (peer,
competitive and aspirant institution curricula; alumni interviewed in person across US
cities; a 50-respondent student/alumni survey), A05 (regional programme mapping showing no
competitors; vocational demand; 366- and 50-respondent demand surveys), A13 (employer
interviews; student focus groups; national teaching guidelines; new accreditation guidance).
The reform's object is modernization of content — logistics and supply chain (A01),
sustainability as an integrating lens (A05), digital and data-analytics competence (A13).

### Important internal differences

- **The nature of the external referent differs fundamentally.** A01 and A13 reference a
  labour market; A03 references a *normative* global framework (UNESCO's eight sustainability
  competencies); A05 references both market demand and state environmental policy; A02
  references an internally authored institutional competency set, which makes its membership
  weak.
- **A05 is internally divided against the cluster's logic**: it uses demand evidence while
  explicitly attacking "cosmetic" market-driven relabeling and insisting on newly authored
  courses over rearranged existing ones.

### Boundary or partial members

- **A02** is admitted only weakly: its warrant is internal (UVA's twelve competencies) even
  though its procedure resembles the cluster's.
- **A16** is secondary: model-context fit references discipline and course context rather than
  a market, but its framing (resource waste from poor model selection; standardization as a
  goal) shares the cluster's external-legitimation logic.

### Studies explicitly excluded despite surface similarity

- **A04** is the clean contrast case: warrant is a *collectively constructed philosophical
  vision* explicitly required to precede any discussion of syllabi. Same activity
  (comprehensive reform), opposite warrant.
- **A14** warrants change through instructors' prior local practice and HESD criteria, and
  explicitly refuses dogmatic imposition of an external line.
- **A08** warrants curriculum through disciplinary and professional-ethical formation, and
  argues that the institution's external-compliance warrant actively excludes a legitimate
  domain.
- **A06** analyses external authority (PSRBs) as a *competing* power over curriculum rather
  than adopting it as a warrant.
- **A11** is driven by an equity-and-access rationale internal to the institution's mission
  and student population, not by an external market referent.

### What this cluster helps explain

The hypothesis it supports: **where the content question is settled externally, a study needs
less deliberative machinery and reports less conflict about ends.** A05 and A13 fit — neither
reports substantive disagreement about *what* should change, only about how to get it
approved.

**This is also the cluster's chief internal challenge.** A01 has a strong external warrant and
*still* required structured conduct norms, reflective sessions, peer review and explicit
management of "difficult debates marked by resistance to change and departmental power
disputes." External warrant therefore does not eliminate deliberative cost. The cluster's
explanatory claim survives as a tendency at best.

### Alternative plausible grouping

Dissolve the cluster and redistribute by **what the external referent is**: {A01, A05, A13} as
*market/demand-referenced*, {A03, A09, A16, A02} as *framework/standards-referenced*. This is
more faithful to D4 and avoids the risk described below, but it separates studies that share
a recognizable modernization narrative.

### Confidence

**MODERATE, deliberately not higher.** This is the cluster most at risk of being a keyword
family: "market," "employability" and "relevance" recur across its members' vocabulary. Its
defence is that warrant-source predicts deliberative load and success criterion — a
prediction A01 partially falsifies from inside the cluster. Treat as provisional.

## Cluster 5 — Second-order synthesis and framework construction

### Defining logic

Studies whose object is a literature or a set of models rather than an institution, and whose
output is a framework or typology. Dimensional signature: **D9 = cross-case aggregate
reach** + **D8 = feasibility or internal coherence** + **institutional context correctly NOT
APPLICABLE**.

### Member studies

- **Primary:** A09, A15, A16
- **Boundary:** A07

### Shared characteristics

No primary fieldwork. Screening funnels are reported in detail (A09: 1,233 → 36 → 13;
A16: 1,672 → 1,322 → 115 → 18; A15: 230 → 133 → 131). Each produces a portable structure:
an eight-domain viability framework with 37 standards and 19 inhibitors (A09), a six-pattern
typology (A15), a four-step integrated development framework (A16). Each acknowledges a
language or geographic restriction on its own base (A09 English-only; A16 English and Chinese;
A15 acknowledged geographic imbalance).

### Important internal differences

**This is the cluster's real payoff: three syntheses of overlapping literatures reach three
incompatible epistemic conclusions.**

- **A16 concludes toward standardization** — ten models collapsed into four steps, with
  scientific rigor and standardization treated as directly affecting effectiveness.
- **A15 concludes toward irreducible plurality** — six qualitatively distinct patterns,
  explicitly "different processes used to achieve institutionalization... rather than
  different stages through which the HEI must transition."
- **A09 concludes toward interpretive diagnosis** — that standards-checking is the wrong
  activity, and that the needed posture is that of an "interpreter," not a "reviewer."

They also differ in evidentiary thinness in ways that matter: A09 derives a 37/19/8 framework
from 13 articles of which only 2 discussed standards and inhibitors jointly (a scope-versus-
evidence tension its own analysis records); A16's 100% positive-efficacy result plausibly
reflects its selection filter; A15's base is acknowledged to carry success bias.

### Boundary or partial members

**A07** is a conceptual guideline rather than an aggregative synthesis. It synthesizes concepts
and draws on three illustrative institutional cases (Birmingham, Oslo, Tufts) that function as
feasibility illustrations, not as a sample. It sits between this cluster and Cluster 1 and is
best described as belonging fully to neither.

### Studies explicitly excluded despite surface similarity

- **A12** is empirical: it collected interviews and questionnaires from practitioners. Its
  Figure 1 model is a synthesis of *its own data*, not of a literature.
- **A11** and **A06** apply formal coding instruments but to primary institutional material.

### What this cluster helps explain

It explains why a reader consulting the corpus's synthesis studies receives no settled
guidance: the disagreement is not about findings but about **what kind of knowledge curriculum
development admits of**. This makes Cluster 5 the corpus's most direct evidence for
thematic-coding V-5 (standardization versus context-sensitivity), and it shows that
divergence is not attributable to differences in primary evidence, since all three work over
published literatures.

### Alternative plausible grouping

Merge A09 and A15 into a *diagnostic/typological* group and relocate A16 alongside A07 and
A05 as *procedural-roadmap* studies. This tracks D7 rather than D9 and would place A16 with
the studies it most resembles in intent. Its cost: it hides the fact that three syntheses of
similar literatures disagreed, which is this cluster's main contribution.

### Confidence

**STRONG** for membership and for the intra-cluster divergence finding.
**Caveat:** this is the one cluster that substantially coincides with a methodology label
(review/meta-study). It is retained because its explanatory payoff — the epistemic divergence
— is not a methodological fact. Acknowledged openly in Part II.

# Part II — Alternative classification test

An honest attempt to break the structure above.

### Could these studies be grouped more usefully along another dimension?

**Yes, for several purposes.** Three alternatives are genuinely competitive:

**Alternative A — by locus of difficulty (D2).** This produces:
*initiation* {A12} · *diagnosis* {A09, A07} · *diagnosis-to-action* {A02} · *design*
{A03, A16, A05} · *deliberation* {A04, A14, A01} · *implementation* {A08, A06} · *uptake
depth* {A11, A06} · *sustaining* {A15, A10}.
This grouping is **more useful than the five clusters for anyone asking what is known about a
particular stage**, and it makes the corpus's complementarity (V-9) immediately legible. Its
weakness is that it forces A13 and A01, which explicitly deny stage-segmentation, into a
framework they reject, and it leaves A03 unplaceable.

**Alternative B — by degree of prescription (D7).** This produces:
*algorithmic* {A03} · *procedural roadmap* {A07, A16, A05, A02} · *diagnostic instrument*
{A09, A06, A15} · *heuristic* {A01, A12, A13} · *deliberately non-prescriptive*
{A11, A14, A10} · *critical/descriptive* {A08, A04}.
This is the cleanest single-axis partition available and it directly organizes the corpus's
sharpest disagreement. It is arguably the best alternative to the five clusters.

**Alternative C — by criterion of success (D8).** Useful for assessing evidential quality but
weak as an intellectual grouping, since it separates studies that clearly belong together
(A04 and A14 land in different bins from A13 and A06 for reasons of evidence, not orientation).

### Are the clusters merely reproducing methodology or domain?

Tested individually.

- **Cluster 5 does substantially coincide with review methodology.** Acknowledged. It is
  retained because its payoff (three reviews, three epistemic conclusions) is not a
  methodological fact.
- **Cluster 1 is not a methodology cluster**: it contains a quantitative optimization study
  (A03), a non-empirical guideline (A07) and a descriptive case report (A02).
- **Cluster 2 is not a methodology cluster**: grounded theory (A04), action research (A14,
  A10), sequential mixed methods (A12), descriptive mixed-methods case (A01).
- **Cluster 3 is not a methodology cluster**: critical discourse analysis (A06), action
  learning (A08), autoethnography (A13), mixed-methods intervention (A11).
- **Cluster 4 is not a methodology cluster**: descriptive case (A01, A05), autoethnography
  (A13), MCDM modelling (A03).

**Domain test.** Domains are dispersed across clusters: medical education appears in Clusters
1 (A02, A07) and 5 (A09, A16); sustainability appears in 1 (A03), 3/4 (A05), 2 (A14) and 5
(A15); business and accounting appear in 1 (A03) and 3/4 (A13). No cluster is a domain label.

A **real corpus-composition pattern** does exist and is recorded separately rather than built
into clusters: the four medical-education studies (A02, A07, A09, A16) all concern mapping,
standards or models, while the four sustainability studies (A03, A05, A14, A15) all concern
depth or breadth of integration. This is a property of how the corpus was assembled, and A09
breaks any simple reading of it by arguing against standardization from within medical
education.

### Does any cluster hide important conceptual differences?

Yes, and each is flagged in place:
- **Cluster 1** hides the representation / computation / diagnosis split (thematic-coding C-1).
- **Cluster 2** hides the facilitated / self-organized split, and hides that A12 has no
  curriculum case.
- **Cluster 3** hides the epistemic-infrastructural / labour-throughput split.
- **Cluster 4** hides the market-referenced / framework-referenced split — the most serious of
  the four, and the reason Cluster 4's confidence is MODERATE.
- **Cluster 5** hides nothing important; its internal difference is its main content.

### Was any cluster created mainly because several studies use similar words?

**Cluster 4 is the one at genuine risk.** "Employability," "market relevance" and "currency"
recur across A01, A05 and A13. The defence is dimensional (shared warrant source with a
predicted consequence for deliberative load), and that prediction is partially falsified by
A01 from inside the cluster. It is presented as provisional and an alternative dissolution is
offered.

Cluster 2 was checked for the same risk — "participation," "collaboration" and "community"
recur widely across the corpus, including in A15, A11 and A13, which are excluded. Membership
was decided by whether findings are about the group, not by vocabulary. A13's exclusion despite
using "collaborative action research" is the test case.

### Would a multidimensional classification be more faithful?

**Yes. Stated explicitly: a single mutually exclusive taxonomy is inadequate for this corpus.**
Seven of sixteen studies hold defensible membership in more than one cluster (A01, A02, A05,
A09, A10, A11, A13), and three (A03, A07, A12) are boundary-defining rather than
comfortably classifiable. The five clusters are useful as an intellectual map; the grid in
Part III is the more honest representation and should be preferred where precision matters.

# Part III — Recommended representation: multi-axis grid

Five axes, applied to all sixteen studies. Cells state the value, not a score. Where a study
holds two values on an axis, both are given — that is information, not indecision.

| Study | D1 conception of curriculum | D2 located difficulty | D5 authority configuration | D7 prescription | D8 success criterion | D9 comparative reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A01 | process + combinatorial | deliberation (denies stages) | peer plenary, self-facilitated | heuristic ("replicable") | relational + structural | none |
| A02 | relational info structure | diagnosis → action | small technical committee | procedural (ToC roadmap) | documentary | pre/post counts |
| A03 | combinatorial | computational planning | expert panel + algorithm | algorithmic | internal model coherence | none |
| A04 | process | deliberation | peer collective, authority-chaired | descriptive + role advice | relational | none |
| A05 | combinatorial + integrating lens | design ("too hard to build") | formulation group + state approval | procedural (10 steps) | proxy metric + structural | none |
| A06 | text / discourse / contract | uptake depth, enactment | central + devolved units | diagnostic instrument | none accepted | cross-unit (15 depts) |
| A07 | relational info structure | diagnosis / communication | generic | procedural (9 steps) | feasibility | none (3 illustrations) |

# Part IV — Outliers, singletons and studies that resist classification

Treated as boundary-revealing rather than as classification failures.

**A03 — the corpus's defining outlier.** It is the only study treating curriculum planning as
a solvable decision problem with no organizational process attached: no governance, no
adoption, no implementation, no institutionalization (all NOT REPORTED; verified against
`analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`). It holds primary membership in Cluster 1 and secondary
in Cluster 4, but neither captures what makes it distinctive. **Its analytical value is that
it marks the corpus's most fundamental split** — between curriculum planning as an
optimization problem and curricular change as an organizational problem — and it is the only
study fully on the first side. Forcing it into barrier/actor/sustainability categories would
destroy that information.

**A07 — non-empirical singleton.** An AMEE Guide. NOT APPLICABLE is correct for its
institutional context and unit of analysis. It belongs to Cluster 1 by conception and to
Cluster 5 by second-order status, and fully to neither. Its distinctive contribution is that
it states the conditions for a mapping system's success (institutional support, workload
credit, dedicated leadership time, a simple interface) while acknowledging little direct
evidence that maps improve learning — a rare explicit gap between prescriptive confidence and
evidentiary base.

**A12 — the study with no curriculum.** Its object is professional practice at the moment of
initiation. It reveals a distinction the rest of the corpus does not make: between studying
curricular change and studying **the professional practice of assisting it**. A10 shares this
partially; A12 holds it in pure form. It also carries an unresolved internal divergence — what
interviewees named as a legitimate starting point (presenting research on change drivers) is
what questionnaire respondents least preferred.

**A06 — unique conception.** The only study conceiving curriculum as text and contract, and
the only one whose evidentiary base is exclusively documentary. It sits comfortably in Cluster
3 by finding, but its conception is shared with no one.

**A02 — low centrality.** A small, well-bounded case (one competency, one institution). It
holds weak membership in Clusters 1 and 4 and anchors no cluster. This is not a defect: its
specific contribution is the diagnosis-to-intervention transition, which is a point about
sequencing rather than about orientation.

**Studies with no ambiguity of placement:** A08 and A13 (Cluster 3), A04 and A14 (Cluster 2),
A15 (Cluster 5). These five are the corpus's most clearly typed studies.
