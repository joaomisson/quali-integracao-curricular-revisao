# Critical Synthesis of the Literature Corpus — A01–A16

**Stage 2, Step 4.** Built from `analysis/corpus/evidence-matrix.md` (read in full),
`analysis/corpus/thematic-coding.md` (D1–D9, adjudications, conceptual distinctions,
cross-study relationships, gaps) and `analysis/corpus/clusters.md` (C1–C5, alternative
classifications, multi-axis grid, outliers).

## What this document is and is not

This is the corpus's **argument-level synthesis**. Its unit of analysis is never a study.
It is a pattern, a relationship, a configuration, a mechanism, a distinction, a tension, or
an argument. Individual studies appear only as evidence, as counterevidence, or as boundary
markers.

The document does three things that Stage 2B did not:

1. it **tests** the Stage-2B dimensions and clusters against each other, and reports where
   they are coupled, where they are exceptions to each other, and where they overstate
   independence;
2. it **adjudicates** the Stage-2B tensions — retaining some, reducing some, and finding one
   partly artificial;
3. it derives **higher-level arguments** with explicit confidence, scope conditions, and an
   explicit statement of the stronger conclusion each argument does *not* license.

It does **not** evaluate the dissertation. No dissertation material, no `literature/` note,
no file under `analysis/dissertation/` or `analysis/critique/` was consulted at any point.

### Provenance

All content traces to the three corpus artifacts named above, which in turn trace to
`analysis/individual/Axx-analysis.md`, which in turn trace to literature notes
(*fichamentos*), **not to the complete papers**. Every claim below inherits that limitation.
No individual analysis was re-read during this step; the evidence matrix served as the
verification layer.

### Standing limitations carried into every conclusion

- **Ten of sixteen studies are single-case and non-comparative** (A01, A02, A03, A04, A05,
  A07, A08, A10, A13, A14). Any corpus claim about *variation between units facing the same
  reform* rests on three studies only (A06, A11 as primary; A15 as second-order).
- **Seven studies evaluate processes their own authors ran** (A01, A04, A05, A08, A10, A13,
  A14). Optimism in this corpus is therefore partly a property of its evidentiary
  architecture rather than of curricular change.
- **A08 is the corpus's principal negative case** and is treated throughout as carrying
  disproportionate weight on impediments precisely because it is atypical. It is never
  marginalized for being an outlier in valence.
- **Learner-outcome evidence is almost absent.** One study (A16) supplies any, at second
  hand, with a probable selection filter recorded in its own analysis.
- **NOT REPORTED is silence in a source, never evidence of absence in the world.**
  **NOT APPLICABLE is a statement about study type, never a negative finding.**
- **Convergence among studies sharing one methodological vulnerability compounds the
  vulnerability, not the confidence.** This is applied explicitly, not decoratively.
- **Several notes render quotations in Portuguese** for English-language articles. No
  argument below turns on fine-grained author wording.
- **How the sixteen studies were selected is not recorded in any corpus artifact.** Selection
  bias in corpus assembly therefore cannot be assessed from inside this analysis.

### Two distinctions used throughout

**Convergence of assertion vs. convergence of evidence.** Several apparent agreements in this
corpus are agreements about what to *claim*, inherited from shared theoretical sources
(constructive alignment, outcome-based education, policy enactment), rather than independent
corroboration by evidence. These are separated wherever they occur, because conflating them
is the single easiest way to overstate this corpus.

**Reported constraint vs. demonstrated determination.** Many strong-sounding institutional
claims in this corpus are counterfactual in form ("this could not have happened because of
X"). A reported constraint is directly evidenced; a determination requires an unobserved
comparison. The corpus supplies far more of the first than the second.

# 1. COLLECTIVE PROBLEM SPACE

## The corpus does not address one curricular problem

The sixteen studies use overlapping vocabulary — *review*, *redesign*, *mapping*, *reform*,
*development* — for problems that are not the same problem, do not have the same solution
form, and in several cases are not even about the same object. Reading them as a single
literature manufactures agreement.

At least **seven distinct problem framings** are documented, each anchored in an explicit
problem statement (matrix field 1):

| Problem framing | The thing that is wrong | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| **Content currency** | The curriculum no longer matches labour-market, disciplinary or societal demand | A01, A05, A13, A03 |
| **Invisibility** | Nobody knows what is taught where, or how assessment connects to the programme | A07, A02, A11 (diagnostic layer) |
| **Diagnosis without action** | Existing methods locate gaps but do not say what to do about them | A02 (explicit), A09 (audits identify non-compliance, not causes) |
| **Method confusion** | Practitioners cannot choose among available models or know how to begin | A16, A12, A03 ("manually and intuitively"), A05 ("too hard to build from scratch") |
| **Under-theorized process** | The literature describes outcomes and models but not how groups actually decide | A04, A14 (explicit "blind spot"), A01 (linear models misrepresent practice), A12 |
| **Enactment failure** | Design is easy; making it happen is not | A08 (explicit premise), A06 (policy ≠ enactment), A14 (block course) |
| **Shallow or non-durable uptake** | Change is adopted at the surface, or does not survive | A11, A06, A15, A10, A05 (critique of "cosmetic" relabeling) |

### What this shows

**First, remedies in this corpus are mostly not rival answers.** A12's "listen much longer
before starting," A03's optimization model, A08's demand to revise workload models, and
A15's typology of institutionalization patterns are answers to four different questions.
D2 (locus of difficulty) captures this precisely: the corpus distributes its located
difficulties across an entire trajectory, and each study's remedy is tightly coupled to
where it located the difficulty. This is the corpus's principal structural feature and its
principal fragility (Section 14).

**Second, the problem framings are not neutral about what would count as a solution.** A
study framing the problem as invisibility can be satisfied by a representation (A07's map);
a study framing it as enactment failure cannot (A08). A02 sits exactly on this seam: its
contribution is defined as the transition from diagnosis to intervention, which presupposes
that representation-framed studies stop too early. A09 makes the parallel criticism of
standards-checking: it establishes *whether* standards are met, not *why* they are not. The
corpus therefore contains **internal criticism between problem framings**, not merely
variety.

**Third, three studies are not addressing an organizational curricular problem at all**, and
this must not be smoothed away:

- **A03** addresses a computational planning problem. There is no governance, no adoption,
  no implementation and no institutionalization in its account (all NOT REPORTED; verified in
  Stage 2B against `analysis/individual/A03-analysis.md`). Its "barriers" field records
  methodological limitations only, which is correct for its type and must not be read as a
  finding that its setting had no barriers.
- **A07** addresses a communication problem and is a guideline, not a study of a case.
- **A12** addresses a *professional practice* problem: how educational developers begin. It
  has no curriculum case. It reveals a distinction the rest of the corpus does not make —
  between studying curricular change and studying the practice of assisting it.

These three define the boundaries of the corpus rather than sitting inside it, and they are
treated that way throughout.

### Connecting problem framing to D1 and D2

The framings above are not free-floating: they follow from what each study takes a
curriculum to be (D1). A combinatorial conception generates a selection-and-balance problem;
a relational conception generates a communication problem; a discursive conception generates
an interpretation problem; a process conception generates a deliberation problem; an
organismic conception generates a diagnostic problem; a formation-domain conception
generates a representability problem. **This is not a tautology, because conception does not
fix normative stance**: A03 and A11 hold the same combinatorial conception and reach opposite
conclusions about whether structure should be optimized (D1's most informative internal
variation; Section 13).

# 2. CONCEPTIONS OF CURRICULUM

## Six conceptions, and what each one makes thinkable

D1 established that at least six qualitatively distinct conceptions are present, each
anchored to a named theoretical source in the matrix rather than inferred. The synthesis
question is different: **what does each conception do to the rest of a study?** The table
below is derived by cross-reading fields 1, 2, 6, 10, 12, 16 and 19 of each entry.

| Conception | What counts as a problem | What counts as evidence | Which actors matter | Plausible intervention | What counts as success |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Combinatorial / structural object** (A03; A05; A11; A16 partly) | Imbalance, drift, wrong course set, incoherent sequence | Credits, prerequisite graphs, complexity scores, course lists | Planners, expert panels, departments as owners of course lists; **the enacting teacher is not required by the account** | Select, sequence, prune, rebalance | Structural properties: balance, credit counts, courses added or removed |
| **2. Relational information structure** (A07; A02; A10 partly) | Invisibility; unknown links between teaching, assessment and outcomes | Maps, coverage counts, link structures | Everyone, as *users* of a representation | Build and maintain a representation | The map exists, is used, and reveals gaps |
| **3. Text / discourse / contract** (A06 alone) | Policy language absorbed without practice change | Graded textual indicators across document types | Departments as interpretive communities; external regulators; "pedagogical translators" | Translation and fusion into disciplinary epistemology | **A06 accepts no success criterion at all** |
| **4. Contextualized social process** (A14; A04; A01; A13; A15; A12 thematizes it) | The process is under-theorized; linear models misrepresent it | Interviews, journals, observation, coded case narratives | The team, the facilitator, champions, middle leaders | Facilitate, build shared vision, listen, coordinate | Relational and processual outcomes |
| **5. Living system with health states** (A09 alone) | Deterioration invisible beneath formal compliance | Standards attained; inhibitors present | Generic role categories only | Diagnostic interpretation rather than audit | Coherence and feasibility of the framework |
| **6. Domains of personal formation** (A08 alone) | A legitimate curricular domain cannot be registered at all | Student work samples, retention, observed workload reality | The team, casual staff, the external regulator through its templates | Institutional "buffers" against documentation regimes | Contested by the study itself |

### Four consequences that matter for the whole corpus

**(a) Conception fixes what can count as failure.** Under conception 1, failure is structural
imbalance; under 2, invisibility; under 3, non-fusion; under 4, breakdown of the group; under
5, undiagnosed deterioration; under 6, exclusion of a domain from the record. Two studies can
therefore both report success while neither has examined anything the other would count. This
is the root of the comparability problem analysed in Section 9.

**(b) The ontological cleavage that organizes the corpus is hybridized in practice — in the
only studies that looked.** A12, the sole study that asked practitioners directly, found that
educational developers *practically adopt* the product model (outcomes, constructive
alignment) while *drawing on* process-model strategies (listening, contextualization, respect
for staff philosophy) — a dual orientation, not an allegiance. A11 performs the institutional
analogue: a formal blocking/delay-factor instrument deployed inside a consultative process
and then explicitly subordinated to it. A14 does the same with neutral data supplied so that
local actors decide. **EXPLICIT** for A12 and A11 (both in field 17); **DERIVED** for the
corpus-level reading that the technical/social cleavage is analytically real but practically
hybrid, with the reasoning being that the three studies positioned to observe hybridization
all report it, while the pure poles (A03 technical; A04 social) are studies that did not
examine the other side. Confidence: **MODERATE** — A12's base is 6 interviews plus 22 of 74
questionnaires, geographically concentrated, and carries an unresolved internal divergence
between its two phases.

**(c) A08 supplies the corpus's only claim that a conception can be *institutionally
enforced***. The "becoming/being" domain could not enter TEQSA-derived documentation because
it could not be reduced to observable behaviours. This is qualitatively different from all
other conception statements in the corpus: every other study *chooses* its conception, while
A08 reports a conception being imposed by the format of institutional systems. If correct, it
means the represented/enacted gap (Section 4) is not only a measurement problem but is
partly manufactured by the recording apparatus.

**(d) Two studies do not hold a conception at all, and this is informative rather than
deficient.** A12 thematizes the product/process contrast as its object. A16 operates at
meta-level, classifying models as competency-centred or objective-centred, and its own
analysis records that whether its four-step framework resolves or merely sets aside that
distinction is NOT REPORTED. A16's conception is therefore *indeterminate*, not merely
unstated — a distinction worth preserving because it bears on how much weight its
standardization argument can carry (Section 13).

### Critical note on D1

D1 is well supported for the *existence* of distinct conceptions (each anchored to a named
source). It is weaker as an explanatory dimension than its position in the framework
suggests, for two documented reasons: **A11 holds conceptions 1 and 4 simultaneously and
treats the tension as its finding**, and **A03 and A11 share conception 1 while reaching
opposite normative conclusions**. Conception constrains the observational choice; it does not
determine stance. D1 should therefore be used to explain *what a study can see*, not *what a
study will recommend*.

# 3. CHANGE TRAJECTORY

## The trajectory is an analyst's partition, not a description of practice

Four studies explicitly deny that curricular change is lived as stages: **A01** (against
linear models), **A13** (real processes are "complex, dynamic, overlapping, and non-linear,
in contrast to the simplified cyclical schemas formally presented in the literature"),
**A04** (the process is characterized through five thematic findings, not phases), and
**A15**, which supports non-linearity with the corpus's only aggregate evidence: its six
patterns are "different processes used to achieve institutionalization... rather than
different stages through which the HEI must transition," and HEIs move between them.

The partition below is therefore used **only to locate where the corpus's attention and
evidence actually sit**. It is not a model of how change happens, and Stage 2B's adjudication
of the diagnosis/decision/design/implementation/evaluation sequence — *analytically useful
for the analyst, misleading if presented as practice* — is carried forward without weakening.

| Position | Studies giving it substantial attention | Evidentiary character |
|---|---|---|
| Problem recognition | A09 (audits do not reveal causes), A07 (nobody knows what is taught where) | Argued, not observed |
| Diagnosis | A07, A09, A02, A13 (Phase 1), A14 (diagnostic phase), A06, A11 | Strong; the corpus's best-covered position |
| Representation / mapping | A07, A02, A10, A11, A09 | Strong, but see Section 4 |
| **Criterion formation** | A04 (collective vision precedes syllabus work), A03 (BWM competency weighting), A14 (Briefing Note), A12 (choice of entry pathway) | **Thin, and split incommensurably** |
| Decision-making as a distinct stage | A03, A12 isolate it; A04 and A14 deny it is a stage at all | Weakly supported as a separable stage |
| Design | A03, A16, A05, A02, A13 (Phase 2), A14 | Strong |
| Coordination | A05 (silos → a new Center), A06 (centre vs. departments), A11 (provost + departments), A15 (coordination as one of five key influences), A07 | Moderate; mostly described |
| Implementation | **A08 (central object)**, A06 (partial, documentary), A14 (pilot), A16 (Step 4; 13 tested implementations) | **Thin: one primary study makes it the object** |
| Evaluation | A09 (reframes it), A16, A11 (pre/post), A06 (rubric), A05, A10 | Moderate |
| Institutionalization | **A15 (only study whose central construct it is)**, A05 (markers over ~2 years), A13 (one durable procedural change) | Very thin |
| Sustainability | A15, A10 (collapse expected), A08 (funding withdrawal ends paid participation), A14, A07 (step 9) | Very thin; two of the four instances are *failures* |

### Which transitions are examined, and which are not

**Well attended:** *problem → diagnosis* and *diagnosis → representation*. These are the
positions where the corpus's portable outputs are concentrated (A07's nine steps, A09's
37/19/8 framework, A02's mapping-plus-ToC procedure, A11's analytics).

**Weakly attended, and consequentially so:**

- **Criterion formation → decision.** The corpus's two most distant studies both locate the
  pivotal moment here and answer it in incommensurable ways: **A03** treats criterion
  formation as expert weighting (five experts, pairwise comparisons, eight UNESCO
  competencies), while **A04** treats it as collective philosophical construction that must
  *precede* any discussion of syllabi. No study in the corpus compares these two ways of
  forming criteria, or reports a case in which they were combined. **A14's Briefing Note is
  the nearest thing to a bridge** — it documents which macro-level decisions need resolution,
  what alternatives partner universities adopted, and each option's impact against HESD
  criteria, precisely so that local actors decide. That is a procedural device for making
  criteria explicit without settling them.
- **Decision → design.** How a decision becomes a written specification is almost unexamined.
  A13's plain-language "Reference Guide," created so that subject specialists could define
  learning outcomes and assessments without technical jargon, is the corpus's clearest
  instrument at this transition — and it appears as an incidental practical device in an
  autoethnography, not as an object of study.
- **Design → implementation.** A08 is the only primary study whose premise is this
  transition. A14 observes it partially, and finds a failure. A06 measures only its
  documentary trace.
- **Implementation → institutionalization.** Only A15 (second-order, success-biased) and A05
  (markers over a short window, no outcome data).
- **Institutionalization → educational effect.** Nobody.
- **Evaluation → next cycle.** Asserted by A02 (CQI), A09 (prophylaxis) and A16 (ongoing
  development); observed across a full cycle by nobody.

### Interpretation

**The corpus is front-loaded, and its portability is front-loaded with it.** Its best-equipped
positions are those that precede enactment; its least-equipped are those in which curricular
change would have to produce educational effects. This is not a criticism of any individual
study — most are explicit that outcome evaluation is future work (A01, A02, A05, A11, A13) —
but it is a structural property of the corpus that no amount of internal reading can repair.

**A second asymmetry:** the two positions where the corpus's evidence is thinnest
(institutionalization, sustainability) are also the two where the available evidence is
predominantly *negative*. A10's participants expected not to continue; A08's practice ended
with the funding; A14's artifact partially failed when handed to a non-participant. The
corpus knows least about the phase in which its few observations are least encouraging. This
should not be read as evidence that curricular change does not endure — it is a coverage
fact, not an outcome fact.

# 4. REPRESENTATION AND ACTION

## The corpus endorses a distinction it rarely measures across

Six studies name a version of the represented/enacted distinction explicitly — A02
(intended/delivered/experienced), A06 (planned vs. enacted), A07 (declared/taught/tested,
after English 1978), A08 (curriculum-as-designed vs. curriculum-as-enacted, after Barnett and
Coate), A09 (planned/delivered/learned), A11 (catalog requirements vs. actual advising
practice). **This is the most widely shared conceptual commitment in the corpus.**

Its measurement is far narrower. D3's four positions, restated as a countable claim:

- **Theorizes the gap, measures only the represented side:** A02, A06.
- **Gathers some enactment-side evidence:** A08 (student work samples, retention, observed
  workload reality), A14 (non-participant observation in the formative pilot), A11
  (incidental — catalog requirements found "routinely waived by advisors and faculty in
  actual practice").
- **Invokes it without measuring either side:** A07, A09.
- **Does not engage it:** A01, A03, A04, A05, A10, A12, A13, A15, A16.

**Confidence: STRONG** for both halves — that the distinction is near-universally endorsed,
and that the corpus rarely measures across it. Both are directly countable from matrix fields
4, 5 and 16 rather than inferred.

## Three functions of representation, and why they are not stages

Stage 2B's C-1 identified three non-equivalent functions of a curricular mapping artifact.
The synthesis adds that these are **not a maturity sequence** — they are different purposes
with different success conditions, and the studies criticize each other across them:

| Function | Instance | Success condition | Its critics inside the corpus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Representation / communication** | A07 (ten windows, nine steps; "the essence of a curriculum map is not the nodes but the links") | Teachers and students can see what is taught where | A07 itself concedes little direct empirical evidence that maps improve learning; A02 argues mapping stops at diagnosis; A09 argues mapping shows *where*, not *why* |
| **Diagnosis** | A02 (7 of 781 objectives in Phase 2; 1 of 642 in Phase 3), A11 (publication errors; waived requirements), A09 (positions its viability framework as the layer mapping cannot supply) | A defensible gap is located | A09: locating gaps does not identify causes |
| **Social mediation** | A10 (the fifteen-page report and alignment map as a "point-at-able object," interpreted through Wenger's boundary object), A14 (Briefing Note anchoring macro decisions) | The artifact anchors collective attention and enables a decision | A10's own finding that participants expect not to continue once the mediators leave |

**The mediation function is the corpus's most under-recognized finding about
representation.** A10 reports that the report's value lay in its physical existence as
something to point at, "beyond the statistical data itself." A13 supplies the complementary
case: its plain-language Reference Guide performed an intermediary function *as an artifact*,
substituting for an intermediary person. **DERIVED**, with reasoning: A10 states the
boundary-object function explicitly for a diagnostic report, and A13 describes a document
created specifically to let non-specialists perform a technical task; juxtaposing them yields
the proposition that intermediary functions can be discharged by artifacts, which neither
study asserts. Confidence: **MODERATE** (two studies, both insider, neither designed to test
it).

## How far does the corpus support movement from representation to change?

**Representation → diagnosis: well supported.** A02, A11 and A09 each demonstrate that
representation surfaces things actors did not know: an objective-count scarcity, published
prerequisite errors, and — in A11's case — the corpus's only incidental empirical
demonstration that formal documentation and actual practice diverge.

**Diagnosis → actionable problem: supported by one study that designed for it.** A02's stated
contribution *is* this transition, and it delivered restructured objectives in the Internal
Medicine clerkship and Phase 3, plus modified performance assessments. This is real, and it
is narrow: one competency, one institution, and the reported outcome is documentary.

**Actionable problem → decision: thin.** A14's Briefing Note and A11's customized failure-rate
and flow reports are the two clearest instruments. Neither study measures whether the
instrument changed a decision that would otherwise have gone differently.

**Decision → actual curricular change: weakly supported, and this is the corpus's decisive
limitation on the representation question.** The strongest available counterweight is
internal to the corpus: **A11 provided a framework, customized data, administrative
assistance through governance, faculty inclusion on the project team and $1,500 per
department — the most heavily instrumented and supported intervention in the corpus — and
obtained 100% completion of low-cost structural changes, zero attempts at signature work or
learning-outcome transparency, and no average change in its own structural complexity metric
(four programmes down, four up, one unchanged).** Representation plus support plus money
produced completion of the cheap changes and no attempt at the expensive ones.

**Overall verdict.** The corpus supports the chain **representation → diagnosis** strongly,
**diagnosis → intervention design** moderately (essentially one study), and
**intervention → curricular change of any depth** weakly and conditionally. It supports
**change → educational effect** almost not at all (Section 9).

## A caution the corpus generates about itself

A06 provides the sharpest self-limiting observation available: its "Application" score is a
measure of *declared* application and is therefore subject to the same mimicry risk it
identifies for "Language." A study measuring the gap from inside the documentary layer cannot
fully escape the layer. A02 illustrates the same problem in the opposite direction: it treats
objective-count scarcity as confirming a real gap while treating student and faculty
satisfaction data as showing the *experienced* curriculum was adequate — and its own analysis
records an unaddressed alternative reading, that the evaluation instruments were simply not
designed to probe that competency. **A02 therefore both demonstrates and undermines the
inference from documents to practice, within a single study.** This is the most instructive
single case in the corpus on representation, and it is instructive because of its
limitations, not despite them.

# 5. JUSTIFICATION AND DECISION LOGICS

## Warrants are combined, not chosen

D4 documented eight recurrent warrants. The synthesis question is not which warrant each
study uses — most use three or four — but **what settles an argument when warrants
compete**, and whether the corpus ever resolves that.

### The warrants, and what each is actually doing

| Warrant | Instances | Function in the argument |
|---|---|---|
| Labour-market / employer demand | A01, A05, A13, A03, A11 (broad competencies) | Supplies the *content* answer from outside the unit |
| Peer / competitor benchmarking | A01 (peer, competitive, aspirant institutions), A05 (regional mapping), A14 (8 partner programmes) | **External legitimation device that settles internal argument** — A01's logistics course was added "as a direct result of benchmarking" |
| Externally authored competency frameworks | A03 (UNESCO's eight), A02 (UVA's twelve), A01 (ITAA), A09 (WFME/LCME/GMC), A13 (accreditation guidance) | Supplies criteria that need not be locally justified |
| Externally *imposed* regulatory format | A08 (TEQSA-derived templates), A06 (PSRBs) | **Not a warrant the study chose** — it constrains what can be said at all |
| Disciplinary epistemology | A06 (outcomes formed "largely around disciplines' own content"), A08 (threshold concepts), A14 (instructors' prior practice) | Defends local authority over content |
| Internally negotiated philosophical vision | A04, A12 (philosophy as an entry pathway), A14 | Constructs the warrant rather than importing it |
| Formal alignment logic | A02 (Bloom), A08 (SOLO), A12 and A13 (constructive alignment), A16 (model steps) | Makes decisions *checkable* without making them *justified* |
| Quantitative structural property | A11 (complexity score), A03 (credit/difficulty balance) | Converts a judgement into a computation |
| Student / alumni voice | A01, A05, A10, A13, A14 | Almost always consultative; see Gap G3 |

### Four distinct relations between competing warrants

This is a synthesis-level result: the corpus does not have one answer to warrant conflict, it
has **four structurally different ones**, and they are not on a scale.

1. **Hybridization.** A12 (practitioners combine product and process logics), A11
   (technical-rational origin plus deliberate departmental empowerment), A14 (external
   expertise supplied as neutral data so local actors decide). Note the honesty of A11's own
   source, which **declines to resolve** whether its hybrid is genuine reconciliation or a
   buy-in strategy for an administratively driven initiative.
2. **Claimed but unrealized synthesis.** A03's He-Xie framing explicitly claims to unite
   human coordination (the He Principle) with mathematical optimization (the Xie Principle);
   its own analysis records that "stakeholder engagement" reduces operationally to five
   experts supplying pairwise comparisons and 1–10 ratings, with no deliberation, negotiation
   or conflict-resolution process described. **The corpus contains one asserted synthesis
   whose human half is thinly instantiated, and two achieved partial syntheses (A11, A14)
   that claim no unifying theory.**
3. **Power ordering.** A06 argues that external professional, statutory and regulatory bodies
   exert substantially more power over local curriculum definition than central institutional
   policy. Here warrants are not reconciled at all — they are *ranked by authority*. This is
   the corpus's only comparative claim between warrants, and its own analysis records that how
   the comparison was operationalized is not described. Confidence: **TENTATIVE**.
4. **Foreclosure.** A08's is the corpus's most radical claim: the institutional warrant
   (behavioural learning outcomes) does not compete with the disciplinary/professional warrant,
   it *excludes* a curricular domain from official existence. Foreclosure is not a weaker form
   of power ordering; it operates before the argument starts.

### Two findings about the corpus's own justificatory practice

**(a) No study in the corpus interrogates the provenance or validity of the external
framework it adopts.** UNESCO's eight competencies (A03), UVA's twelve (A02), ITAA (A01),
WFME/LCME/GMC (A09), national and professional accreditation guidance (A13, A10, A05) are
imported, not examined. This is an inference from silence, but the silence is *documented*:
A02's note-layer ledger explicitly records that how the twelve competencies were developed or
validated is not reported, and A03's tensions field records that the sustainability mapping
index's correspondence to actual pedagogical content is not evidenced. **Confidence:
MODERATE**, and it cannot be raised from the note layer alone, because a fichamento may omit
what an article contains.

**(b) No study reports a case in which competing warrants were adjudicated by evidence about
learning.** The only learner-outcome evidence in the corpus (A16, second-hand, 13 of 18
underlying studies) is not used to arbitrate between warrants; it is used to argue that
theory-based development works. **DERIVED**, reasoning: field 10 across all sixteen entries
lists criteria, and in no entry does an outcome measurement resolve a disagreement about
what should be taught. Confidence: **MODERATE**, note-layer bounded.

### Internally divided warrants are common and should not be treated as incoherence

**A05** uses demand data (99% survey support; vocational demand) while explicitly attacking
"cosmetic" market-driven relabeling, and insists on newly authored courses rather than
rearranged existing ones. **A16** argues for standardization and for model-context fit in the
same breath. **A08** critiques metric-driven quality evaluation while evidencing its own
success with a retention metric. In each case the division reflects a real situation — an
author using the evidence an institution supplies while rejecting the logic that makes that
evidence authoritative. Section 15 treats this as a tension worth keeping, not a fault.

### Boundary cases for D4

- **A07** supplies no warrant for content at all; it warrants *visibility* of content.
- **A09** inverts the dimension: its object is why warranted standards go unmet, not which
  warrant is right.
- **A12** treats warrant selection as the practitioner's *choice variable* (outcomes-first vs.
  philosophy-first vs. graduate-profile-first), which is a different move from every other
  study — it declines to say which warrant should win, and reports empirically what
  practitioners pick (43.47% at programme level and 40.90% at module level start from learning
  outcomes).
- **A01 breaks the tempting D4↔D5 relationship** (that externally warranted reforms need less
  internal deliberative machinery): it had a strong external warrant *and* required structured
  conduct norms, reflective sessions, peer review and explicit management of "difficult debates
  marked by resistance to change and departmental power disputes."

# 6. AUTHORITY, PARTICIPATION, AND INTERMEDIARIES

## Six things that "who decides" can mean

Stage 2B rejected top-down vs. bottom-up as a dimension and demoted it to one value inside
D5. The synthesis strengthens that rejection by showing that **six distinguishable functions
routinely sit with different actors in the same study**:

| Study | Initiation | Formal authority | Practical influence | Participation | Mediation | Implementation responsibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A13 | Programme Director (middle leader), under crisis | Academic Board (103 documents) | Director + 4-person steering committee | 17 faculty (review), 13 specialists (redesign) | **A plain-language guide, not a person** | Subject specialists |
| A08 | Project leader | **Split: team controls 7 of 11 required units** | TEQSA, via documentation templates | Teaching team, incl. paid casuals while funded | Instructional designer (partial) | Casual/sessional staff |
| A06 | Central strategy | Devolved departments; PSRBs above both | PSRBs argued dominant | Departments completing redesign forms | "Pedagogical translators" | Departments |
| A10 | Developers and faculty jointly | The three tenured faculty | Developers, through data and the report | The same three faculty | Developers + the report as boundary object | The same three faculty, who expect not to continue |
| A11 | Provost's office | Departments + university/state approval system | Project team, data, money | 9 programmes; 18 survey respondents | Instructional designers named as a *requirement* for deep change | Departments |
| A04 | Academic vice-president | The institution | The ten-person team | 5 faculty + 5 administrators | **The facilitator was an authority holder** | Faculty |
| A03 | The modellers | NOT REPORTED | Five experts' ratings; the algorithm | Five experts | The model itself | NOT REPORTED |

Reading across the rows shows why the binary fails: **A13 is a middle leader who is neither
top nor bottom; A11 is a self-described hybrid its own source declines to resolve; A06 finds
neither internal pole dominant; A14 interposes an external actor who deliberately holds no
authority; A08's team lacks authority over part of its own object.** The one place the
top-down/bottom-up vocabulary works is exactly where A15 uses it: as a descriptor of
*initiation impetus* within an empirically derived six-pattern typology.

## What intermediary functions actually do

Seven instances of an interposed, method-supplying, non-deciding actor recur: A10 and A12
(educational developers), A14 (scientific support team as "coach"), A06 ("pedagogical
translators"), A11 (instructional designers named as a requirement), A08 (instructional
designer, partial), A13 (a Reference Guide substituting for an absent intermediary).
Disaggregating them yields **five functions**, each traceable:

1. **Absorb labour.** A10's two developers spent ~100 collective hours on a three-faculty
   programme; A11 supplied administrative assistance to speed proposals through governance
   plus customized failure-rate and flow reports; A14's support team ran the diagnostic
   content analysis; A13's guide reduced others' drafting labour.
2. **Supply method and technical language.** A12 (the developer's core offering), A10
   (adapted belonging scale, alignment mapping plan), A11 (Curricular Coherence Framework),
   A13 (jargon-free outcome and assessment writing).
3. **Produce a shared object that anchors collective attention.** A10's report ("point-at-able
   object"), A14's Briefing Note, A11's flow reports, A02's competency map.
4. **Translate between institutional and disciplinary registers.** A06's translators, in the
   institution→discipline direction; A13's guide, in the pedagogy→discipline direction.
5. **Render the decision space explicit without deciding it.** A14's Briefing Note documents
   which macro decisions need resolution, what partner universities did, and each option's
   impact against HESD criteria. A11's guide is explicitly "non-prescriptive."

### What the corpus's own evidence says intermediaries do *not* do

- **They do not guarantee continuation.** A10's participants stated a low likelihood of
  pursuing improvements without continued facilitation. A14's integrative block course
  partially failed when delegated to an instructor who had not participated. A08's paid
  evaluation meetings ended with the funding.
- **They do not confer authority.** A08's team could not modify the 4 of 11 units controlled
  elsewhere; attempts "failed or met resistance."
- **They do not, by themselves, change depth of adoption.** A11 is the decisive case
  (Section 4).

### Neutrality: argued, contested, and tested in the wrong conditions

A10 argues facilitator political neutrality is vital because curricular processes generally
arrive top-down and erode trust. **Two things weaken this as a general claim.** First, its own
analysis records that the study environment proceeded "without institutional interference or
pressure for compulsory reform" — an unusually stable setting the authors themselves credit
with facilitating success. **The neutrality claim was therefore tested precisely where
neutrality was least likely to be contested.** Second, **A04 is a direct counterexample**: its
facilitator was the academic vice-president — an authority holder — and although his
leadership was contested ("questions about leadership of the process being held by an
administrator"), a four-year comprehensive reform is reported as completed. **A01** is a
second counterexample: self-facilitated by the department.

**Synthesis:** the corpus supports intermediary *functions* considerably better than it
supports intermediary *attributes*. Confidence: **MODERATE** for the functional account
(seven instances, heterogeneous settings); **TENTATIVE** against the necessity of external
neutrality (two counterexamples, and the supporting evidence is three participants in a
non-political setting).

## Participation: what the corpus does and does not establish

- **Participation is nearly universal in the corpus and therefore explains little by itself.**
  Cluster 2's membership test was deliberately not "did people participate" but "are the
  findings about the group."
- **The defensible version is narrower and mechanism-specific:** the individuals who will
  *enact* a change should have participated in producing it. A14 supplies the only direct
  observation (the block course); A06 supplies the documentary analogue (only departments that
  fused the reform into their own epistemology enacted it); A15 links participatory
  vision-building to durability at aggregate level; A04 makes shared vision a precondition of
  technical work. Confidence: **MODERATE**, and Section 12 adds a scope condition that the
  corpus makes visible.
- **Participation has an unpriced cost.** A04's four years proceeded with no financial
  resources or teaching-load reduction; A01 reports faculty fatigue with an overwhelming
  volume of data; A13 involved 17 then 13 faculty *on top of* the director's ~200 hours; A10's
  three faculty required ~100 developer-hours. **No study in the corpus costs participation
  against its benefit.** This is developed as a tension in Section 15.
- **Students are almost never decision participants.** Across sixteen studies they appear as
  survey respondents (A01, A05, A10, A14), institutional data (A11, A13), a source of
  resistance (A09), and a mapping window (A07). The single exception is A15's bottom-up
  cluster (N=26), where initiation by "students and faculty through small projects" is
  recorded — second-order, aggregate evidence.

# 7. IMPEDIMENTS AND ENABLING CONDITIONS

## Four kinds of impediment, and why the kind determines the remedy

A generic "barriers" list would make this corpus look convergent where it is divergent. D6's
four kinds have **different remedies, different evidentiary status, and different
implications for whether the studied intervention could have addressed them at all.**

### (i) Resource and labour accounting

*The required work exceeds what institutional systems allocate or recognize.*

A08: workload model allocates **39 hours** per major unit redevelopment; the team found
**200–250 hours** required, excluding subsequent adjustments. A13: **~200 hours** of
bureaucratic writing carried by one person, on top of regulated teaching duties, causing
temporary abandonment of her own research. A10: **~100 collective developer-hours** for a
three-faculty programme. A04: no financial resources or teaching-load reduction at project
outset. A07: workload credit named as a *necessary condition*. A11: deep change argued to
require "sustained external resources and instructional designers." A14: time and human
resources cited by instructors as limiting deeper implementation. A01: budget constraints and
faculty fatigue. A15: lack of resources characterizes the "limited institutional change"
cluster (N=8).

**Remedy implied:** change workload models, fund time, add staff, restructure rewards.
**No study in the corpus tests such a remedy.**

### (ii) Governance and procedural throughput

*Approval machinery consumes the change.*

A13: **103 documents** through the Academic Board against a historical average of **9**; the
approval apparatus described as "an active disincentive." A11: bureaucratic barriers in the
university/state approval system named as one of the two most-cited obstacles. A05:
state-level approval by the New Jersey Council of Presidents. A01.

**Remedy implied:** simplify approval. **A13 is the corpus's only documented instance of a
governance impediment actually being reduced** — the internal peer-review rite in the
business school was simplified, attributed to the author's advocacy. A11's administrative
assistance is a workaround, not a reform of the machinery.

### (iii) Epistemic-infrastructural

*The format of institutional systems excludes legitimate curricular content or misrepresents
it.*

A08: behavioural-only outcome templates make the "becoming/being" domain unregisterable.
A06: programme specifications as contractually binding documents; entrenched exam structures
at 70–90% of final grades. A09: documental compliance coexisting with severe hidden
dysfunction. A11: the complexity algorithm scores a pedagogically beneficial change (splitting
a unified 4-credit course into 3-credit theory plus 1-credit lab) as *worse*, raising STEM
average complexity from 316 to 322.

**This is the corpus's most distinctive impediment type and is irreducible to (i) or (iv).**
Its remedy is neither more effort nor better attitudes: **more hours would not have made
"becoming/being" registrable in A08's forms.** A08's own suggestion is institutional
"buffers"; A06's implication is fusion into disciplinary epistemology; A11's is metric
humility. Note also the two very different theoretical registers inside this one category —
A08's is critical and epistemological (positivist institutional epistemology vs.
social-constructivist pedagogy), A11's is technical (a metric with poor construct validity).
Preserving both is better than merging them.

### (iv) Dispositional and cultural

*Attitudes, identity, territory.*

A01: senior faculty comfort with prepared courses; junior faculty tenure-clock constraints;
departmental power disputes. A04: faculty autonomy and disciplinary "territory wars";
contested administrator leadership; role ambiguity for half the team. A15: faculty resistance
under top-down mandate; ownership deficits. A09: student resistance as a cross-sectoral
inhibitor; leaders as monopolistic "gatekeepers." A13: systemic resistance; "behind closed
doors" decision-making. A05: academic silos.

**Remedy implied:** deliberation norms (A01's structured conduct rules), shared vision (A04),
community formation (A04, A10), or an external shock (A13's interpretation).

### (v) Delivery conditions — a fifth kind, recorded but not generalized

A16 reports inadequate scheduling, insufficient offline practical activities (especially in
online/hybrid modes), and inappropriate textbook selection, drawn from student feedback across
its underlying studies. One study; recorded, not built into a framework.

## Why the kinds must not be merged

**A remedy matched to the wrong impediment kind produces motion without change.** A labour
remedy applied to an epistemic-infrastructural impediment produces more work registered in the
same excluding format (A08). A deliberative remedy applied to a throughput impediment produces
better relations and the same queue (contrast A01/A04's relational outcomes with A13's 103
documents). This is the practical payoff of D6 and it is derivable only by comparison; no
single study states it.

## Resistance is not automatically unwillingness

Three studies recast dispositional appearances as structural conditions:

- **A11 is pivotal because it measured attitude and behaviour separately and found them
  decoupled:** perceived usefulness 4.23 ± 0.75, confidence 3.5 ± 0.55, concern **1.27 ±
  0.46** — and yet **no department attempted signature work or learning-outcome
  transparency**. Its own analysis records this as a documented discrepancy the source does
  not resolve, and its survey is 18 respondents with an acknowledged self-selection caveat.
- **A10** locates the impediment in absent time and expertise, not opposition: faculty
  welcomed the process and still expected not to continue it.
- **A13** names workload allocation systems that reward pure research over pedagogical
  innovation.

**A09 is the corpus's cleanest case of the barriers/absent-enablers distinction being
collapsed by design:** it has no "facilitators" construct at all; its positive pole is
standard attainment.

**Counter-position preserved.** A01 and A04 document impediments that are genuinely
dispositional and first-hand — comfort, tenure-clock pressure, territoriality, contested
leadership — and are not reducible to missing enablers. **No study tests whether removing
structural impediments would eliminate them.** Section 13 adjudicates this divergence rather
than dissolving it.

## Enabling conditions the corpus actually documents

Distinct from "the opposite of barriers": dedicated organizational homes (A05's Center for
Sustainability Studies, founded specifically to enable cross-college faculty affiliation);
external project funding (A14, 2013–2016; A08 during the pilot); financial incentives (A11's
$1,500); structured conduct norms (A01); a shared vision constructed before technical work
(A04); informal collaboration among champions compensating for absent formal leadership
support (A15); crisis conditions credited with breaking habitual resistance (A13, the author's
own interpretation, with no independent evidence recorded).

# 8. PRESCRIPTION VS CONTEXTUAL ADAPTATION

## The corpus's disagreement is about determinacy, not about portability

D7 arranged the corpus along a prescription continuum. The synthesis finds that **a single
continuum conflates two independent properties**:

- **Determinacy** — does the output return an answer, or structure a judgement?
- **Claimed scope** — is the output offered for use elsewhere?

Separating them produces a result that reframes Stage 2B's V-5:

| | Low claimed scope | High claimed scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Determining** (returns the answer) | — | **A03** (BWM–FFR–MCGP returns 35 of 53 courses and a four-semester schedule) |
| **Sequencing** (prescribes an order, not the content) | — | A07 (9 steps), A16 (4 steps), A05 (10 steps), A02 (ToC as roadmap) |
| **Structuring** (organizes judgement, refuses to settle it) | A08, A04 (descriptive/critical) | A09 (37/19/8, anti-checklist), A06 (28-indicator rubric), A15 (typology + self-reflection scheme), A11 (non-prescriptive reflective guide), A14 (Briefing Note, Pedagogical Workshop), A12 (Figure 1), A01, A13 |

**The decisive observation: every study on the "contextualist" side of V-5 nonetheless offers
a portable instrument.** A12 offers Figure 1 while concluding there are "no universal
recipes"; A14 offers the Briefing Note and Pedagogical Workshop while refusing "dogmatic
imposition"; A15 offers a self-reflection scheme built on 131 cases while insisting the six
patterns are not stages; A11 offers a metric and a guide while arguing against optimizing the
metric; A01 offers a "replicable" framework while arguing against linear models.

**Therefore the live disagreement is not standardization vs. contextualization.** It is:
*should the portable instrument determine the decision, or structure the deliberation that
makes it?* On that question, **A03 stands alone in the determining position**, which is
precisely why it is the corpus's defining outlier, and why the split it marks — curriculum
planning as an optimization problem vs. curricular change as an organizational problem — is
the corpus's most fundamental.

## The transferability paradox is partly artificial

Stage 2B recorded a tension (V-14, C-3): artifacts are argued to be non-transferable, yet
transferable artifacts dominate the corpus's outputs. Closer synthesis shows the two sides
mostly refer to **different objects**:

- **What the non-transferability evidence targets is curricular artifacts and enacted
  practices**: A14's integrative block course (partially failed when handed to a
  non-participant); A10's changes (participants expect not to sustain them); A06's reform
  pillars (only departments that fused them into their own epistemology enacted them); A15's
  durability (linked to participatory vision-building); A04's technical work (must be preceded
  by shared vision).
- **What the transferability claims target is procedures**: A07's nine steps, A16's four
  steps, A05's ten steps, A12's Figure 1, A13's "replicable guidelines," A01's framework.

These are compatible. A procedure for generating a local process is not the artifact that
process produces. **The paradox as stated therefore substantially dissolves** — and converts
into something sharper and more damaging:

> **The corpus's portable procedures are offered without any comparative test of their
> portability.** Not one study compares two approaches to curricular change against each other
> (Gap G7, corroborated by A10, A03 and A12 each naming this absence as future work).

So the residue is not a contradiction; it is an **evidential vacancy**. Confidence:
**STRONG** for the vacancy (directly countable); **MODERATE** for the dissolution of the
paradox (the object distinction is derived by comparison, not asserted by any study).

**A03 is the one case where the paradox does not dissolve**, because its output is both a
procedure *and* an artifact (35 selected courses; a four-semester schedule), and its own
analysis records no evidence bearing on transferability to other institutions, disciplines or
regulatory contexts.

## Self-limiting clauses are characteristic — and almost never operationalized

Four studies hedge their own prescriptions internally: A12 (a model plus "no universal
recipes"), A11 (metrics plus a warning against optimizing them), A13 ("replicable guidelines"
plus acknowledged institution-specific vulnerabilities), A16 (standardization plus
model-context fit). These hedges are mostly *not* self-contradictions — a structuring
instrument coexists comfortably with a denial of recipes.

What is missing is the operationalization: **most prescriptive outputs in this corpus do not
state the conditions under which they would not apply.** A05's analysis records explicitly
that no discussion of how institutional, regulatory or resource differences might limit
transferability is reported. Two partial exceptions deserve credit:

- **A07 states preconditions for its own framework**: unrestricted institutional support
  including workload credit, clear academic leadership with dedicated time, and a simple
  user-facing interface. This is the corpus's clearest operationalized scope condition — and
  it appears in its least empirical study.
- **A16 supplies model-selection scope conditions** (Kern for general medical curricula, Fink
  for integrating theoretical and affective learning, DACUM for professional-competency
  analysis in technical courses) — but not for its own four-step framework, whose relation to
  its competency-centred/objective-centred typology its own analysis records as NOT REPORTED.

## Critical note on D7

D7's placements are well evidenced. Two cautions carry forward. **Prescription is not
readable off study type**: A05 is a descriptive single case producing a ten-step roadmap; A11
is an empirical intervention refusing prescription; A15 is a meta-study of 131 cases producing
a deliberately non-developmental typology. And **A09 is a hard boundary case**: a highly
structured framework (37 standards, 19 inhibitors, 8 domains) whose central argument is
against checklist application — structured form, anti-procedural intent. Any attempt to
collapse D7 into D1 or D9 fails on these four.

# 9. WHAT COUNTS AS SUCCESS

## Four levels of success, and no study spanning all of them

The corpus's success claims are not comparable, and the incomparability is systematic rather
than incidental. Mapping every study's *highest* evidenced level:

| Level | What is demonstrated | Studies claiming it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Design completed** | A curriculum or intervention was produced and approved | A03 (model output; validation internal), A02 (maps filled; assessments modified), A05 (72 credits, 11 new courses; unanimous state approval), A13 (11 programmes/51 subjects → 2/25; 103 documents approved), A16 (framework proposed, not field-validated), A07, A09 (frameworks; feasibility only) |
| **2. Implementation achieved** | The design was enacted, at least partly | A08 (7 of 11 units; retention >90% amid enrolment 102→134), A14 (kick-off and annual theme worked; block course partially failed), A11 (100% completion of the low-cost change categories), A06 (about one-fifth of departments applied the guidelines explicitly), A05 (programme launched, revised, enrolled) |
| **3. Institutionalization / durability** | The change persisted in institutional structures | A05 (Center founded January 2011; dedicated director; NJSSEP transfer partnerships; enrolment 13→25→31→projected 40), A13 (one durable procedural change: peer-review simplification), A15 (the "redesign" depth level, reached by clusters 1 and 2) |
| **4. Educational effectiveness** | Learning changed | **A16 only** — second-hand: 13 of 18 underlying studies report knowledge and skill score gains, with a probable selection filter recorded in its own analysis |

**No study in the corpus demonstrates the full chain.** A05 comes closest (levels 1→2→3) and
has no outcome data over a roughly two-year window. A02 achieves level 1 with a systemic
design and explicitly does not measure whether student prognostication competence improved.

## Six criteria that studies actually accept

Beyond the level mapping, D8's criterion taxonomy holds and is worth stating with its
evidentiary character attached:

- **Structural / documentary output** — the corpus's *modal* criterion (A02, A13, A03, A05,
  A11). Cheap to observe; silent about effect.
- **Institutional proxy metric** — A05 (enrolment), A08 (retention), A11 (complexity score).
  **A11 is the only study in the corpus to report a null result on its own headline metric**,
  and it did so having declared the metric in advance and measured pre/post.
- **Relational / cultural outcome** — A01 ("re-established more honest relationships,"
  described as an *unexpected* behavioural benefit), A04 (boundary dissolution, shared
  responsibility, community), A10 (community-of-practice formation), A14 (transparency;
  instructors cross-referencing).
- **Participant self-report / satisfaction** — A10, A11, A12 ("people return"), A14, A04.
  Often the sole evaluative instrument.
- **Depth of institutional embedding** — A15 alone (denial → bolt-on → build-in → redesign).
- **Feasibility / internal coherence only** — A03, A07, A09, A16. **NOT APPLICABLE is correct
  for these study types and is not a deficiency finding.**
- **No success criterion accepted at all** — **A06**, which evaluates documentary engagement
  at a two-year snapshot of a nine-year reform and explicitly declines outcome claims. This
  boundary case strengthens rather than weakens the dimension: it shows that declining to
  claim success is an available and defensible position that most of the corpus does not take.

## Are studies more comparable than they look? Yes — and dangerously so

Three examples make the risk concrete:

1. **A13 and A08 both report on Australian universities under resource pressure and both
   describe substantial curricular work.** A13's success is level 1 with one level-3 element;
   A08's success is level 2 with a self-contested proxy. Read side by side without the level
   distinction, A13 looks successful and A08 looks unsuccessful — when in fact they are
   claiming at different levels with different evidence.
2. **A02 and A11 both report structural change in US institutions.** A02 reports its maps
   filled and assessments modified (level 1); A11 reports its structural target *not* moving
   (level 2, null). A02's is the weaker evidentiary claim and reads as the stronger success.
3. **A04 and A14 share conception, located difficulty and success criterion**, and differ on
   authority configuration — which is exactly why A04 works as a counterexample to A10's
   neutrality claim. Surface similarity of "success" conceals the difference that matters.

## The relational-outcome finding, and why it is not upgraded

**A01, A04 and A10 — three countries, three disciplines, three methods, three decades —
independently report improved collegial relations as a primary outcome of curricular review,
not a side effect.** A01 explicitly calls it unexpected. A14 adds a documentary analogue
(instructors cross-referencing each other's content).

This is genuinely under-recognized, and it is deliberately held at **MODERATE**. The three
supporting studies share one methodological vulnerability: **in each, the people reporting
improved relationships are the people who underwent the process**, with no independent
instrument or external evaluator (A01's analysis records this; A04's insider-chair limitation;
A10's three-participant self-report). Three converging studies with a common vulnerability do
not compound to strong evidence — they compound the vulnerability.

One qualification cuts slightly the other way and should be preserved: **A01 did not seek the
finding**, which weakens an expectancy-confirmation explanation for that one case. It does not
weaken the self-report problem.

## Learner outcomes: absence of evidence, honestly declared

Only A16 supplies any, at second hand, with a 100% positive rate that its own analysis
attributes plausibly to the eligibility filter (studies had to name a theoretical model and
report process detail). Every primary study either omits outcome data or **explicitly defers
it** — A01, A02, A05, A11 and A13 all name it as future work. **This is absence of evidence,
declared by the studies themselves. It is not evidence that curricular change is
ineffective**, and it equally does not license the opposite conclusion.

# 10. EVIDENTIARY ARCHITECTURE

## Design does not rank studies; it determines which claims are available

D9 is the corpus's meta-dimension. Used as a quality ranking it would be misleading, because
the corpus's most valuable impediment findings come from its most compromised positions. Used
correctly, it explains **which claim-classes each design can support**.

| Architecture | Studies | Claims it makes available | Claims it forecloses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insider as change agent** | A04, A13, A08, A14, A10, A05, A01 | Hidden labour; motivations; failure mechanisms; process detail invisible from outside | Independent verification of success; ruling out self-serving framing |
| **Institutionally internal, external to the unit** | A11 | Heterogeneity of departmental response; pre/post on a declared metric | Enactment-level observation |
| **External documentary** | A06 | Cross-unit comparison at scale under one reform | Anything about practice; its own "application" measure is documentary |
| **Practitioner studying practitioners** | A12 | Variation in professional practice; what practitioners actually choose | Any link from practice to curricular outcome (its own stated limitation) |
| **Modeller, not implementer** | A03 | Internal consistency; sensitivity stability across ten weight scenarios | Adoption, governance, effect — all NOT REPORTED |
| **Cross-case aggregate** | A09 (13 articles), A15 (131 cases), A16 (18 articles) | Distributional patterns; typologies; configuration–outcome association | Inherits the primary literature's biases: A15 names success bias explicitly; A16's 100% positive result plausibly reflects its filter; A09 derives a 37/19/8 framework from 13 articles of which only 2 discussed standards and inhibitors jointly |
| **Conceptual** | A07 | Systematization; stated preconditions | Effectiveness — its own analysis concedes little direct evidence maps improve learning |

### Four architecture-level results

**(a) The corpus's impediment findings exist *because of* insider position, and its success
findings are weakened by the same fact.** A08's 39-vs-200–250-hour comparison and A13's ~200
hours are knowable only from inside. The same proximity that makes those figures available
makes favourable self-evaluation unfalsifiable. **Insider position raises the credibility of
impediment findings and lowers the credibility of success findings, for the same reason.**
Confidence: **MODERATE** — this is an interpretation of the pattern, and the hour figures
themselves carry measurement caveats (A08's analysis records that how the hours were measured
is not reported; A13's is author self-report; A10's is study-internal).

**(b) The insider→favourable-findings rule is false as stated, and the counterexamples matter
more than the rule.** **A08** is an insider action-learning study whose findings are
predominantly barriers and structural failure, and which partially undercuts its own single
positive result. **A14** is insider and reports a partial failure. **A11** is semi-internal and
reports a null result on its own headline metric. **A06** declines success claims altogether.
The position→valence covariation is a real tendency, offered as an interpretive caution, and
must never be stated as a rule.

**(c) Two designs in the corpus can observe heterogeneity of response to a single reform, and
both find it.** A06 (15 departments: roughly one-third Active, one-third Engaged, one-third
Passive) and A11 (9 programmes across 7 departments and 5 colleges: uniform completion of
cheap changes, uniform non-attempt of expensive ones). **Ten other studies could not have
found heterogeneity had it existed.** This is the corpus's most under-exploited design, and it
is the reason claims about "how institutions respond to reform" cannot be built from the
single-case majority.

**(d) One natural comparison the corpus had, and did not exploit.** A08's team directly
managed 7 of 11 required units and did not control the other 4. That is a within-course
contrast between units subject to the reform and units not subject to it. **A08's own
note-layer ledger records that no retention, satisfaction or performance data are reported for
the four uncontrolled units, precluding exactly that comparison.** This is the sharpest single
illustration of why the corpus's institutional claims remain claims about *reported
constraint* rather than *demonstrated determination* (Section 11). Note carefully: this is a
NOT REPORTED at the note layer, so it cannot be asserted that the original article omitted the
comparison — only that it is unavailable here.

### Is the corpus's optimism architectural?

Partly, and the qualification matters. The pressure toward favourable findings is real and
multiply sourced: seven insider self-evaluations; A15's explicit statement that its source
literature shows "a strong tendency to publish success stories, omitting structural failures";
A16's selection filter; and **no study in the corpus reports an abandoned reform** (Gap G6).

But the corpus is not uniformly favourable, and four studies show the architecture does not
fully determine the finding: **A08** (predominantly negative), **A11** (null on its declared
metric), **A14** (partial failure of a designed artifact), **A06** (declines to claim success),
with **A10** adding an explicit expectation of non-continuation from inside a study reporting
success. **Confidence: MODERATE** that the corpus's overall favourable tone is structurally
rather than empirically produced — the mechanism is well documented, but the presence of five
non-confirming cases shows it is a pressure, not a determination.

# 11. MAJOR CONVERGENCES

Each entry states the exact point of agreement, the reason the evidence is comparable, the
shared-vulnerability caveat, and a confidence rating. **Frequency is never treated as
strength**: two convergences below are supported by eight studies and rated MODERATE, while
one supported by three is rated STRONG on the strength of what those three could observe.

---

## CV-1. Curricular change requires labour that institutional systems do not allocate or recognize

**Claim.** The work curricular change demands systematically exceeds what workload models,
funding and reward structures allocate to it — and the discrepancy is between *measured or
estimated effort* and *allocated recognition*, not between effort and willingness.

**Supporting studies.** A08 (39 allocated vs. 200–250 required hours per unit), A13 (~200
hours of approval writing carried by one person), A10 (~100 collective developer-hours for a
three-faculty programme), A04 (no financial resources or teaching-load reduction at outset;
reform sustained by intrinsic motivation and community), A14 (time and human resources cited
by instructors as limiting deeper implementation), A11 ($1,500 incentive; deep change argued
to require "sustained external resources and instructional designers"), A01 (budget
constraints; faculty fatigue), A07 (workload credit named as a necessary condition), A15 (lack
of resources characterizes the "limited institutional change" cluster; dedicated resources and
faculty training characterize the deepest).

**Why the evidence is comparable.** All nine bear on the same relation — required versus
allocated — across five countries (Australia, USA, Switzerland, UK, international aggregate)
and structurally unrelated methods (action learning, autoethnography, action research,
grounded theory, mixed-methods intervention, conceptual guideline, case survey). Crucially,
the corroboration is not all of one type: three studies supply *counts*, one supplies an
*absence* (A04's zero load reduction), one supplies a *stated precondition* (A07), one
supplies a *purchased incentive plus an explicit resource requirement* (A11), and one supplies
an *indicator variable at aggregate level* (A15).

**Shared-vulnerability caveat.** The hour figures are all reported by the people who did the
work. A08's analysis records that how the hours were measured is not reported; A13's is author
self-report; A10's is study-internal. If insider position biases these figures, it plausibly
biases them *upward*. The qualitative relation does not depend on the magnitudes, but the
magnitudes should never be quoted as measurements.

**Confidence: STRONG** for the qualitative relation; **MODERATE** for any magnitude.

**Assessment against the alternative candidate.** This is, on the evidence available, the
corpus's **strongest** convergence — stronger than the institutional-apparatus convergence
below — because it is a claim about an observed discrepancy rather than about an unobserved
counterfactual, and because its corroborating evidence takes several structurally different
forms rather than repeating one.

---

## CV-2. Institutional apparatus shapes what curricular change is possible

**Claim.** What can be changed is set by institutional machinery — workload models, staffing
arrangements, documentation formats, quality regimes, approval throughput, reward structures,
and the relative authority of disciplines, centres and external regulators — rather than by
curricular ideas or willingness to change.

**Supporting studies.** Primary: A08, A06, A11, A13. Secondary: A09 (documental compliance
coexisting with hidden dysfunction; leadership as gatekeeping; faculty exclusion from
decision-making as a structural inhibitor), A10 (labour accounting). Inverted: A05.
Contributory: A04 (resourcing absence), A15 (configuration-linked resource and coordination
variables).

**Why the evidence is comparable — and why this is the strongest form of corroboration the
corpus offers.** The four primary members reach compatible conclusions **from incompatible
framings**. A08 and A06 are critical and epistemological (A08: "chronic misalignment between
an institutional positivist epistemology... and social-constructivist/critical approaches";
A06: policy enactment and transformational learning). A11 and A13 are managerial and
practitioner-facing (A11 offers a consultative approach reducing resistance to central
administration; A13 offers replicable guidelines for programme leaders). Their methods are
equally unrelated: critical discourse analysis, action learning, autoethnography,
mixed-methods intervention. **Cross-framing convergence is stronger than same-framework
convergence, because the framings do not share assumptions that could jointly bias the
result.** Each member also supplies a *distinct mechanism* rather than repeating one.

**Caveats that prevent a higher rating on the strong form.**
1. **The determinative claim is counterfactual.** "This could not have happened because of the
   apparatus" requires an unobserved comparison. No study observes a comparable unit under
   altered apparatus.
2. **A08's available natural comparison — 7 controlled versus 4 uncontrolled units — is not
   reported** (Section 10). The corpus's cleanest opportunity to convert constraint into
   determination is unavailable at the note layer.
3. **Three of four primary members are insider or semi-internal.** A06 is the only fully
   external member, and it observes documents only.
4. **A05 inverts the valence** and must be preserved as a boundary member: institutional
   apparatus appeared first as an obstacle (academic silos; state approval) and then as the
   *solution* — a dedicated Center founded to enable cross-college affiliation. The apparatus
   can be built, not only endured.

**Confidence: STRONG** for "institutional apparatus is a mechanistically specified, repeatedly
reported constraint on curricular change." **MODERATE** for "institutional apparatus
determines what is possible."

---

## CV-3. Where units retain discretion, shallow change is preferentially completed

**Claim.** Not that reform is hard, but that **when given a choice, units select the changes
with the lowest labour, negotiation and approval cost.**

**Supporting studies.** A11: prerequisite removal attempted by 8 programmes and completed by
100% of them (maximum progress score), "boutique" course removal attempted by 3 and completed
by 100%, and **zero departments attempted signature work or learning-outcome transparency**.
A06: Assessment Reform and Active Learning rated "Present" or "Explicit" at the language level
in **all** departments, with an average full categorical decline moving to application, and
only about one-fifth applying the guidelines explicitly. A15: the top-down mandated cluster
(N=30) reaches build-in rather than redesign. A05: the critique of "cosmetic" relabeling
identifies the low-cost option other institutions take. A13 (diagnostic phase): redundancy and
misalignment accumulated over years without correction.

**Contextual independence.** UK / US / international aggregate / US / Australia; discourse
analysis / mixed-methods intervention / case survey / descriptive case / autoethnography. The
independence is high and the methods are unrelated.

**Counterexamples and minority positions, preserved.**
- **A15 also documents deep change**: clusters 1 (N=19) and 2 (N=26) reach redesign — 45 of
  131 cases. Deep change is a minority outcome in that sample, not an absent one. (And because
  A15 acknowledges success bias in its source literature, the population rate is plausibly
  lower still — **DERIVED**, reasoning: a success-biased sample over-represents deep outcomes.)
- **A13 and A04 achieved deep change**, under crisis conditions and over four years
  respectively.
- **A02 documents narrow-but-deep change**: one competency, systemic intervention across three
  curriculum phases.
- **A06 supplies the corpus's minority reading**: shallow linguistic uptake may be a
  "necessary but insufficient threshold," a "minimal contractual agreement enabling later
  reforms." **Every other study reads shallow uptake as failure.** The corpus cannot adjudicate
  (Sections 13 and 15).

**Confidence: STRONG** for the tendency; the counterexamples constrain it to a tendency, never
a law, and the scope condition (units retain discretion over which changes to attempt) is
essential.

---

## CV-4. Documents and practice diverge

**Claim.** The curriculum as represented and the curriculum as enacted are not the same, and
formal compliance can coexist with substantive non-enactment.

**Supporting studies.** Endorsed explicitly by A02, A06, A07, A08, A09, A11. Evidenced:
**A11** supplies the cleanest empirical instance — Curricular Analytics revealed that formal
catalog requirements "were routinely waived by advisors and faculty in actual practice," an
incidental by-product rather than a designed test. **A06** supplies the only quantified
gradient, but across *documentary layers* (Language → Intent → Application), not across the
document/practice boundary. **A08** supplies the strongest structural claim: the gap is
produced by the recording apparatus.

**Confidence: STRONG** that the corpus endorses the distinction; **MODERATE** that divergence
occurs (one incidental empirical instance, one documentary-internal gradient, one
argumentative case); **TENTATIVE** for anything about its size.

---

## CV-5. External regulatory and accreditation bodies materially shape curricula, including their expressible form

**Claim.** These bodies determine not only content but **the format in which curriculum may be
expressed** — A08 (TEQSA-derived templates requiring behavioural outcome statements), A06
(programme specifications as contractually binding, described as a "negotiated pact").

**Supporting studies.** A06, A08, A09, A10, A13, A05, A11, A15 — eight studies.

**Why this is nonetheless MODERATE.** Eight mentions do not make a strong finding. These
bodies appear almost entirely **as inferred context, not as measured objects**. A06's claim
that PSRBs outrank central policy is the corpus's only comparative claim, and its own analysis
records that how the comparison was operationalized is not described. **No study in the corpus
studies any of these actors directly** (Gap G4). This is the clearest case in the synthesis
where frequency and confidence diverge.

**Confidence: MODERATE.**

---

## CV-6. Change does not proceed as the linear or cyclical schemas depict

**Supporting studies.** A01 (against linear models), A13 ("complex, dynamic, overlapping, and
non-linear, in contrast to the simplified cyclical schemas formally presented in the
literature"), A04 (no stepwise account given), A15 (patterns are "different processes... rather
than different stages," and HEIs move between them), A12 (implicitly: initiation is
situational, with four pathways and no fixed order).

**Why not higher.** Four of the five are single-case self-accounts, and denying linearity is a
low-cost claim for a practitioner narrating a messy experience. **A15 is the only study with
the reach to support it as a pattern**, and it does. This is a **convergence of assertion with
one convergence of evidence inside it**.

**Confidence: MODERATE.**

---

## A convergence flagged as doctrinal rather than evidential

**Content-last / ends-before-means sequencing.** A12 reports *unanimous* participant agreement
that discussion of specific subject content should be the last step; A04 makes shared vision
an indispensable precondition of syllabus work; A02 works backwards from a long-term goal;
A16's four steps place content design third; A14 settles macro-level decisions before
micromethods; A05 begins from programme objectives.

**This is a convergence of assertion, not of evidence.** Five studies in the corpus draw on a
shared doctrinal inheritance — constructive alignment, outcome-based education, Bloom, SOLO
(A02, A08, A12, A13, A16) — which supplies the sequencing norm directly. Independent
corroboration would require a study observing that content-last sequencing produced better
outcomes than content-first; none does. **Counterexamples inside the corpus:** A11 began from
structural features (prerequisites, complexity), and A01 began from industry scanning and
course-level self-assessment.

**Confidence: STRONG** that the corpus asserts it; **TENTATIVE** as an empirical claim.

# 12. PARTIAL CONVERGENCES

Patterns that hold under stated conditions and fail outside them.

**PC-1. Participation by prospective enactors conditions faithful enactment — where enactment
requires interpretive work.**
Supported by A14 (the only direct observation: the integrative block course partially failed
when delegated to an instructor who had not participated in the redesign), A06 (only
departments fusing the reform into their own epistemology enacted it), A15 (participatory
vision-building linked to durability), A04 (shared vision as precondition), A10 (the CoP as
the vehicle of change).
**Scope condition the corpus makes visible but does not state:** this holds where enactment
depends on an individual instructor's interpretation. It does *not* obviously hold for
structural changes — A11's prerequisite removals were completed at 100% regardless of who
enacted them. **DERIVED**, reasoning: the failure cases all involve changes requiring
interpretive delivery, while the uniformly completed cases involve changes executed by
administrative amendment.
**Confidence: MODERATE.** A14's causal attribution has unexplored alternatives recorded in its
own analysis (instructor style, subject difficulty, timing); A15 is second-order with
acknowledged success bias; A10's evidence is prospective concern, not observed failure; A06
measures documents only. **Four studies here do not compound to STRONG.**

**PC-2. Authority configuration is associated with depth of outcome — in one second-order
dataset only.**
A15 is the only study with the reach to establish this: collaborative (N=19) and bottom-up
(N=26) reach redesign; top-down mandated (N=30) mostly reaches build-in with "little formal
participation, absent unified vision, low ownership, and faculty resistance."
**Limits:** cross-sectional; published, self-reported, success-biased source cases; missing
information statistically treated as irrelevant, which the authors acknowledge may mask real
influences. **And a primary-study caution sits beside it:** A11's consultative hybrid produced
favourable faculty experience and a null structural result. Not a contradiction — different
measures — but it blocks any inference that consultative configuration secures depth.
**Confidence: MODERATE within A15's design; TENTATIVE as a general association.**

**PC-3. Informality can substitute for missing formal support, but does not override adverse
formal structure.**
For substitution: A15 (informal collaboration among champions compensating for absent
formalized leadership support), A04 (informal community as "principal motivational fuel" where
formal resourcing was absent), A13 (informal advocacy changed a formal rite).
Against override: A08 (informal pedagogical intent could not survive adverse documentation
requirements), A06 (formal external requirements outrank formal internal policy).
**Confidence: MODERATE.** Derived by juxtaposition; asserted by no study.

**PC-4. External warrant reduces conflict about ends — but not deliberative cost.**
Holds for A05 and A13, neither of which reports substantive disagreement about *what* should
change, only about approval. **Falsified from inside its own cluster by A01**, which had a
strong external warrant (peer/competitive/aspirant benchmarking, alumni interviews, a
50-respondent survey) and still required conduct norms, reflective sessions and explicit
management of departmental power disputes.
**Confidence: TENTATIVE.**

**PC-5. Relational outcomes accompany deliberative processes.**
Holds within Cluster 2 plus A01; reported by participants in every instance (Section 9).
**Confidence: MODERATE, deliberately not upgraded.**

**PC-6. Crisis as an enabling condition.**
A13 alone (COVID-19 credited with breaking habitual resistance — the author's own
interpretation, with no independent evidence recorded). A06 raises COVID-19 and Black Lives
Matter only as future research. **A singleton on a condition that plausibly matters a great
deal. Confidence: SPECULATIVE.**

# 13. DIVERGENCES AND CONTRADICTIONS

## Strict contradiction is nearly absent — and that is a finding about the corpus, not a merit

Studies differ so widely in object, scale, conception (D1) and accepted success criterion (D8)
that they rarely make commensurable claims. Manufacturing contradiction where the corpus
offers **incommensurability** would misdescribe it. But incommensurability is not a virtue: it
is a symptom of a literature whose parts do not test each other (Section 14, and Gap G7).

**Supporting observation:** as recorded in the matrix, **no study in this corpus takes another
corpus study's framework as its object of evaluation.** The closest adjacencies are shared
authorship lineages — Harden's ten-question model cited in A09 while A07 is Harden's own AMEE
Guide; Kandiko and Blackmore (2010) cited by A11 while A06 is by Kandiko Howson. **DERIVED**
from field 6 across all sixteen entries; **note-layer bounded**, since the fichamentos may not
preserve complete citation lists.

---

## DV-1. Should curricular structure be simplified? — divergence, with an unadjudicable normative core

**A03** optimizes toward credit and difficulty balance and treats deviation from targets as
cost (goal deviations G1 = 2, G2 = 1.2857, G3 = 2, G4 = 0.375, reported as high balance).
**A11** argues that "blindly reducing complexity should not be established as a final goal,"
because an unstructured curriculum shifts the coherence burden onto students — and documents
its own metric *rising* (STEM 316 → 322) for a change it regards as pedagogically beneficial.

**Classification: DIVERGENCE, not contradiction.** They answer different questions (how to
plan a prospective schedule vs. whether an existing metric should be an optimization target)
and use different constructs (credit/difficulty balance vs. blocking and delay factors).

**But there is a genuine normative incompatibility inside it:** A03's design logic treats a
computable structural optimum as a legitimate target; A11 denies that any such optimum should
be a goal. **The corpus cannot adjudicate, because neither study measures learning.** A11's
own analysis flags its metric's construct validity as questionable; A03's analysis records that
the mapping index's correspondence to actual pedagogical content is not evidenced.

**Note what this divergence rules out:** since both studies formalize curricular structure
mathematically, **the disagreement cannot be explained by a quantitative/qualitative
methodological split.** Confidence that the disagreement is real: **STRONG**.

---

## DV-2. Can curriculum development be standardized? — real, substantive, and reframed

Standardizers: **A16** (standardization and scientific rigor treated as normative goals
directly affecting teaching-learning effectiveness; ten models collapsed into four steps),
**A07** (a definitive nine-step process), **A03** (an algorithm), **A05** (a ten-step roadmap).
Contextualizers: **A12** ("no universal recipes," from 6 interviews and 22 questionnaires),
**A14** (anti-technocratic; multiple didactic frameworks preserved), **A15** (six patterns;
context-dependent trajectories), **A11** (explicitly non-prescriptive), **A01** (against linear
models), **A09** ("interpreters," not "reviewers").

**A09 breaks the tempting domain explanation.** The standardizers cluster in medical education
and operations research — fields with strong accreditation and formal-method traditions — but
A09 is a medical-education scoping review arguing against checklist evaluation. Report the
divergence; do not attribute it confidently to domain.

**Reframing (Section 8).** Both camps produce portable instruments. The disagreement is about
**determinacy**: whether the instrument should decide or should structure the deliberation.
Only A03 occupies the determining position. Confidence that the disagreement is real:
**STRONG**; confidence in the reframing: **MODERATE** (derived by comparison of D7 placements
against fields 17–18).

---

## DV-3. What is a curriculum map for? — divergence that is also complementarity

A07 (representation and communication) vs. A02 (representation stops too early; the point is
the transition to intervention) vs. A09 (mapping shows *where*, not *why*) vs. A10 (its value
is social mediation, not informational content). These are four different answers, and two of
them are explicit criticisms of the others. **Confidence: STRONG** that the divergence is real.
Section 14 shows the same set also forms a complementarity.

---

## DV-4. Is resistance dispositional or structural? — largely reconcilable, with a residue

**Dispositional:** A01 (comfort, tenure clock, power disputes), A04 (autonomy, territory wars,
contested leadership), A15 (faculty resistance, ownership deficits).
**Structural:** A10 (absent time and expertise, not opposition), A11 (attitudes and behaviour
decoupled), A13 (unrewarded labour; bureaucracy as active disincentive).

**Adjudication (a Stage-2C result).** These accounts are largely about **different
observables**. A01 and A04 observed *conflict during deliberation* — difficult debates,
territoriality, contested leadership. A11, A10 and A13 observed *non-adoption of available
changes*. Deliberative conflict and non-adoption are different phenomena, and both accounts
can be true simultaneously: **dispositional factors explain conflict inside the process;
structural factors explain the ceiling on what the process produces.** **DERIVED**, reasoning:
the two study sets report on different fields (16) content — interpersonal dynamics versus
completion rates — and no study reports both.

**Residue that does not reconcile.** Whether *non-adoption itself* is dispositional remains
open. A11 addresses it best and does not settle it: its own analysis records the
attitude/behaviour discrepancy as unresolved, offering two live readings — an instrument that
failed to capture resistance, or low concern coexisting with rational avoidance of high-effort
tasks. Its survey is 18 respondents with an acknowledged self-selection caveat. **A15 is worth
noting here:** even its "faculty resistance" appears inside a cluster also characterized by
absent unified vision, little formal participation and low ownership — i.e. embedded in
configuration rather than free-standing.

**Confidence: MODERATE** that the structural reading is better supported for non-adoption;
**the dispositional account is preserved, not defeated.**

---

## DV-5. Is shallow uptake a threshold or a ceiling? — the corpus cannot adjudicate

**A06 alone** offers the developmental reading: linguistic conformity as a "necessary but
insufficient threshold" that "may represent the beginning of a minimal contractual agreement
enabling later reforms." **Every other depth-oriented study reads shallow uptake as failure**
(A11, A15, A05, A13).

**Why it cannot be adjudicated — a design-level, not merely evidential, gap.** A06's own
hypothesis is untestable within its design: a second-year documentary snapshot of a nine-year
reform. A15 asserts that HEIs move between patterns but tracks no trajectory. A11's window
covers one intervention cycle. **No study in the corpus has a longitudinal design capable of
distinguishing a threshold from a ceiling.**

**One piece of indirect, mechanism-divergent evidence exists and should be recorded.** A05
supplies the corpus's only documented case of an initially shallow curricular realization
being subsequently deepened: the launched programme "resembled a modified biology degree," and
a full scope-and-sequence revision followed, making sustainability the priority focus of each
course, adding new courses and mandatory integrative seminars across all four years.
**The mechanism was not accumulated linguistic conformity — it was the appointment of a
dedicated programme director with authority and time.** This neither confirms nor refutes
A06's hypothesis; it identifies a different deepening mechanism. **Confidence: TENTATIVE**
(single case; the director is the study's first author; the "modified biology degree"
characterization is the author's own).

**Stakes.** If A06 is right, interventions producing only linguistic uptake are worthwhile
groundwork; if wrong, they consume reform capacity while producing institutional mimicry. The
practical stakes are high and the corpus is silent.

---

## DV-6. Three syntheses of curriculum literatures reach three incompatible epistemic conclusions

**A16** concludes toward standardization. **A15** concludes toward irreducible plurality
("different processes... rather than different stages"). **A09** concludes toward interpretive
diagnosis ("interpreters," not "reviewers").

**Stage 2B read this as divergence not attributable to differences in primary evidence, since
all three work over published literatures. That inference needs qualifying.** The three do not
review the same literature: A16 reviews curriculum-*development models* in medical education;
A09 reviews curricular *quality standards and inhibitors* in medical/health professions
education; A15 reviews *institutionalization processes* for sustainability curricula in HEIs.
**The divergence is therefore not attributable to evidence *type*, but may be partly
attributable to object.** The divergence remains real and substantive; the control is weaker
than it first appears. **Confidence: STRONG** that the disagreement exists; **MODERATE** that
it is a disagreement about the nature of curricular knowledge rather than about three
different subject matters.

---

# 14. COMPLEMENTARITIES

## The corpus jointly covers a trajectory that no single study covers

**CP-1.** A12 (initiation) + A04 / A14 / A10 (deliberation) + A08 (implementation) + A06 / A11
(uptake depth) + A15 (sustaining). **This is the corpus's principal complementarity and
simultaneously its principal vulnerability**: each segment rests on one or two studies, none
overlaps enough to corroborate another, and the integration is performed by the analyst, not
by the studies. Stage 2B's Adjudication 2 sharpens the point: the three question-types —
*what should change* (A03, A05, A13, A16, A02), *how change decisions are made* (A04, A12,
A14, A10, A01), *how change is institutionalized* (A15, with A10 and A08 contributing failure
cases) — have **nearly disjoint study sets, with only A05 and A15 substantially spanning more
than one.** The near-disjointness is itself the finding.

**Interpretation.** This corpus is not a cumulative literature. It is a set of partially
overlapping local literatures that do not test one another. Its complementarity is real but
**constructed**, and its coherence is therefore the analyst's responsibility rather than a
property of the field.

## Three layers of the mapping problem

**CP-2.** A07 (how to represent) + A02 (how to move from a detected gap to an intervention) +
A09 (why standards go unmet even when documented) + A10 (why an artifact works socially even
when its informational content is modest). Together these form a fuller account than any
supplies alone, and **A09 explicitly positions itself as the missing layer**. Note that
DV-3 and CP-2 describe the same four studies: they disagree about purpose *and* jointly
compose a fuller account. Both readings are correct.

## The two halves of the technical/social cleavage

**CP-3.** A03 (how to choose content formally) + A04 / A14 (how content decisions are actually
settled socially). A03's He-Xie framework *claims* to unite exactly these halves; its own
analysis records that the human-coordination half is thinly instantiated. **The corpus
therefore contains one asserted synthesis whose evidence does not realize it, and two studies
(A11, A14) that realize a version of it without claiming a unifying theory.**

**Verdict on the question of whether technical/analytical and social/organizational
perspectives compete.** They are better understood as **complementary layers**, on three
grounds: (i) the two studies that combined them did so successfully at the level of practice
(A11's instrument inside a consultative process; A14's neutral data supplied for participatory
decision); (ii) the one study that examined practitioners found them hybridized rather than
chosen (A12); (iii) the pure poles are pure largely because each did not examine the other
side — A03 reports no organizational process, A04 reports no curricular structure.
**Confidence: MODERATE.** The competing reading is not refuted: A03 and A11 draw opposite
normative conclusions from the same formalization (DV-1), which is a genuine conflict that
layering does not dissolve.

## Mechanism and outcome, supplied by different studies

**CP-4.** A11 supplies the **outcome pattern** — openness without action, 100% completion of
cheap changes, zero attempts at expensive ones, null movement on its declared metric. A08,
A13, A06 and A09 supply candidate **mechanisms** — workload underestimation, approval
throughput, documentation format, reward structures, gatekeeping. Neither alone explains;
jointly they do. **This joining is the analyst's, and the studies do not corroborate one
another's mechanisms.** Without CP-4 the depth finding (CV-3) is a description; with it, it
becomes an explanation — but a proposed one.

## Institutional structure as constraint and as instrument

**CP-5 (new).** **A08** and **A05** are inverse cases of one variable. A08's team was bounded
by institutional structures it could not alter — workload models, staffing arrangements,
documentation templates, and control over only 7 of 11 units. A05's project *built* structure:
a dedicated Center for Sustainability Studies founded specifically to enable cross-college
faculty affiliation, plus transfer partnerships and a dedicated director.

**Jointly they support a proposition neither states:** institutional structure is a
*manipulable variable* in curricular change and not only a constraint — but manipulating it
requires authority and resources (a dean, a formulation group, state approval, a new
organizational unit) that A08's team did not have. **DERIVED**; **Confidence: MODERATE**, and
scope-limited to cases where new organizational units can be created.

## The pivotal moment, approached from opposite ends

**CP-6 (new).** **A03** and **A04** are the corpus's most distant studies, and both locate the
pivotal work at **criterion formation** — A03 as expert weighting of eight competencies, A04 as
collective philosophical construction preceding syllabus work. **A14's Briefing Note** is the
only device in the corpus that performs this function without settling it, by documenting the
decisions requiring resolution, the alternatives adopted elsewhere, and each option's impact
against stated criteria. Together the three describe a phase the corpus otherwise leaves thin
(Section 3). **Confidence: MODERATE** as a complementarity; **the three approaches are not
commensurable**, and no study compares them.

# 15. UNRESOLVED TENSIONS

Tensions retained only where closer synthesis confirms them. One Stage-2B tension is found
partly artificial and is reported as such.

---

## T-1. Shallow adoption: developmental threshold or persistent ceiling? — RETAINED, unresolvable within the corpus

Fully argued at DV-5. Retained because both readings are live, the corpus contains no design
capable of separating them, and the practical consequences diverge sharply. **A05's deepening
case supplies indirect evidence for a different mechanism (a dedicated authorized person with
time), not for A06's threshold mechanism.** Status: **UNRESOLVED, and structurally
unresolvable from this corpus.**

---

## T-2. Transferability of artifacts vs. context-dependence of their production — PARTLY ARTIFICIAL; reduced

Fully argued at Section 8. The non-transferability evidence targets **artifacts and enacted
practices**; the transferability claims target **procedures**. Once distinguished, most of the
tension dissolves. **What survives is not a contradiction but two other things:** (i) a live
disagreement about determinacy (DV-2); (ii) an evidential vacancy — no study tests the
portability of any procedure (Gap G7). **A03 remains a genuine instance** of the original
tension, since its output is both procedure and artifact.

**Reported honestly:** this is a case where Stage 2B's tension was partly an artifact of
describing two different objects with one word.

---

## T-3. Individual resistance vs. structural impediment — REDUCED, with a preserved residue

Fully argued at DV-4. Reduced by distinguishing deliberative conflict from non-adoption.
Residue retained: whether non-adoption is dispositional is unsettled, and **A01 and A04's
dispositional evidence is first-hand and is not defeated by the structural reading.**

---

## T-4. Simplification / optimization vs. preservation of curricular complexity — RETAINED

Fully argued at DV-1. Retained as a normative divergence the corpus cannot adjudicate because
neither party measures learning, and because it survives the elimination of the obvious
explanation (both parties formalize mathematically).

---

## T-5. Intermediary necessity vs. intermediary dependency — RETAINED (the corpus's sharpest internal tension)

**A10 argues that developer technical support "is the very condition that permits the
manifestation of a viable CoP" — and reports, from the same three participants, that they
considered the likelihood of continuing without facilitation to be low.** The condition of
success is simultaneously the mechanism of fragility. Corroborated from two directions: A14's
artifact failed when handed to a non-participant; A08's paid participation ended with the
funding.

**Why this is not simply a limitation.** If the intermediary's core function is to absorb
labour the institution does not allocate (CV-1), then dependency is not a design flaw of
facilitation — it is the predictable consequence of financing change from outside the
allocation system. **DERIVED**, reasoning: CV-1 establishes the unallocated-labour relation;
A10 establishes that the intermediary supplied that labour; the dependency follows.
**Confidence: MODERATE.** Retained as unresolved because no study observes what happens after
an intermediary withdraws.

---

## T-6. Studies that critique metric-driven evaluation evidence their own success with metrics — RETAINED, reinterpreted

A08 critiques "student-as-client" quality metrics and evidences success with retention (its
own analysis flags this). A11 warns against its complexity metric while using it. A13
critiques bureaucracy while counting approved documents. A05 critiques cosmetic programmes
while counting enrolment.

**Reinterpretation.** This is not hypocrisy. **Institutions supply only the metrics these
studies distrust**, and Cluster 3's observation applies: the studies most critical of
institutional metrics are those with the least outcome data, because the metrics they reject
are the ones their institutions would have supplied. The tension is real and is a property of
the institutional information environment, not of authorial inconsistency. Retained on that
reading.

---

## T-7. Prescriptive confidence is inversely related to evidentiary reach — RETAINED and sharpened

Strong prescriptive claims from non-comparative single cases: A05 (a ten-step roadmap from one
institution in one state policy context), A01 (a "replicable" framework from one department),
A13 ("replicable guidelines" from one crisis-affected university), A02 (a single case described
as demonstrating the ToC approach's effectiveness), A03 (an algorithm from one programme and
five experts). All are flagged in their own analyses.

Meanwhile, of the six studies with any comparative reach (A06, A09, A11, A12, A15, A16),
**five are non- or anti-prescriptive**; A16 is the sole standardizing exception. Section 17
develops this as a corpus-level argument. Retained as a tension because the corpus's most
usable outputs are produced by the designs least able to warrant them.

---

## T-8. Participation is recommended while its cost is never priced — RETAINED (new)

The corpus recommends participation widely (PC-1, PC-2, CV-1's remedies, Cluster 2 throughout)
while its own strongest convergence establishes that curricular labour is unallocated and
unrewarded. **Participation multiplies the number of people whose unallocated labour is
required:** A04's four years with no load reduction; A01's faculty fatigue; A13's 17 then 13
faculty on top of one director's ~200 hours; A10's ~100 developer-hours for three faculty.
**No study in the corpus costs participation against its benefit**, and A10 names comparative
evaluation of its own deep-advisory model against standard faculty-development services as
future work. Retained; **Confidence: MODERATE** that the tension is real and unaddressed.

---

## T-9. Facilitator neutrality is argued vital, and the corpus's most comprehensive reform was chaired by an authority holder — RETAINED

A10 argues neutrality is vital, in a setting its own analysis records as free of
compulsory-reform pressure; A04's four-year comprehensive reform was chaired by the academic
vice-president, with his leadership contested but not fatal. Coexistable; unresolved.

# 16. CROSS-STUDY CONFIGURATIONS

## What the cluster structure makes understandable

C1–C5 are not restated here; their analytical payoff is.

- **C1 (curriculum formalized as an analyzable object)** explains **why the corpus's
  formalizing studies do not accumulate.** Representation (A07), computation (A03) and
  diagnosis (A02, A09) are separate projects that partially criticize each other. A reader
  encountering all three finds apparent redundancy where there is actually disagreement.
- **C2 (curricular change as facilitated social process)** explains **why relational outcomes
  appear as primary findings rather than side effects** — for these studies, changing the
  group *is* the mechanism by which the curriculum changes — and it localizes the
  facilitator-dependency problem as a property of that cluster rather than of curricular
  change in general.
- **C3 (institutional apparatus as determinant of curricular possibility)** supplies the
  **mechanism** for the corpus's depth finding (CV-3 + CP-4) and explains a corpus-level
  asymmetry: the studies most critical of institutional metrics have the least outcome data.
- **C4 (externally warranted curricular modernization)** is **most valuable because it fails
  informatively.** Its predictive claim — external warrant reduces deliberative load — is
  falsified from inside by A01. Stage 2B rated it MODERATE and provisional, and this synthesis
  concurs: it is the cluster at greatest risk of being a keyword family ("employability,"
  "market relevance," "currency").
- **C5 (second-order synthesis)** explains **why a reader consulting the corpus's syntheses
  receives no settled guidance**: the disagreement is about what kind of knowledge curriculum
  development admits of — although Section 13 qualifies this by noting the three reviews do
  not share an object.

**Overlap is essential and must not be removed.** Seven of sixteen studies hold defensible
membership in more than one cluster (A01, A02, A05, A09, A10, A11, A13). A11 is the principal
bridging study: as formal as A03 in instrument, wholly Cluster 3 in finding — which
demonstrates that formal instruments can be deployed inside, and subordinated to, an
institutional-process account. Three studies (A03, A07, A12) are boundary-defining rather than
classifiable, and forcing them into change-process categories would destroy the information
they carry.

## Comparing the two representations

**The cluster view** is an intellectual map: it shows what kinds of project this literature
contains and why they talk past each other. Its cost is that it hides four internal splits
(representation/computation/diagnosis inside C1; facilitated/self-organized inside C2;
epistemic-infrastructural/labour-throughput inside C3; market-referenced/framework-referenced
inside C4).

**The dimensional view (D1–D9)** is more precise per study and supports finer comparisons —
studies adjacent on one axis may be maximally distant on another (A03 and A11 share a
conception and oppose each other on prescription; A04 and A14 share conception, difficulty and
success criterion but differ on authority configuration).

**A criticism this synthesis adds: the multi-axis grid overstates the independence of its
axes.** The dimensions are not orthogonal. Reading the interaction notes inside D1–D9 together
yields **four coupled bundles plus one cross-cutting dimension**:

| Bundle | Dimensions | Coupling | The exception that keeps it a bundle, not a collapse |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ontology–output** | D1 + D7 | Formal conceptions tend to yield procedural outputs | **A11**: formal conception, explicitly non-prescriptive output |
| **Problem-location–success** | D2 + D8 | Where a study locates difficulty predicts what it accepts as success | **A06**: locates difficulty at uptake depth and enactment, accepts no success criterion at all |
| **Evidence** | D3 + D9 | Crossing the representation gap requires observation or a second data stream, which most single-case documentary designs lack | **A08, A14**: single-case designs that nonetheless gather enactment-side evidence |
| **Institutional** | D5 + D6 | Authority configuration produces or removes enablers; sometimes authority *is* the impediment | **A04**: authority-chaired and *still* unresourced — configuration did not supply enablers |
| **Cross-cutting** | D4 | Warrant interacts with D5 (tendency), with D2 (design-located difficulties tend to be externally warranted), and with D6-iii (A08: warrant format *is* the impediment) | **A01**: strong external warrant with heavy deliberative cost |

## Verdict on the most faithful representation

**A combined representation, with the clusters read as recurring joint settings of the coupled
bundles — not as categories.** Specifically:

- **the bundles** (rather than nine independent axes) describe each study most economically;
- **the clusters** name the recurring joint settings and explain what each configuration makes
  visible or invisible;
- **the outliers** (A03, A07, A12) and **bridges** (A11, A01, A05, A09, A10, A13) are carried
  as first-class information, not as residue;
- **a single mutually exclusive taxonomy remains inadequate**, and Stage 2B's conclusion is
  confirmed rather than merely inherited: seven studies hold multiple defensible memberships
  and three resist classification for substantive reasons.

**One alternative deserves standing recognition:** grouping by **locus of difficulty (D2)** is
more useful than the clusters for anyone asking what is known about a particular phase, and it
makes CP-1's complementarity immediately legible. Its cost is that it forces A13 and A01 —
which explicitly deny stage segmentation — into a framework they reject, and leaves A03
unplaceable.

# 17. HIGHER-LEVEL ARGUMENTS

Nine arguments the corpus defensibly supports. Each is stated so that it could be shown wrong,
and each closes by naming the stronger conclusion the corpus does **not** license.

## AR-1. Curricular change in this corpus is financed by unallocated labour, and the depth attained tracks whether someone absorbs it

### Argument
Curricular change consistently requires labour, negotiation and approval effort beyond what
institutional systems allocate. Whether a change of any depth occurs depends on whether some
actor absorbs that unpriced cost — an individual leader, a facilitating intermediary, an
externally funded project, or a group's uncompensated commitment. Where no absorber exists,
change stalls at the level that requires no additional labour.

### Evidence base
A08, A13, A10, A04, A14, A11, A05, A06, A07, A15, A01, A02, A12.

### Reasoning
The **cost side** is established by CV-1 across nine studies, five countries and unrelated
methods, with three independent numerical instances (39 vs. 200–250 hours; ~200 hours; ~100
developer-hours) and one structural instance (A04's zero load reduction).
The **absorber side** is visible by comparing outcomes against who paid:

| Absorber | Case | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| A single middle leader | A13 (~200 hours; temporary sacrifice of her own research) | Deep structural change: 11 programmes/51 subjects → 2/25; 103 documents approved; one durable governance simplification |
| External intermediaries | A10 (~100 developer-hours) | Community of practice formed; participants expect no continuation once the absorber leaves |
| External project funding | A14 (2013–2016), A08 (pilot period only) | Redesign delivered; paid participation ends with the funding |
| A group's intrinsic motivation | A04 (four years, no resourcing) | Comprehensive reform completed; sustainability flagged as a risk by its own analysis |
| An institution that builds a permanent absorber | A05 (dedicated director; Center for Sustainability Studies) | The corpus's only documented case of an initially shallow realization being deepened |
| **No absorber for the expensive changes** | A11 (money and support supplied for the process, but deep change explicitly requiring "sustained external resources and instructional designers") | 100% completion of cheap changes; **zero attempts** at expensive ones; null movement on the declared metric |

A06 completes the pattern from the documentary side: language-level uptake is nearly costless
and occurred in all departments; application is not costless and dropped a full category.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A02 achieved a documented change using existing resources by design** — the Theory of
  Change explicitly mobilized existing curricular time, existing assessment materials and
  existing leadership. This looks like a counterexample, and on closer reading is a **scope
  qualification**: the change was narrow (one competency) and its outputs were documentary.
  The argument should be restated as *labour cost scales with scope*, and narrow-scope changes
  can fit inside existing allocation.
- **A03 has no labour dimension at all.** An optimization model displaces the labour question
  rather than answering it. This is a boundary of the argument's applicability, not a refutation.
- **A01 and A04 attribute non-adoption partly to disposition**, not to cost (DV-4). Their
  first-hand evidence stands.
- The hour magnitudes are self-reported by those who did the work, with measurement procedures
  NOT REPORTED (A08) or explicitly diary-based (A13).

### Confidence
**STRONG** for the descriptive claim (curricular change is accompanied by labour that
institutional systems do not allocate or recognize).
**MODERATE** for the explanatory claim (depth tracks the presence of an absorber). No study
manipulates labour supply; the absorber typology is the analyst's construction; A11's null
result is consistent with, but not diagnostic of, a labour explanation, and its own analysis
records the attitude/behaviour discrepancy as unresolved.

### Scope conditions
Higher-education institutions in which curricular work is not a costed component of workload
models. Applies where units retain discretion over what to attempt. Does not apply to
optimization-style planning problems with no organizational process attached (A03), and
weakens for narrow-scope changes payable from existing allocation (A02).

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **allocating labour would produce deep change.** No study in the corpus tests a labour
remedy. A11 supplied money ($1,500), data and administrative help and obtained no attempts at
the expensive changes — which is at least compatible with labour being necessary but not
sufficient. The corpus supports a *constraint* claim, not a *lever* claim.

## AR-2. Where units retain discretion, depth of change is better predicted by the cost of a change than by expressed attitudes toward it

### Argument
Given a menu of possible curricular changes, units complete the low-cost ones and do not
attempt the high-cost ones, largely independently of how favourably they report viewing the
reform.

### Evidence base
A11 (decisive), A06, A15, A05, A13 (diagnostic phase).

### Reasoning
A11 is the only study in the corpus that measured attitude and behaviour separately: perceived
usefulness 4.23 ± 0.75, concern **1.27 ± 0.46** — and **zero** departments attempted signature
work or learning-outcome transparency, while prerequisite removal was attempted by 8
programmes and completed by 100%. Attitude was uniformly favourable; behaviour was uniformly
cost-selected. A06 shows the same profile in documents rather than actions: uniform uptake
where uptake is a matter of wording, a full categorical drop where it is a matter of practice.
A15 associates the shallowest outcomes with the configuration that supplies neither
participation nor resources. A05 identifies the low-cost option — rearranging existing courses
— as the field's characteristic failure mode.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A15's clusters 1 and 2 (45 of 131 cases) reach redesign.** Cost-selection is a tendency,
  not a law, and deep change is a minority outcome rather than an absent one.
- **A13 and A04 achieved deep change under high cost** — by absorbing it (AR-1).
- **A01 and A04 report genuine dispositional resistance** that cost does not explain.
- **A11's own analysis leaves the discrepancy unresolved** and offers a competing reading: the
  instrument may have failed to capture resistance. Its survey is 18 respondents with an
  acknowledged self-selection caveat, and self-selection would plausibly bias reported
  attitudes favourably.

### Confidence
**MODERATE.** The claim rests decisively on one study with a small survey, supported by one
documentary study and one success-biased aggregate. It is well motivated and not established.

### Scope conditions
Settings where units choose which changes to attempt from a menu, and where the reform does
not mandate specific changes. The corpus contains no primary case of a mandated change with no
discretion; A15's top-down cluster is second-order evidence only.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **faculty attitudes are irrelevant to curricular change.** The corpus shows attitude and
behaviour decoupled in one study measuring both; it does not show attitudes doing no work.
A01's and A04's deliberative conflict is attitudinal and consequential.

## AR-3. The corpus's portable outputs come disproportionately from its least comparative designs, and none has been tested for portability

### Argument
Prescriptive generality in this corpus is **inversely associated** with comparative evidentiary
reach: the studies offering the most transferable procedures are among those least able to
warrant transfer, and the studies with the greatest comparative reach are the most reluctant
to prescribe.

### Evidence base
Prescribing from minimal reach: A03, A05, A07, A02, A13, A01. Declining to prescribe from
greater reach: A15 (131 cases → an explicitly non-developmental typology), A12 (6 + 22
practitioners → "no universal recipes"), A09 (13 articles → "interpreters," not "reviewers"),
A11 (9 programmes, pre/post → "non-prescriptive reflective guide"), A06 (15 departments → a
diagnostic instrument and no accepted success criterion). Counterexample: A16.

### Reasoning
Of the six studies with any comparative reach, **five are non- or anti-prescriptive**. Of the
ten single-case or non-empirical studies, **six produce transferable procedures or claim
replicability**. Meanwhile Gap G7 records that **not one study in the corpus compares two
approaches to curricular change against each other** — and three studies (A10, A03, A12) name
that absence as future work, including A12's observation that developers assess success only
through informal, intuitive means.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A16 is a clean counterexample**: 18 empirical articles across eight countries, and the
  corpus's strongest standardization claim.
- **A09 partially resists the pattern in the other direction**: cross-case reach and a highly
  structured 37/19/8 framework, though its *intent* is anti-procedural.
- The association is descriptive of these sixteen studies. **How the corpus was assembled is
  not recorded**, so this cannot be generalized to the underlying literature.

### Confidence
**MODERATE.** The counts are directly readable and one counterexample exists; the D7
placements rest on explicit statements in fields 17, 18 and 21; the sample's representativeness
is unknown.

### Scope conditions
A statement about this corpus, not about curriculum research generally.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **the portable procedures do not work.** The corpus does not test them, which is a
different claim from testing and refuting them. A07 is the one study that states the conditions
under which its framework would be expected to work (institutional support, workload credit,
dedicated leadership time, a simple interface) — untested, but at least falsifiable.

## AR-4. The corpus's dispute about standardization is a dispute about determinacy, not about portability

### Argument
Both camps in the corpus's sharpest disagreement produce portable instruments. They disagree
about whether the instrument should **determine** the curricular decision or **structure the
deliberation** that makes it. On that question A03 stands alone.

### Evidence base
Determining: A03. Sequencing: A07, A16, A05, A02. Structuring: A09, A06, A15, A11, A14, A12,
A01, A13. Descriptive/critical: A08, A04.

### Reasoning
The contextualists all supply artifacts intended for use elsewhere: A12's Figure 1, A14's
Briefing Note and Pedagogical Workshop, A15's self-reflection scheme, A11's reflective guide
and metric, A01's two-phase framework. Portability is therefore not what divides the corpus.
What divides it is whether the artifact returns the answer (A03's model selects 35 of 53
courses and schedules them) or organizes a judgement someone else must make (A09's explicit
"reviewers vs. interpreters"; A14's coach role, "not a technocratic imposition of inaccessible
theoretical models").

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A16 genuinely wants standardization as a normative goal**, treating scientific rigor and
  standardization as directly affecting teaching-learning effectiveness — this is more than a
  sequencing claim and sits between "sequencing" and "determining."
- **A09 is a hard boundary case**: highly structured form, anti-procedural intent.
- The reframing is derived by comparison across D7 and fields 17–18; no study states it.

### Confidence
**MODERATE**, bordering STRONG. The placements are explicit; the reframing is analytic.

### Scope conditions
Applies to the corpus's methodological disagreement. It does not resolve which position is
correct — that would require the comparative evidence Gap G7 records as absent.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **the standardization debate is therefore trivial or merely verbal.** DV-1 shows a real
normative incompatibility surviving underneath, between treating a computable optimum as a
legitimate target and denying that any optimum should be a goal.

## AR-5. Institutional apparatus is a repeatedly reported and mechanistically specified constraint — but its determinative form is untested

### Argument
Across studies with incompatible theoretical framings and unrelated methods, institutional
machinery is reported to set the boundary of feasible curricular change, and each study
supplies a distinct mechanism. The stronger claim — that apparatus *determines* what is
possible — is counterfactual and is not tested anywhere in the corpus.

### Evidence base
A08, A06, A11, A13 (primary); A09, A10 (secondary); A05 (inverted); A04, A15 (contributory).

### Reasoning
Set out at CV-2. The decisive strength is cross-framing agreement on the same explanandum
(why enactment lags design) from critical-epistemological and managerial-practitioner
positions that share no assumptions. The decisive limitation is that the claim's strong form
requires a comparison nobody makes, and that the corpus's one natural comparison — A08's 7
controlled vs. 4 uncontrolled units — is unavailable at the note layer.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A05 inverts the valence**: apparatus built as a solution (a Center founded specifically to
  enable cross-college affiliation).
- **A13 overrode apparatus impediments** by absorbing the cost personally and under crisis,
  showing the constraint is defeasible rather than absolute.
- **Three of four primary members are insider or semi-internal.**
- **A03 contains no institution in its account** and must not be admitted as support; doing so
  would convert a NOT REPORTED into a finding.

### Confidence
**STRONG** for reported, mechanistically specified constraint. **MODERATE** for determination.

### Scope conditions
Higher-education institutions with formal workload models, documented approval machinery and
external quality regimes. All corpus evidence is from such settings.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **curricular ideas and willingness do not matter.** A04 and A13 achieved substantial change
inside adverse apparatus; A01 and A04 document dispositional impediments that apparatus does not
explain.

## AR-6. "Success" is not comparable across this corpus, and the corpus nowhere demonstrates the chain from design to educational effect

### Argument
Each study's accepted success criterion is largely fixed by its conception of curriculum (what
it can regard as failure) and its evidentiary architecture (what it can observe). Studies
therefore claim success at four different levels while using one word, and no study in the
corpus evidences all four.

### Evidence base
All sixteen; decisively A16 (the sole learner-outcome evidence, second-hand), A05 (the longest
chain, levels 1–3, no outcome data), A06 (declines any success claim), A11 (the only declared
null result), A08 (self-contested proxy).

### Reasoning
Set out in Section 9. The mapping is directly countable, and the level-confusion risk is
demonstrable by pairing studies (A13 vs. A08; A02 vs. A11). Five primary studies explicitly
defer outcome evaluation to future work (A01, A02, A05, A11, A13), which converts the absence
from an omission into a declared boundary.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **NOT APPLICABLE is correct for A03, A07, A09 and A16**, whose success is feasibility and
  internal coherence. This is not a deficiency finding.
- **A16 does supply outcome evidence**, and dismissing it entirely would overcorrect: 13 of 18
  underlying studies reported knowledge and skill score gains. Its own analysis records that
  the eligibility filter plausibly produced the 100% positive rate.

### Confidence
**STRONG.** The claim is a countable property of the corpus, not an interpretation of it.

### Scope conditions
Applies to reading this corpus. It says nothing about whether curricular change is effective.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **documentary or structural success is meaningless.** The corpus does not establish that
either. Absence of outcome evidence cuts in both directions: it neither validates nor
invalidates the level-1 and level-2 achievements the studies report.

## AR-7. What the corpus supports about intermediaries is a set of functions, not a role or an attribute — and the same intermediation that enables change produces dependency

### Argument
The recurring intermediary in this corpus is defined by five functions (absorbing labour,
supplying method, producing a shared object, translating registers, rendering the decision
space explicit). Its attributes — externality, neutrality, non-authority — are contingent and
contested. And because its core function is to supply labour the institution does not allocate,
dependency is a predictable consequence rather than a design defect.

### Evidence base
A10, A12, A14, A06, A11, A13, A08; counterexamples A04 and A01.

### Reasoning
Set out in Section 6. The functional account is supported by seven heterogeneous instances,
including one (**A13's plain-language Reference Guide**) in which the functions are discharged
by an artifact rather than a person, and one (**A10's report as a "point-at-able object"**) in
which the artifact's value is explicitly social rather than informational. The dependency
proposition follows from combining CV-1 with A10's own finding that participants expected not
to continue.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A04 is decisive against the necessity of neutral external facilitation**: its facilitator
  was the academic vice-president; a four-year comprehensive reform completed.
- **A01 was self-facilitated.**
- **A10's neutrality claim was tested in a setting explicitly free of compulsory-reform
  pressure**, i.e. where neutrality was least likely to be contested; and it rests on three
  participants.
- **A11 shows heavy intermediation with no depth effect** — which limits the functional claim
  as much as it supports it.

### Confidence
**MODERATE** for the functional account; **TENTATIVE** against the necessity of neutrality;
**MODERATE** for the dependency proposition.

### Scope conditions
Applies where curricular work is not allocated in workload models and where an intermediary
supplies the shortfall. It does not extend to settings where curricular labour is costed.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **intermediaries are necessary for curricular change.** A04 and A01 refute the necessity
claim directly. The defensible statement is that intermediary *functions* recur as a condition
of change, and that they may be performed by authority holders, by peers, or by artifacts.

## AR-8. The corpus's recommendations are complementary rather than competing — but the complementarity is the analyst's construction, not a tested integration

### Argument
Because studies locate difficulty at different points of a change trajectory, their remedies
address different failure points and are largely not rivals. However, the resulting composite
account is assembled by the reader; the studies do not corroborate, test or extend one another.

### Evidence base
CP-1 and CP-4; Stage 2B Adjudication 2 (near-disjoint study sets for *what should change*,
*how decisions are made*, *how change is institutionalized*, with only A05 and A15 spanning
more than one); Gap G7.

### Reasoning
D2's placements are drawn from explicit problem-framing statements rather than topic
inference, so the distribution of located difficulties is well grounded. Each segment of the
trajectory is covered by one or two studies with no overlap sufficient for corroboration. And
no study evaluates another corpus study's framework (Section 13, DERIVED, note-layer bounded).

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A01, A13, A04 and A15 deny stage segmentation**, so a trajectory-based complementarity is
  imposed on studies that reject the underlying partition.
- **Some pairs are genuinely rivals, not complements**: A03 vs. A11 on optimization; A16 vs.
  A12/A15/A09 on standardization.
- **A03 sits outside the trajectory altogether.**

### Confidence
**MODERATE.**

### Scope conditions
A claim about how this corpus can legitimately be read, not about the field.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **the corpus therefore provides a complete account of curricular change.** Joining
single-study segments produces a narrative, not a corroborated model, and the joints are
exactly where the evidence is thinnest (design→implementation; implementation→institutionalization;
institutionalization→effect).

## AR-9. Insider position simultaneously strengthens the corpus's impediment findings and weakens its success findings

### Argument
Researcher position does not simply degrade or improve evidence; it shifts which *class* of
claim is available. Insider action research is the corpus's only route to hidden labour,
documentation-format constraints and enactment failure — and the same proximity removes any
independent check on favourable self-evaluation.

### Evidence base
A08, A13, A10, A04, A14, A05, A01 (insider); A11 (semi-internal); A06 (external documentary);
A15, A09, A16 (aggregate); A03 (modeller); A12 (practitioner-on-practitioner).

### Reasoning
A08's 39-vs-200–250-hour finding, A13's ~200 hours, A10's ~100 developer-hours, A04's absent
load reduction and A14's block-course failure are all knowable only from inside. Meanwhile the
corpus's favourable relational findings (A01, A04, A10) share one vulnerability: the people
reporting improved relationships are the people who underwent the process, with no independent
instrument recorded.

### Counterevidence / boundary cases
- **A08, A14, A11 and A06** show insider or semi-internal position producing negative, partial
  failure, null and withheld findings respectively. The pattern is a pressure, not a rule.
- **A04 describes explicit bias-control procedures** — responses pre-recorded before interviews,
  a six-month wait after the reform concluded — which is the corpus's most developed
  methodological response to the problem, though its own analysis notes no independent
  verification.

### Confidence
**MODERATE.**

### Scope conditions
Applies to how the corpus's findings should be weighted; does not license ranking studies by
design.

### What would overstate the evidence?
That **insider studies are unreliable**, or that **external designs would have found the same
things.** A06, the corpus's only fully external primary study, could not have produced the
labour findings at all; it can only see documents.

# 18. COLLECTIVE CONTRIBUTIONS

Separated by kind. **Using a theory is not a theoretical contribution**; only constructions,
transpositions and operationalizations are listed as such, and imported constructs are marked
as imports.

## Conceptual contributions

**Constructed or sharpened within the corpus:**
- **Curriculum viability**, distinguished from curriculum quality, together with the
  **standards / inhibitors** pairing and the **"reviewer" vs. "interpreter"** evaluative
  posture (A09).
- **The unregisterable curricular domain** — "becoming/being" as content that institutional
  documentation formats cannot record (A08). This is the corpus's strongest conceptual claim
  about representation, because it makes the gap a product of the recording apparatus rather
  than a measurement failure.
- **The "area of tension"** between the theoretically optimal and the institutionally possible
  curriculum, offered as a permanent condition rather than a problem to be solved (A14).

# 19. GAPS

Each gap is assigned to one of three categories. **(A)** the corpus does not investigate it;
**(B)** this specific sixteen-study sample cannot address it; **(C)** the information may exist
in the original articles but was not preserved in the literature notes. **A (C)-type gap can
never be converted into an (A)-type claim, and no gap of any type is evidence of absence in the
world.**

## (A) Corpus-level absences of evidence

**G-A1. Learner outcomes.** Only A16, second-hand, with a probable selection filter. Every
primary study omits or explicitly defers outcome data (A01, A02, A05, A11, A13 name it as
future work). *This is absence of evidence, declared by the studies themselves.*

**G-A2. Whether shallow adoption deepens or plateaus.** No longitudinal design exists in the
corpus capable of separating A06's threshold hypothesis from the ceiling reading (DV-5). This
is a **design-level** gap, not merely a coverage gap, and it prevents the corpus from
adjudicating its own strongest tendency.

**G-A3. Comparative evaluation of alternative approaches.** Not one study compares two
approaches to curricular change against each other. Named as future work by A10 (its
deep-advisory model against standard faculty-development services), A03 (other MCDM methods on
the same data) and A12 (any effectiveness measurement of advising strategies).

**G-A4. Direct study of non-participating influential actors.** Accreditors, regulators,
employers and state bodies shape curricula throughout (CV-5) and are studied by nobody. They
are inferred from their effects.

**G-A5. Failure and abandonment.** **No study in this corpus reports an abandoned reform.**
A15 names success bias explicitly in its source literature; A08 and A14 are the only studies
foregrounding failure, and A14's is partial.

**G-A6. Provenance and validity of the frameworks used as warrant.** UNESCO's competencies,
ITAA, WFME/LCME/GMC, accreditation guidance and institution-authored competency sets are
adopted, not examined (Section 5). **Partly (C)-type**: A02's note-layer ledger records that the
development or validation of its twelve competencies is not reported.

**G-A7. The cost of participation, and of curricular change generally.** Labour costs are
reported as impediments; **no study performs a cost–benefit assessment of any process it
recommends** (T-8).

**G-A8. What happens after an intermediary withdraws.** A10 predicts collapse; nobody observes
the aftermath.

**G-A9. Criterion formation.** How curricular criteria are actually formed, and whether
expert-weighting and collective-vision approaches produce different curricula, is examined by
nobody (Section 3, CP-6).

**G-A10. Students as decision-making participants.** The sole instance is A15's bottom-up
cluster (N=26), at aggregate second-order level. **No primary study places students in a
curricular decision role.**

**G-A11. Measurement across the represented/enacted gap.** Six studies endorse the distinction;
three gather any enactment-side evidence, one of them incidentally. No study is designed to
measure the gap.

**G-A12. Crisis and external shock as change conditions.** A13 alone, on the author's own
interpretation with no independent evidence recorded. A06 raises COVID-19 and Black Lives
Matter only as future research.

## (B) Limitations of this specific sixteen-study sample

**G-B1. Geographic and system concentration.** Primary studies: United States ×6 (A01, A02,
A04, A05, A10, A11), Australia ×2 (A08, A13), UK ×1 (A06), Ireland/UK ×1 (A12), Switzerland ×1
(A14), China ×1 (A03). Syntheses: A09 English-only; A16 English and Chinese only; A15 with
explicitly acknowledged geographic imbalance. **Any generalization from this corpus is a
generalization about Anglophone and Western-European higher education plus one Chinese case.**
No primary study is set in a Latin American institution; Brazil appears only inside A16's
underlying multi-country literature base.

**G-B2. Educational level.** All sixteen entries concern higher education. The corpus contains
no study of any other educational level.

**G-B3. Domain concentration.** Four medical/health-professions studies (A02, A07, A09, A16) all
concern mapping, standards or models; four sustainability studies (A03, A05, A14, A15) all
concern depth or breadth of integration. This is a property of how the corpus was assembled.
**A09 breaks any simple reading of it** by arguing against standardization from within medical
education.

**G-B4. Comparative reach.** Ten of sixteen are single-case and non-comparative; only A06 and
A11 among primary studies can observe heterogeneity of response to one reform.

**G-B5. Disciplinary variation in change dynamics.** Only A06 compares disciplines within one
institution, and only Medicine is named among its fifteen departments. **Four studies attribute
outcomes to disciplinary culture; the corpus cannot test the attribution.**

**G-B6. The corpus's own assembly procedure is unrecorded.** No corpus artifact documents how
these sixteen studies were selected. **Selection bias therefore cannot be assessed from inside
this analysis, and this limitation cannot be improved by further reading of the artifacts.**

## (C) Note-layer limitations

These constrain what can be claimed *here*, and say nothing about the original articles.

- **Measurement procedures for the corpus's most quoted figures**: how A08's 39 and 200–250
  hours were measured is not reported; A13's ~200 hours is author self-report; A10's ~100 hours
  is study-internal.
- **Reliability and procedure details**: A06's inter-rater statistics and the procedure for
  sorting departments into Active/Engaged/Passive; A11's survey sampling, response rate and
  significance testing; A14's inter-rater procedures.
- **Instrument content**: A12's Figure 1 is described only functionally; A03's full mapping
  index formulation; A15's 111 variables; A13's Reference Guide content.
- **Mappings**: which of A16's 18 studies used which of the 10 models; which of A09's 13
  articles contributed which of the 37 standards and 19 inhibitors; which of A08's 11 units were
  team-controlled, and outcome data for the 4 that were not.
- **Bibliographic identifiers**: publication year missing for A04, A07, A12; DOI missing for
  A04, A07, A11, A12.
- **Language layer**: several notes render quotations in Portuguese for English-language
  articles (A01, A06, A07, A09, A10, A11, A12, A13, A14, A15, A16). **No argument in this
  document rests on fine-grained author wording**, and no quotation here should be treated as
  verified original phrasing.

# 20. WHAT THIS CORPUS DOES NOT JUSTIFY CONCLUDING

For each tempting claim: why the corpus falls short, and what evidence would be needed.

**1. That there is a universal best process for curriculum reform.**
Not one study compares two approaches (G-A3). A15 finds six qualitatively distinct patterns and
explicitly frames them as alternative processes rather than stages; A12 concludes there are no
universal recipes; A14 refuses dogmatic imposition; A09 argues against checklist application.
*Needed:* comparative or matched-case designs evaluating two or more approaches against common
outcome measures.

**2. That greater stakeholder participation necessarily improves outcomes.**
The defensible claim is narrower and conditional: participation *by those who will enact a
change* is associated with faithful enactment where enactment requires interpretive work
(PC-1). Against the general claim: A01 (heavy participation, heavy conflict, two concrete
curricular changes, several identified demands not reported as acted upon); A11 (consultative
configuration, null structural result); A10 (participation, expected non-continuation). And
participation's cost is never priced (T-8). *Needed:* designs varying participation with outcome
measurement and cost accounting.

**3. That curriculum reform follows one universal sequence.**
Four studies deny stage segmentation (A01, A13, A04, A15); A15 finds movement between patterns
rather than progression through them. The trajectory used in Section 3 is an analyst's
partition. *Needed:* longitudinal process tracing across multiple institutions.

**4. That a single taxonomy explains all forms of curricular change.**
Seven of sixteen studies hold defensible membership in more than one cluster; three (A03, A07,
A12) resist classification for substantive reasons. Both Stage 2B and this synthesis reach the
same conclusion by different routes.

**5. That all barriers operate similarly across institutions.**
D6's four kinds have different remedies, and mismatching them produces motion without change
(Section 7). The only two designs able to observe within-institution heterogeneity (A06, A11)
both find it. *Needed:* multi-institution designs with a common impediment instrument.

**6. That successful redesign implies successful implementation.**
A08 is the standing counterexample; A06 quantifies a full categorical drop from language to
application; A14's designed artifact partially failed at delivery. *Needed:* implementation
observation attached to redesign studies.

**7. That successful implementation implies educational effectiveness.**
Only A16 supplies outcome evidence, second-hand and filter-suspect. A08's retention rise
coincides with a 30% enrolment increase and a content reduction, with no counterfactual; A05's
enrolment growth is a demand proxy, not an effectiveness measure. *Needed:* learner-outcome
measurement with comparison conditions.

**8. That observed associations establish causal mechanisms.**
A15's configuration–depth association is cross-sectional and drawn from success-biased published
cases with missing data treated as irrelevant. A13's crisis-as-catalyst claim is the author's
interpretation with no independent evidence recorded. A14's attribution of the block-course
failure to non-participation has unexplored alternatives recorded in its own analysis. A11's
attitude/behaviour discrepancy is presented as unresolved by its own source.

**9. That the corpus establishes which disciplinary content should be taught.**
Content decisions are warranted by external frameworks whose provenance and validity no study
examines (G-A6); A03's sustainability mapping index rests on five experts' 1–10 ratings with no
independent validity criterion; A01's benchmarking establishes what competitors offer, not what
should be offered. *Needed:* validation of competency frameworks against something other than
expert consensus.

**10. That the corpus provides direct evidence about Computing education, teacher education, or
BNCC Computação.**
**It does not.** As recorded in the matrix, the corpus's domains are fashion merchandising and
design, medical and health-professions education, accounting, theology, sustainability science,
higher-education policy, youth work, faculty development, general undergraduate programme
review, educational development practice, and higher education for sustainable development.
There is no study of Computing curricula, no study of teacher education, and no study of any
Brazilian national curriculum framework. No claim about those subjects is derivable from this
corpus, and none is made in this document.

**11. That curriculum mapping improves learning.**
A07, the corpus's dedicated mapping guide, explicitly concedes little direct empirical evidence
of the map's effectiveness in improving student learning, relying on an analogy to Ausubel's
advance organizers. No study in the corpus supplies that evidence.

**12. That intermediaries or facilitators are necessary.**
A04 and A01 are direct counterexamples (AR-7).

**13. That insider researchers systematically report success.**
A08, A14, A11 and A06 are counterexamples (AR-9). The pattern is a pressure, not a rule.

**14. That shallow adoption is a ceiling.**
Unresolved and unresolvable within this corpus (DV-5, T-1). A06's minority developmental
reading is not refuted by anything in the corpus.

**15. That documentary or structural success is meaningless.**
The corpus contains no evidence for this. Absence of outcome data does not invalidate the
level-1 and level-2 achievements reported.

**16. That the corpus's quantitative figures are measurements.**
The most-quoted figures (39 vs. 200–250 hours; ~200 hours; ~100 developer-hours; 103 vs. 9
documents) are self-reported or study-internal, with measurement procedures NOT REPORTED at the
note layer. They support a qualitative relation, not a magnitude.

**17. That the absence of a topic from this corpus indicates its absence from the literature.**
The corpus's assembly procedure is unrecorded (G-B6). Every gap in Section 19 is a gap in what
is available here.
