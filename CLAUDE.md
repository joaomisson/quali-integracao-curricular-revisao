# Project purpose

This repository supports a critical literature analysis for a master's
dissertation.

The corpus consists of:
- 16 Markdown literature-review notes, one per scientific study;
- 3 chapters from the current master's dissertation.

The project has two major goals:
1. understand the literature corpus independently and rigorously;
2. use the resulting evidence to critically reassess the dissertation.

# Fundamental rules

- Treat files under literature/ as literature notes, not as the complete papers.
- Never invent information absent from a source file.
- Clearly distinguish explicit evidence from interpretation.
- Every important literature claim must be traceable to one or more study IDs.
- Do not treat frequency as equivalent to importance or scientific consensus.
- Distinguish convergence, partial convergence, divergence, contradiction,
  complementary perspectives, tensions, and absence of evidence.
- Do not force the literature into the dissertation's current argument.
- Analyze the literature independently before using it to evaluate the dissertation.
- Actively look for evidence that challenges the dissertation's assumptions.
- Preserve minority or contradictory findings rather than smoothing them away.
- Avoid generic summaries. Prefer analytical relationships between studies.

# Evidence status

Use these labels when relevant:

EXPLICIT:
The information appears directly in the literature note.

DERIVED:
The conclusion is a reasonable interpretation derived from information
explicitly present in the literature note. Explain the reasoning.

UNSUPPORTED:
The information would be necessary for the analysis but is not available
in the literature note. Do not infer it.

# Dissertation rules

- Files under dissertation/original/ are immutable source material.
- Never modify files under dissertation/original/.
- Proposed changes must be written under dissertation/proposed/ or analysis/.
- First reconstruct the dissertation's current reasoning before suggesting changes.
- Structural changes may be substantial when justified by evidence.
- Do not preserve the current organization merely because it already exists.

# Evidence and traceability

Whenever making a cross-study claim:
- identify the supporting studies;
- identify counterexamples when they exist;
- state whether the claim is strong, moderate, tentative, or speculative;
- explain the reasoning connecting evidence to the claim.

# Working principle

Prefer:

evidence → comparison → pattern → interpretation → implication

Avoid:

desired conclusion → search for supporting examples
